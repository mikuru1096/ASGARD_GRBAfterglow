\begin{abstract}
Multiwavelength \grb\ afterglow fits are degenerate because different local
processes can produce similar \(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\).  Density structure,
reverse shocks, inverse-Compton cooling, hadronic losses, secondary pairs, and
observer geometry can all change the same light curve.  We present \asgard, a
Python/Fortran toolkit that records the radius-ordered local state of the
blast-wave dynamics, particles, photons, optional reverse shocks, optional
hadronic/pair feedback, absorption, and observer components.  The emission is
projected to \(t_{\rm obs}\) only after these local quantities have been written.
The supported calculation includes top-hat, Gaussian, and power-law jets;
uniform, \(k=2\) wind, and tabulated media; several electron transport solvers;
forward-shock synchrotron and SSC emission; a one-dimensional shell-averaged
hadronic branch; reverse-shock diagnostics; and component-resolved observer
products.  The central object is the state sequence containing \(\Gamma(R)\),
\(B'(R)\), particle spectra, photon fields, optical depths, and component
luminosities.  This record lets dynamical breaks, angular-projection effects,
cooling transitions, component crossings, and photon attenuation be traced back
to local source terms before observer projection mixes them.  Source-data-backed
figures test the state chain, validation metrics, five-code public-output
benchmarks where assumptions can be matched, and ASGARD-only complex-state
dashboard.  The implementation is bounded: PyBlastAfterglowMag is used through a
compiled repo-local \texttt{pba.out} for the FS and Gaussian-geometry rows, but
not for ASGARD RS/SSC or complex-state claims; formal hadronic feedback is
one-dimensional, \(\chi\)-resolved hadronic transport and IC-mediated
electromagnetic cascades are unsupported, user-defined media do not enter the
Fortran transport kernels, wind profiles with \(k\ne2\) are rejected, and jet
spreading is unsupported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}

Gamma-ray burst afterglows are commonly interpreted as synchrotron radiation
from nonthermal particles in a relativistic blast wave
\citep{MeszarosRees1997,Sari1998,Granot2002}.  The same observed
\(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\), however, can be produced by more than one physical
history.  A density jump can change the shock energy budget.  A reverse shock can
add a second emitting region.  Inverse-Compton cooling can move the cooling break.
Absorption can remove photons after they are emitted.  Angular structure and
off-axis projection can reshape the same local emission before it reaches the
observer.  The modeling problem is therefore not only to fit a light curve, but
also to identify which local state made the fitted feature.

The need for this diagnostic view has grown with modern afterglow data.  GRB
170817A required structured-jet and off-axis calculations, and it continues to
constrain late-time jet dynamics
\citep{Troja2017GW170817Xray,Hallinan2017GW170817Radio,LambKobayashi2017Structured,Mooley2018WideAngle,Mooley2018Superluminal,Lazzati2018CollimatedJet,Margutti2018GW170817,Alexander2018GW170817,Ghirlanda2019StructuredJet,Troja2019GW170817,Hajela2022GW170817,Katira2025GW170817,Ryan2024}.
GRB 190114C, GRB 190829A, and GRB 221009A made very-high-energy emission,
SSC-dominated interpretations, and narrow structured jets part of routine model
comparison
\citep{MAGIC2019TeV,MAGIC2019IC,HESS2019VHE,HESS2021GRB190829A,LHAASO2023TeVAfterglow,LHAASO2023Beyond10TeV,Laskar2023GRB221009A,OConnor2023GRB221009A}.
These cases show why a method paper must expose the dynamics, particle spectra,
photon targets, attenuation, and observer projection separately when those
objects are used to interpret a broadband feature.

Several public codes define the comparison landscape.  \afterglowpy\ provides
efficient synchrotron flux calculations for structured jets and off-axis
geometries \citep{Ryan2020}.  \jetsimpy\ emphasizes hydrodynamically motivated
thin-shell evolution and lateral structure \citep{Wang2024Jetsimpy}.  \vegas\
gives a recent code-paper reference for jets, media, dynamics, radiation,
observer geometry, examples, performance, and reverse-shock jump conditions
\citep{Wang2025VegasAfterglow}.  \pyblast\ is a modular multiphysics blast-wave
framework for transient counterparts \citep{Nedora2025PyBlastAfterglow}.  ASGARD
is complementary to these tools.  It records the radius-ordered local state of
dynamics, particles, photons, optional reverse shocks, optional hadronic/pair
feedback, absorption, and observer components, so that complex multi-physics
light-curve features can be traced back to their local physical causes before
observer projection mixes them.  Claims outside that stored state, projection
operator, and benchmark evidence are treated as unsupported.

The manuscript follows the logic of the calculation, but it separates the main
physical state from technical solver details.  Section~\ref{sec:contract}
defines the radius-ordered state.  Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} gives the jet,
external medium, and forward-shock dynamics.  Section~\ref{sec:electron} gives
particle and radiation transport, including the electron, synchrotron, SSC, and
absorption state.  Sections~\ref{sec:reverse-shock} and~\ref{sec:hadronic} give
the reverse-shock and hadronic/pair-feedback branches.  Section~\ref{sec:projection}
gives observer projection, and Section~\ref{sec:fitting} records runtime and
reproducibility products.  Section~\ref{sec:verification} states the benchmark
evidence, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets what that evidence can and
cannot show, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives the hard model boundaries.

\section{Model Definition and Radius-Ordered State} \label{sec:contract}

\asgard\ treats an afterglow calculation as a radial history of a shocked shell.  The public input specifies the jet, external density, observer, microphysics, frequency and time grids, and the optional hadronic or reverse-shock switches.  The code first turns these inputs into one runtime model and then advances the shock from small to large radius.  Observer products are not evolved as independent variables.  They are assembled only after the local emitting and absorbing material has been computed.

The radius ordering is physical, not only computational.  At a given shock radius \(R\), the dynamics determine the external density \(n(R)\), Lorentz factor \(\Gamma(R)\), swept-up mass \(M_{\rm sw}(R)\), internal energy \(U(R)\), and comoving magnetic field \(B'(R)\).  These quantities define the shell in which electrons are injected and cooled.  The same shell also supplies the synchrotron photon field, self-absorption optical depth, inverse-Compton targets, and, when requested, the \(p\gamma\), Bethe-Heitler, \(pp\), and \(\gamma\gamma\) targets.  Only after these local quantities are known does the observer module apply Doppler factors, equal-arrival-time geometry, distance factors, and attenuation.

This order is required because the observer light curve is many-to-one with respect to the local physics.  A break in \(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\) may be produced by blast-wave deceleration, angular structure, a cooling transition, a component crossing, a reverse shock, or local photon absorption.  If the calculation stores only the final flux, those possibilities cannot be separated.  The radius-ordered state instead keeps the quantities that generated the flux visible before they are mixed by projection \citep{Granot2002,vanEerten2010,vanEerten2012,vanEerten2018BlastWaves,Salafia2022JetStructure}.

The calculation then follows five visible steps.  Public inputs define a runtime configuration.  The dynamics module writes the shell state.  The electron and photon modules write the lepton distribution, synchrotron/SSC source terms, and photon-survival factors.  Optional hadronic and reverse-shock modules append their own state only when their switches are active.  The observer module projects the assembled local emissivity and attenuation to \(t_{\rm obs}\).  If a supported electron-photon or hadronic feedback loop is active, the coupled transport is iterated before projection.  The projection step only reads the local state produced by the earlier modules.

For one active radial cell, the state record can be summarized as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}(R)=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\Gamma,M_{\rm sw},U,B',
N_e,n_\gamma,N_p,N_\pi,N_\mu,
\\
Q_e^{\rm sec},\tau_{\gamma\gamma},L_\nu^{\rm comp}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
This notation is a state list, not a claim that every run contains every field.
A one-dimensional leptonic calculation writes the forward-shock dynamics,
electron spectrum, photon field, synchrotron and SSC components, and absorption
factors.  A formal hadronic calculation additionally writes the proton, pion,
muon, neutrino, photon-loss, and secondary-pair fields.  A reverse-shock
calculation writes a separate region-3 state before the same observer-time
projection is applied.  Unsupported combinations are absent from the state
record rather than inferred from partial fields.

The supported observer products are derived from this state record: multi-frequency
fluxes, fixed-time spectra, band-integrated fluxes, exposure-averaged fluxes,
component-resolved fluxes, and diagnostic state tracks.  The component record
contains the total flux and the active physical channels: forward-shock
synchrotron and SSC emission, reverse-shock synchrotron and SSC emission,
cross-zone inverse Compton emission, hadronic synchrotron emission, pair
synchrotron emission, and neutrino outputs.  The diagnostic state exposes the
radius, Lorentz factor, magnetic field, electron spectra, \(\chi\)-resolved
electron fields, hadronic species, photon-survival factors, reverse-shock state,
and secondary reverse-shock event diagnostics when those calculations are active.

The public model definition is intentionally bounded.  \asgard\ is not presented
as a replacement for faster light-curve-only tools.  Its purpose in this paper is
to make a coupled afterglow calculation inspectable by preserving the state that
precedes observer projection.  The supported runtime families are listed in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}; physical combinations outside those families are not
interpreted as modeled behavior.

\section{Dynamics and Jet/Medium Setup} \label{sec:dynamics}

The dynamics section defines the blast wave before any radiation is computed.
The forward shock first needs the external density \(n(R)\), the angular energy
profile, and the angular Lorentz-factor profile.  From these inputs the dynamics
module writes \(\Gamma(R)\), swept-up matter, internal energy, and the magnetic
field used by the particle and radiation modules.

\subsection{External media}

The external medium is the gas swept up by the forward shock.  In the dynamics it
enters through the proton number density \(n(R)\).  \asgard\ supports three
families: a uniform medium, a Chevalier--Li \(k=2\) stellar wind, and a tabulated
radial profile.  The same \(n(R)\) is then passed to particle injection, photon
production, absorption, and observer projection at that radius.

\subsubsection{Analytic media}

The analytic media give the upstream density directly.  A uniform medium has a
constant density, while a stellar wind falls as \(R^{-2}\).  The implemented
forms are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n(R) &= n_{\rm ISM},\\
n_{\rm w}(R) &= \frac{A_\ast A_0}{m_pR^2},\\
n_0(R) &=
\begin{cases}
n_{\rm ISM}, & n_{\rm w}(R)\le n_{\rm ISM}/\eta_{\rm w},\\
n_{\rm w}(R), & n_{\rm w}(R)> n_{\rm ISM}/\eta_{\rm w},
\end{cases}\\
R_0 &= \left(\frac{A_\ast A_0}{m_p n_{\rm cap}}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(n_{\rm ISM}\) is the uniform density, \(A_0\) is the Chevalier--Li wind
mass-loading scale, \(m_p\) is the proton mass, and \(\eta_{\rm w}\) sets the
wind-to-ISM switch \citep{ChevalierLi2000}.  If a user supplies a cap density
\(n_{\rm cap}\), the code defines \(R_0\) and holds the wind density at
\(n_{\rm w}(R_0)\) inside that radius.  A uniform medium remains at
\(n_{\rm ISM}\).  This cap is a hard inner density state; a wind profile with
\(k\ne2\) is unsupported.

\subsubsection{Tabulated media and jumps}

A tabulated medium supplies the upstream density from user points.  The table
contains positive radii \(R_i\) and positive densities \(n_i\), with the radii in
strictly increasing order and \(N_\rho\le N_{\rho,\max}\).  Between two adjacent
points, the code uses the local power law
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n(R) &=
\exp\left[
\operatorname{interp}\left(\ln R;\,\ln R_i,\,\ln n_i\right)
\right],\\
n(R) &=
n_i
\left(\frac{R}{R_i}\right)^{q_i},\qquad
q_i\equiv\frac{\ln(n_{i+1}/n_i)}{\ln(R_{i+1}/R_i)} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first two table points set the extrapolation below the table, and the last
two set the extrapolation above it.  The table is therefore extended as a power
law at both ends.  A tabulated medium and the Gaussian jump model are separate
input families; they are not combined in one run.

Density jumps are local perturbations of an analytic baseline density.  They are
used only with the analytic medium family.  For several Gaussian structures, the
density is
\begin{equation}
n(R)=n_0(R)
\left[
1+\sum_j
(f_j-1)
\exp\left(
-\frac{(R-R_j)^2}{2(\Delta R_j)^2}
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}
Here \(R_j\) is the center, \(f_j\) is the density factor, and
\(\Delta R_j\equiv w_jR_j\) is the width.  One Gaussian gives the older
single-jump interface.  Values with \(f_j<1\) represent density dips, and they
are allowed only when the total density remains positive.  These Gaussians are
controlled density perturbations for afterglow calculations; this paper does not
claim a unique environment for each Gaussian
\citep{DaiLu2002,NakarGranot2007,UhmZhang2014}.

The medium setup also defines hard input boundaries.  It supports
\(N_J\le N_{J,\max}\) Gaussian structures or \(N_\rho\le N_{\rho,\max}\)
tabulated points.  It rejects nonpositive jump radii, factors, or widths;
nonpositive table radii or densities; tables with fewer than two points; and
tables whose radii are not ordered.  These checks define the supported medium
families, not extra physical assumptions.

\subsection{Jet angular profiles}

The jet profile sets the initial energy and Lorentz factor of each polar
element.  It is an initial condition, not an angular-evolution model.  A top-hat
jet is uniform inside \(\theta_j\) and empty outside it:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm iso}(\theta) &=
\begin{cases}
E_c, & \theta<\theta_j,\\
0, & \theta\ge\theta_j,
\end{cases}\\
\Gamma_0(\theta) &=
\begin{cases}
\Gamma_c, & \theta<\theta_j,\\
1, & \theta\ge\theta_j .
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A Gaussian jet decreases both the isotropic-equivalent energy and
\(\Gamma_0-1\) away from the axis:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm iso}(\theta) &=
E_c\exp\left[-\frac{\theta^2}{2\theta_c^2}\right],\\
\Gamma_0(\theta) &=
1+(\Gamma_c-1)\exp\left[-\frac{\theta^2}{2\theta_c^2}\right],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian profile is used only inside the declared outer support
\(\theta_{\max}\).  A power-law jet has a flat core and power-law wings:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm iso}(\theta) &=
E_c
\begin{cases}
1, & \theta\le \theta_c,\\
(\theta/\theta_c)^{-k_E}, & \theta>\theta_c,
\end{cases}\\
\Gamma_0(\theta) &=
\begin{cases}
\Gamma_c, & \theta\le \theta_c,\\
1+(\Gamma_c-1)(\theta/\theta_c)^{-k_\Gamma}, & \theta>\theta_c .
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(E_c\) and \(\Gamma_c\) are the on-axis values, \(\theta_c\) is the core
angle, and \(k_E\) and \(k_\Gamma\) set the wing slopes.  The public profile uses
\(k_\Gamma=k_E\) unless a separate Lorentz-factor slope is supplied.  These
profiles feed independent angular elements; the present backend does not evolve
lateral spreading.

\subsection{Forward-shock dynamical variables}

The forward-shock state carries the variables needed by later radiation modules.
The state vector is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{M},U,R),
\end{equation}
and the independent variable is the source-frame on-axis arrival coordinate
\(t_{\rm on}\).  Here \(\Gamma\) is the blast-wave Lorentz factor, \(U\) is the
internal energy, and \(R\) is the shock radius.  The second entry
\(\mathcal{M}\) depends on the dynamics branch: branches 1 and 2 store a swept
number column, while branch 3 stores the swept mass.  This vector is the local
dynamical input to electron injection and radiation.

The coordinate \(t_{\rm on}\) tracks on-axis photon arrival, not laboratory time.
Along the radial sequence,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dt_{\rm on}}{dR} &= \frac{1-\beta}{\beta c},\\
\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}} &= \frac{\beta c}{1-\beta},\\
\beta &= \sqrt{1-\Gamma^{-2}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second relation is therefore not the lab-frame shock speed.  It is the rate
at which the radius grid is traversed when \(t_{\rm on}\) is the independent
variable.  Off-axis observer times are assembled later from
\(t_{\rm obs}=(1+z)[t_{\rm on}(R)+R(1-\mu)/c]\), so this coordinate is a
dynamical ordering variable rather than the final observer clock.

The magnetic field is set by the local post-shock energy density.  For the
forward shock, the code uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e' &\simeq4(\Gamma^2-1)n(R)m_pc^2,\\
B' &=\left(8\pi\epsilon_B e'\right)^{1/2}
=\left[32\pi\epsilon_B n(R)m_pc^2(\Gamma^2-1)\right]^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(n(R)\) is the upstream proton density and \(\epsilon_B\) is the magnetic
energy fraction.  The output \(B'(R)\) sets synchrotron cooling, synchrotron
emission, SSC seed production, and magnetic diagnostics.

The dynamics branch also records electron energy scales used for startup and
diagnostics.  With \(t'_{\rm dyn}\equiv\Gamma t_{\rm on}\), these scales are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_c=
\frac{6\pi m_ec}{\sigma_{\rm es} B'^2t'_{\rm dyn}},
\\
\gamma_m=
1+\frac{\epsilon_e}{f_e}
\frac{m_p}{m_e}
\frac{p-2}{p-1}
(\Gamma-1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\gamma_c\) is the synchrotron cooling Lorentz factor, \(\gamma_m\) is the
minimum Lorentz factor of injected nonthermal electrons, \(\epsilon_e\) is the
electron energy fraction, \(f_e\) is the accelerated electron fraction, and \(p\)
is the injected electron index.  If \(\gamma_c>\gamma_m\) and \(p\ge2\), the
dynamics branch estimates the radiated fraction as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rm eff}=\epsilon_e\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma_c}\right)^{p-2};
\end{equation}
otherwise it uses \(\epsilon_{\rm eff}=\epsilon_e\).  This estimate affects only
the dynamics branch that asks for a radiative efficiency; the electron transport
module still evolves \(N_e\) explicitly.

The first radial grid needs a deceleration scale.  The setup first defines
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_0=
\frac{E_{\rm iso}}{4\pi(\Gamma_0-1)m_pc^2},
\end{equation}
where \(E_{\rm iso}\) and \(\Gamma_0\) are the initial isotropic-equivalent
energy and Lorentz factor of the angular element.  The analytic estimates for
the deceleration radius are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm dec,ISM}
&=\left(\frac{n_{\rm ISM}\Gamma_0}{\mathcal{D}_0}\right)^{-1/3},\\
R_{\rm dec,wind}
&=\frac{3\mathcal{D}_0}{2(A_\ast A_0/m_p)\Gamma_0}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_\ast A_0/m_p\) is the wind number-density normalization.  For wind
media the setup uses \(R_{\rm dec}=\min(R_{\rm dec,ISM},R_{\rm dec,wind})\);
otherwise it uses \(R_{\rm dec}=R_{\rm dec,ISM}\).  The corresponding on-axis
arrival scale is
\[
t_{\rm dec}=\frac{R_{\rm dec}(1-\beta_0)}{\beta_0c},
\qquad
\beta_0=(1-\Gamma_0^{-2})^{1/2}.
\]
For \(\Gamma_0\gg1\), this reduces to
\(t_{\rm dec}\simeq R_{\rm dec}/(2\Gamma_0^2c)\).  The solver uses this scale to
place early radial points across the coasting-to-decelerating transition; it does
not impose a broken power law.

\subsection{Dynamical branches}

The dynamics module provides three ways to evolve the same forward-shock state.
Branch 1 is the radiative-efficiency form often used in afterglow calculations
\citep{BlandfordMcKee1976,HuangDaiLu1999}.  Branch 2 is a legacy
variable-index form.  Branch 3 is the default mass-energy baseline.  All three
branches output \(\Gamma(R)\), swept matter, \(U(R)\), and \(R\); they differ in
how they write the inertia and energy budget.

\subsubsection{Shared equation of state and source terms}

Branches 2 and 3 use a variable adiabatic index so that the shocked gas connects
the relativistic and Newtonian limits.  The fixed equation-of-state fit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u&=\Gamma\beta,\qquad
\Theta=\frac{u}{3}\frac{u+a_\Theta u^2}{1+u+a_\Theta u^2},\\
\zeta&=\frac{\Theta}{a_\zeta+\Theta},\qquad
\hat{\gamma}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{r=0}^{N_{\hat\gamma}}a_r\zeta^r .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(u\) is the dimensionless four-velocity, \(\Theta\) is the temperature-like
fit variable, and \(\zeta\) is the polynomial coordinate.  The coefficients
\((a_\Theta,a_\zeta,\{a_r\})\) and \(N_{\hat\gamma}\) are fixed by the
Maxwell--Juttner equation-of-state fit \citep{Service1986,Peer2012}; only
\(\hat{\gamma}(\Gamma)\) enters the dynamics.

The optional energy-injection source is a prescribed source-frame luminosity,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(t_{\rm on})=
\begin{cases}
L_0(t_{\rm on}/t_1)^{q}, & t_1\le t_{\rm on}\le t_2,\\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with source-frame bounds \(t_1,t_2\), normalization \(L_0\), and index \(q\).
Public observer-frame bounds are divided by \(1+z\) before this source is
evaluated.  Branches 1 and 3 add \(\mathcal{L}\) to the local blast-wave budget;
branch 2 is retained as a no-injection legacy comparison.

\subsubsection{Swept-medium state variables}

Branches 1 and 2 evolve a swept number column \(\mu\), while branch 3 evolves the
physical swept mass \(M\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_\mu &\equiv\frac{d\mu}{dt_{\rm on}}
=n(R)R^2\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}},\\
\frac{dM}{dR} &=4\pi n(R)R^2m_p,\\
\mu_0 &=\frac{E_{\rm iso}}{4\pi(\Gamma_0-1)m_pc^2},\qquad
M_0 =\frac{E_{\rm iso}}{(\Gamma_0-1)c^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The public swept mass is \(M_{\rm sw}=4\pi m_p\mu\) for branches 1 and 2 and
\(M_{\rm sw}=M\) for branch 3, with
\[
\frac{dM}{dt_{\rm on}}=\frac{dM}{dR}\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}}.
\]

\subsubsection{Branch 1: radiative-efficiency dynamics}

Branch 1 balances swept-up deceleration against optional energy injection:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\Gamma}{dt_{\rm on}}=
\frac{-(\Gamma^2-1)q_\mu
+\mathcal{L}(t_{\rm on})/(4\pi m_pc^2)}
{\mu_0+\epsilon_{\rm eff}\mu
+2(1-\epsilon_{\rm eff})\Gamma\mu},
\\
\frac{dU}{dt_{\rm on}}=(\Gamma-1)q_\mu .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The swept-column state \(\mu\) supplies both the deceleration term and the
radiative-efficiency denominator.

\subsubsection{Branch 2: variable-index legacy dynamics}

Branch 2 keeps the swept column \(\mu\) and changes the effective inertia through
\(\hat{\gamma}\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{N}_2&=
\hat{\gamma}(\Gamma^2-1)-(\hat{\gamma}-1)\Gamma\beta^2,\\
\mathcal{B}_2&=
2\hat{\gamma}\Gamma-(\hat{\gamma}-1)(1+\Gamma^{-2}),\\
\mathcal{D}_2&=\mu_0+\epsilon_{\rm eff}\mu\\
&\quad +(1-\epsilon_{\rm eff})\mu\mathcal{B}_2,\\
\frac{d\Gamma}{dt_{\rm on}}&=-
\frac{\mathcal{N}_2}{\mathcal{D}_2}q_\mu,\\
\frac{dU}{dt_{\rm on}}=(\Gamma-1)q_\mu .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This branch is selectable for legacy dynamics comparisons but is not the default
state used for the publication figures.

\subsubsection{Branch 3: mass-energy baseline}

Branch 3 uses the physical swept mass \(M\), so the inertia and heat budget are
written directly.  With \(X=\Gamma^2-1\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{N}_3&=
-\frac{dM}{dR}c^2\Gamma X
(\hat{\gamma}\Gamma-\hat{\gamma}+1)\\
&\quad
-(\hat{\gamma}X+1)\Gamma(1-\hat{\gamma})\frac{3U}{R}\\
&\quad
+\Gamma^2\mathcal{L}(t_{\rm on})
\left(\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}}\right)^{-1},\\
\mathcal{D}_3&=
\Gamma^2(M_0+M)c^2+(\hat{\gamma}^2X+3\hat{\gamma}-2)U,\\
\frac{d\Gamma}{dt_{\rm on}}&=
\frac{\mathcal{N}_3}{\mathcal{D}_3}\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The three numerator terms are newly swept mass, pressure work in the shocked gas,
and optional injected luminosity.  This explicit budget makes branch 3 the
default forward-shock state.

The corresponding internal-energy equation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}_R &=
\frac{3}{R}
-\frac{1}{\Gamma}
\left(\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}}\right)^{-1}
\frac{d\Gamma}{dt_{\rm on}},\\
\mathcal{H}_R &=(1-\epsilon_{\rm eff})(\Gamma-1)
\frac{dM}{dR}c^2,\\
\frac{dU}{dt_{\rm on}}&=
\left[\mathcal{H}_R-(\hat{\gamma}-1)\mathcal{E}_R U\right]
\frac{dR}{dt_{\rm on}}
+\mathcal{L}(t_{\rm on}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This thermal budget later sets electron injection and the magnetic field.

\subsection{Forward-dynamics integration}

The forward-dynamics integrator stores the selected branch as a radial state.  It
reads the public model, fixes the density profile, and integrates
\(\mathbf{y}=(\Gamma,Y_2,U,R)\) on the \(t_{\rm on}\) grid, where \(Y_2=\mu\)
for branches 1 and 2 and \(Y_2=M\) for branch 3.  After each accepted step,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
t_{{\rm obs},i}&=(1+z)t_{{\rm on},i},\qquad
\Gamma_i=Y_{\Gamma,i},\qquad R_i=Y_{R,i},\\
M_{{\rm sw},i}&=
\begin{cases}
4\pi m_pY_{2,i},
& \text{branches 1 and 2},\\
Y_{2,i},
& \text{branch 3}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These arrays are the dynamics output read by the electron, photon, reverse-shock,
hadronic, and observer modules.  Runtime containers and grid-start details are
given in Appendix~\ref{app:projection-runtime}.

The numerical update is fourth-order Runge--Kutta.  For
\(t_{{\rm on},i-1}>0\), the integration variable is
\begin{equation}
s=\ln(t_{\rm on}/t_{{\rm on},i-1}),\qquad
\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{ds}
=t_{\rm on}F(t_{\rm on},\mathbf{y}),
\end{equation}
where \(F\equiv d\mathbf{y}/dt_{\rm on}\) is the selected branch.  The first
interval starts at \(t_{\rm on}=0\) and is advanced in linear \(t_{\rm on}\).

The adaptive check compares the current \(m\)-substep trial
\(\mathbf{y}^{\rm f}\) with the previous \(m/2\)-substep solution
\(\mathbf{y}^{\rm c}\):
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rm RK}^\ast=
\max_{a\in\mathcal{I}_y}
\frac{2|y_a^{\rm f}-y_a^{\rm c}|}
{y_a^{\rm f}+y_a^{\rm c}},
\qquad
\mathcal{I}_y\equiv\{a\,|\,y_a^{\rm f}+y_a^{\rm c}>0\}.
\end{equation}
The subdivision is refined until \(\epsilon_{\rm RK}^\ast<\epsilon_{\rm RK}\).
Accepted trials must also keep \(\Gamma>1\).  These conditions define the
relativistic state domain of an accepted radius step.

\subsection{Python binding boundary}

The Python dynamics namespace is only a binding layer.  It exposes the compiled
forward and reverse dynamics kernels to higher-level orchestration.  It does not
add a blast-wave equation, a shock jump condition, or an integration step.  The
physical equations are the compiled forward dynamics described above and the
reverse-shock equations in Section~\ref{sec:reverse-shock}.

\section{Particle and Radiation Transport} \label{sec:electron}

The particle and radiation layer follows the material that produces the
afterglow spectrum.  At each radius, the shock injects nonthermal electrons.
Those electrons cool by synchrotron, inverse-Compton, and adiabatic losses, and
their radiation builds the photon field used by SSC, absorption, and optional
feedback branches.  The one-dimensional solvers return \(N_e(\gamma,R)\),
synchrotron luminosity, seed-photon density, and the break frequencies
\(\nu_m\), \(\nu_c\), and \(\nu_a\).  The two-dimensional path adds downstream
\(\chi\)-resolved electron and photon fields.  The solver choice changes the
discretization, not the physical injection law
\citep{LeVeque2002,JiangShu1996,HesthavenWarburton2008}.

\subsection{Shared electron state, sources, and coordinates}

The Python electron namespace only selects a compiled transport kernel.  The
physical forward-electron problem is the same for all one-dimensional solvers:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial N_e}{\partial R}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}
\left(g_RN_e\right)
=Q_e+Q_e^{\rm sec},
\end{equation}
where \(g_R=d\gamma/dR\) contains radiative and adiabatic cooling.  The primary
shock source is \(Q_e\); \(Q_e^{\rm sec}\) is supplied only by active hadronic or
pair-feedback branches.  The default storage coordinate is \(x=\ln\gamma\), so
\begin{equation}
N_x\equiv\frac{dN_e}{dx}=\gamma N_e,
\qquad x=\ln\gamma .
\end{equation}
Four-velocity storage changes only the coordinate Jacobian.  A representative
implicit shell step is
\begin{equation}
\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_i\right)\mathbf{N}_{i+1}
=\mathbf{N}_{i}+\Delta R_i\,\mathbf{Q}_{i},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{A}_i\) is assembled from face drift coefficients.  The update
acts on cell averages; point-value and conservative kernels differ only in how
they store and reconstruct the same \(N_e\).

Electron injection uses the same density \(n(R)\) as the dynamics.  No separate
electron-density law is introduced.  With
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(R)&=\frac{4\pi R^3}{3},\\
N_{\rm init}&=\int_0^{R_{\rm ini}}4\pi R^2 n(R)\,dR,\\
\Delta V_s&\equiv V(R_s+\Delta R)-V(R_s),\\
q_R(R_s,\Delta R)&\equiv n(R_s)\frac{\Delta V_s}{\Delta R},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the first active population and every later shell are defined by the same finite
volume measure.  The acceleration cutoff and injection minimum are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\max}&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{\left(8e^3B'\right)^{1/2}},\\
\Xi_e&\equiv\frac{\epsilon_e}{f_e}\frac{m_p}{m_e}(\Gamma-1),\\
\mathcal{C}_{\max}(\gamma)&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \gamma\le\gamma_{\max},\\
\exp(1-\gamma/\gamma_{\max}), & \gamma>\gamma_{\max},
\end{cases}\\
\gamma_m&=1+\frac{p-2}{p-1}\Xi_e,\qquad p>2,\\
\gamma_m&=
\left[\frac{2-p}{p-1}\Xi_e\gamma_{\max}^{p-2}\right]^{1/(p-1)}+1,
\qquad 1<p<2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For \(p=2\), \(\gamma_m\) is set by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_m\ln\left(\frac{\gamma_{\max}}{\gamma_m}\right)=\Xi_e .
\end{equation}

The forward-shock source is a power law with the high-energy cutoff above.  The
natural-log cell source is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&a_p=f_e\frac{1-p}{\gamma_{\max}^{1-p}-\gamma_m^{1-p}},
\qquad A_R=q_R(R_s,\Delta R)a_p,\\
S_x(x)
&=\exp[(1-p)x]
\mathcal{C}_{\max}[\exp(x)]H[\exp(x)-\gamma_m],\\
Q_{x,j}
&=A_R\,\mathcal I_{x,j},\\
\mathcal I_{x,j}
&=\frac{1}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}
S_x(x)\,dx .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(H\) is the Heaviside function, and \(Q_{x,j}\) is the injected
cell-averaged source.  The initial spectrum uses \(N_0\equiv N_{\rm init}\) on
the same grid: the fast-cooling branch has slopes \(-2\) and \(-p-1\), the
slow-cooling branch has slopes \(-p\) and \(-p-1\), and conservative solvers store
\(\Delta x_j^{-1}\int dN_e/dx\,dx\) with splits at
\(\gamma_m\), \(\gamma_c\), and \(\gamma_{\max}\).  Full quadrature formulas are
given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

Only the coordinate measure changes when a solver stores a coordinate \(y\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_y(y)&=S_x[x(y)]\,\frac{dx}{dy},\\
Q_{y,j}
&=A_R\,\mathcal I_{y,j},\\
\mathcal I_{y,j}
&=\frac{1}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}
S_y(y)\,dy .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus each solver receives the same injected physical distribution.

Reverse-shock electrons use a kinetic-energy power law.  This is different from
the forward-shock source, which is written as a power law in \(\gamma\).  In the
log-gamma coordinate,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_m(\gamma)&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \gamma>\gamma_m,\\
0, & \gamma\le\gamma_m,
\end{cases}\\
\Psi_x(\gamma)&=\gamma(\gamma-1)^{-p}
\mathcal{C}_{\max}(\gamma)I_m(\gamma),\\
Q_{x,j}^{\rm kin}=Q\,
\frac{\bar\Psi_{x,j}}
{\sum_b \bar\Psi_{x,b}\Delta x_b}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Four-velocity coordinates apply the same Jacobian and normalization.

The optional thermal component adds a Maxwell--Juttner electron population.  Its
temperature and continuous shape are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Theta(u)&=
\frac{u^2(1+a_{\rm MJ}u)}{3(1+u+a_{\rm MJ}u^2)},\\
\phi_\gamma(\gamma;\Theta)&=
\frac{\gamma^2\beta_\gamma e^{-\gamma/\Theta}}
{\Theta K_2(1/\Theta)},
\\
\beta_\gamma&=(1-\gamma^{-2})^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite-grid normalization converts this shape to a log-coordinate density and
then adds either \(N_{\rm th}\phi_x\) as a source or \(N_{\rm th}\phi_x/\gamma\)
as an initial centered population.  These coordinate maps are storage maps, not
new electron physics; their explicit Jacobians are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

\subsection{Shared conservative shell-transport primitives}

The shared transport layer assembles the physical source, cooling, and adiabatic
terms before choosing the finite-volume \texttt{fullhide} or DG representation.
In a storage coordinate \(y\), the representative conservative update is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{y,j}(R)&=\frac{1}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}\frac{dN_e}{dy}\,dy,\\
\Delta y_j&=y_{j+1/2}-y_{j-1/2},\\
\mathcal{V}_{j+1/2}&=
\frac{(D_j+D_{j+1})/2+D_{\rm ad}}
{(dx_g/dy)_{j+1/2}},\\
\mathcal{F}_{j+1/2}^{n+1}&
=\mathcal{V}_{j+1/2}^{+}N_{j+1}^{n+1}
+\mathcal{V}_{j+1/2}^{-}N_j^{n+1},\\
N_j^{n+1}&=N_j^n+\Delta R\,Q_j
+\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta y_j}
\left(\mathcal{F}_{j+1/2}^{n+1}
-\mathcal{F}_{j-1/2}^{n+1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(D_j\) and \(D_{\rm ad}\) are the radiative and adiabatic parts of
\(d\ln\gamma_e/dR\), \(x_g=\ln\gamma_e\),
\(\mathcal{V}^{+}=\max(\mathcal{V},0)\), and
\(\mathcal{V}^{-}=\min(\mathcal{V},0)\).  The low-energy face is closed, and the
positive projection \(\Pi_+(X)=\max(X,0)\) acts only on solved cell averages.  It
does not mix neighboring bins.  Sweep details are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

The cooling-break and adaptive-step diagnostics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{\Lambda}(\gamma_c)&=\frac{1}{R},
\\
\mathcal{A}&\equiv
\left\{j\,\middle|\,N^{\rm h}_j>0\right\},
\\
\epsilon_{\rm step}&=
\max_{j\in\mathcal{A}}
\frac{|N^{\rm h}_j-N^{\rm f}_j|}
{|N^{\rm h}_j|}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\Lambda}\) is the face-averaged loss coefficient, \(N^{\rm f}\) is
the full-step solution, and \(N^{\rm h}\) is the two-half-step solution.  The
search support may extend to \(\chi_{\rm sup}\gamma_{\max}\) for numerical
coverage; \(\gamma_{\max}\) remains the physical cutoff.  The acceptance
condition is \(\epsilon_{\rm step}\le\epsilon_{\rm tol}\), with reconstruction
formulas given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

\subsection{Characteristic remap primitive}

The characteristic solver traces the inverse energy
\(u=1/\gamma_e=\exp(-x)\) backward across the radial step.  For an affine cooling
law,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{du}{dR}&=a+bu,\\
u_{\rm back}&=
\begin{cases}
u-a\ell, & b=0,\\
(u+a/b)e^{-b\ell}-a/b, & b\ne0,
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\ell\) is the radial lag.  For a piecewise cooling curve, the cell-local
equation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{du}{dR}=a_r+(s_r+b_{\rm ad})u,
\qquad w(u)\equiv uD(u)=a_r+s_ru .
\end{equation}
Face crossings continue the remaining lag in the adjacent cell.  With traced
arrival faces \(x_{j\pm1/2}^{\rm back}(\ell)\), the conservative remap is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_j[M;\ell]\equiv
\frac{
M[x_{j+1/2}^{\rm back}(\ell)]
-M[x_{j-1/2}^{\rm back}(\ell)]
}{\Delta x_j}.
\end{equation}
The source-coupled update adds newly injected electrons while old electrons cool
along traced characteristics:
\begin{equation}
N_j^{n+1}
=\mathcal R_j[M_N;\Delta R]
+\Delta R\,s_Q
\sum_{m=1}^{N_{\rm G}^{\rm C}}w_m\mathcal R_j[M_Q;s_m\Delta R].
\end{equation}
Here \(M_N\) is the old-population prefix, \(M_Q\) is the positive-source prefix,
and \(s_Q\) is the current source normalization.  The source-free remap sets
\(s_Q=0\).  Quadrature weights, split forms, and support diagnostics are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

\subsection{Implicit finite-volume \texttt{fullhide\_1d} solver}

The \texttt{fullhide\_1d} solver follows the forward-shock electron population on
a finite-volume energy grid.  The nonthermal branch uses the four-velocity
coordinate \(y\) and stores
\begin{equation}
N_{y,j}(R)=\frac{1}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}\frac{dN_e}{dy}\,dy .
\label{eq:fullhide-cell-average}
\end{equation}
The solver evolves these cell averages as the shock moves from one radius to the
next; it changes the numerical representation, not the injection or cooling law.

Each radial shell supplies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{s,i}&=R_{i-1},\qquad
\bar\Gamma_i=\frac{\Gamma_i+\Gamma_{i-1}}{2},\qquad
n_i=n(R_{s,i}),\\
B_i'&=\left[32\pi\epsilon_B n_i m_pc^2
\bar\Gamma_i(\bar\Gamma_i-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{\max,i}&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8B_i'e^3)^{1/2}},\\
a_{c,i}&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i'^2\bar\Gamma_i t_{{\rm obs},i}}
{6\pi m_ec(1+z)},\qquad
\gamma_{c,i}=a_{c,i}^{-1},\\
\beta_i&=(1-\bar\Gamma_i^{-2})^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(B_i'\) sets synchrotron losses, \(\gamma_{\max,i}\) sets the injection
cutoff, \(\gamma_{c,i}\) is the diagnostic cooling Lorentz factor, and
\(\beta_i c\) sets radial cooling rates.

Fast cooling can split one shell into smaller radial substeps.  The estimate
compares synchrotron and adiabatic drifts,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{m,i}&=\frac{R_i+R_{i-1}}{2},\\
a_i&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i'^2}
{6\pi m_ec^2\beta_i\bar\Gamma_i},\\
\Delta R_0&=\frac{\eta_{\rm fh}}
{a_i\gamma_{\max,i}+\eta_{\rm ad}/R_{m,i}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The shell width and public substep bounds set the number of fixed substeps; the
exact grid choice is given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

One implicit substep treats cooling as flux through energy-cell faces.  With
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar D_{j+1/2}&=\frac{D_j+D_{j+1}}{2},\\
v_{j+1/2}&=
\frac{\bar D_{j+1/2}+R^{-1}}
{(dx_g/dy)_{j+1/2}},\\
N_j^{n+1}&=N_j^n+\Delta R\,Q_j
+G_{j+1/2}-G_{j-1/2},\\
G_{j+1/2}&=\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta y_j}
\left(v_{j+1/2}^{+}N_{j+1}^{n+1}
+v_{j+1/2}^{-}N_j^{n+1}\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
positive \(v\) moves electrons toward lower energy and the low-energy boundary
is closed.  The high-energy loss flux is solved at the new radius rather than
applied explicitly.

Electron injection remains a finite-shell source per unit radius.  For a substep
beginning at \(R_s\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
V(R)&=\frac{4\pi R^3}{3},\\
q_R(R_s,\Delta R)&\equiv
n(R_s)\frac{V(R_s+\Delta R)-V(R_s)}{\Delta R},\\
a_p(R)&\equiv
f_e\,\frac{1-p}{\gamma_{\max}^{1-p}(R)-\gamma_m^{1-p}(R)} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Uniform media without thermal electrons can merge fixed substeps into one
implicit solve.  Otherwise the solver re-evaluates the local density, field, and
injection scales on each substep.  Both paths use the same source and cooling
terms, with details in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

Adaptive substeps compare one full implicit step with two half steps:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{I}&\equiv
\left\{j\,\middle|\,N_j^{\rm h}>0\right\},\\
\epsilon_{\rm adapt}&=
\max_{j\in\mathcal{I}}
\frac{|N_j^{\rm h}-N_j^{\rm f}|}
{N_j^{\rm h}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The accepted state is \(N_j^{\rm h}\) when
\(\epsilon_{\rm adapt}\le\epsilon_{\rm tol}\); otherwise the trial step is
reduced down to \((R_i-R_{i-1})/N_{\rm sub,max}\).  The controller changes only
the radial step size and does not modify the electron spectrum after the solve.

\subsection{Coupled \texttt{fullhide} electron--photon pass}

The coupled electron--photon pass keeps the \texttt{fullhide} electron state but
lets the supplied photon field alter cooling and add secondary pairs.  On the
\(x=\ln\gamma_e\) grid, each radial substep receives \(n_\nu^{\rm cool}\) and
\(Q_{x,j}^{\rm sec}\).  The former sets the inverse-Compton cooling coefficient
and the local synchrotron field sets the self-absorption heating, while the cell
source is
\begin{equation}
Q_{x,j}=Q_{x,j}^{\rm inj}+Q_{x,j}^{\rm sec},
\end{equation}
where \(Q_{x,j}^{\rm inj}\) is the primary source on the same finite radial
shell.  The conservative triangular sweep is unchanged except for the
right-hand side and the face drift,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_{j+1/2}&=\bar D_{j+1/2}+\frac{1}{R},\\
\lambda_{j+1/2}&=-\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta x}w_{j+1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar D_{j+1/2}\) is the face-averaged radiative cooling coefficient, and
\(1/R\) is the adiabatic drift in log energy.  The high-to-low sweep is the one
defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:triangular-cooling-sweep}; the coupled diagonal and
right-hand-side details are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The
step returns a local electron spectrum; observer projection is unchanged.  At
the last output radius, \(\nu_m\), \(\nu_c\), and \(\nu_a\) are recomputed on
the same synchrotron optical-depth grid used by the separated path, so the
reported break arrays describe the coupled post-cooling state.

\subsection{Hybrid thermal \texttt{fullhide} branch}

The \texttt{fullhide\_1d\_hz} branch follows the same shell state and the same
finite-volume update as \texttt{fullhide\_1d}; only the transported coordinate
and source shape change when \texttt{thermal\_electrons} is enabled.  It evolves
the cell average \(N_{y,j}\) in Equation~\eqref{eq:fullhide-cell-average}, with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\varpi_e&=\gamma_e^2-1,\qquad s=\gamma_s^2-1,\\
y&=\ln\!\left(1+\frac{\varpi_e}{s}\right),\\
\gamma_e(y)&=\left[1+s(\exp(y)-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\frac{dx}{dy}&=\frac{s\,\exp(y)}{2\gamma_e^2},\qquad
\frac{dN_e}{dy}=\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\gamma_e\frac{dx}{dy}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(s\) sets the transition scale of the \(y\) coordinate, while radiation
calls continue to use \(\gamma_e\).

The hybrid branch uses the same finite-shell factor \(q_R(R_s,\Delta R)\) to
normalize new electrons.  Without thermal electrons the source is the ordinary
nonthermal shape on the \(y\) grid; with thermal electrons it is the same swept
population distributed over a unit-normalized hybrid density:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{y,j}^{\rm S}&=
\frac{q_R(R_s,\Delta R)a_p(R_s)}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}\phi_y(R_s,y)\,dy,\\
Q_{y,j}^{\rm h}&=
\frac{q_R(R_s,\Delta R)}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}
\phi_{\rm h}(\gamma_e)\gamma_e\frac{dx}{dy}\,dy .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian \(\gamma_e\,dx/dy\) converts the physical density in
\(\gamma_e\) into the transported \(y\) coordinate.

The hybrid density assigns a fraction \(1-f_e\) of the newly swept electrons to
the thermal part below \(\gamma_m\) and a fraction \(f_e\) to the cutoff
power-law tail:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\phi_{\rm h}(\gamma)&=
\begin{cases}
(1-f_e)\phi_{\rm th}(\gamma), & 1<\gamma<\gamma_m,\\
f_e\phi_{\rm pl}(\gamma), & \gamma\ge\gamma_m .
\end{cases}\\
\phi_{\rm th}(\gamma)&=
\frac{\gamma\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}\exp(-\gamma/\Theta)}
{I_{\rm th}},\\
\phi_{\rm pl}(\gamma)&=
\frac{\gamma^{-p}\exp(-\gamma/\gamma_{\max})}{I_{\rm pl}},\\
I_{\rm th}(\gamma_m,\Theta)&=
\int_1^{\gamma_m}\gamma\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}
\exp(-\gamma/\Theta)\,d\gamma,\\
I_{\rm pl}(\gamma_m,\gamma_{\max},p)&=
\gamma_{\max}^{1-p}\Gamma\!\left(1-p,\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma_{\max}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The normalizations \(I_{\rm th}\) and \(I_{\rm pl}\) cover the two active
supports.  The temperature is fixed by continuity at \(\gamma_m\), not by
tuning:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(1-f_e)\phi_{\rm th}(\gamma_m)&=f_e\phi_{\rm pl}(\gamma_m),\\
F(\Theta)&=\frac{\gamma_m}{\Theta}+\ln I_{\rm th}-\ln C=0,\\
\ln C&=
\ln\!\left(\frac{1-f_e}{f_e}\right)
+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\gamma_m^2-1)\\
&\quad +(1+p)\ln\gamma_m
+\ln I_{\rm pl}+\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma_{\max}},\\
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with analytic derivative and scalar update
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_2(\gamma_m,\Theta)&=
\int_1^{\gamma_m}\gamma^2\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}
\exp(-\gamma/\Theta)\,d\gamma,\\
\frac{dF}{d\Theta}&=
-\frac{\gamma_m}{\Theta^2}
+\frac{I_2}{\Theta^2I_{\rm th}},\\
D_r&\equiv\left.\frac{dF}{d\Theta}\right|_{\Theta_r},\\
\Theta_{r+1}&=\Theta_r-\frac{F(\Theta_r)}{D_r}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The solved \(\Theta\) sets the thermal width of the hybrid source.  The branch
requires a positive solution of this equation; otherwise the thermal-electron
source is not an accepted local state.

The assembled hybrid source is advanced with the same implicit finite-volume
system as \texttt{fullhide\_1d}.  Special-function evaluation, thermal-tail
moment subtraction, and root-seeding details are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}; the final output radius is evaluated from
the final spectrum on the active-shell synchrotron optical-depth grid.

\subsection{Semi-Lagrangian log-\texorpdfstring{\(\gamma\)}{gamma} solver}

The \texttt{slc1\_1d} branch solves the same electron cooling problem with a
semi-Lagrangian remap.  It uses the uniform coordinate
\(x=\ln\gamma_e\), stores the conservative cell averages \(N_{x,j}\), and
shares the local \(B'\), \(\gamma_m\), \(\gamma_{\max}\), \(\gamma_c\), seed
synchrotron state, and cooling array with the log-\(\gamma\) \texttt{fullhide}
path.  Each output shell is split as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_i&=R_i-R_{i-1},\\
L_i&=\max\!\left[
N_{\rm S,min},
\min\!\left(N_{\rm S,max},
\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta R_i}{\Delta R_{\rm S}}\right\rfloor\right)
\right],\\
\Delta R&=\frac{\Delta R_i}{L_i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(L_i\) is the number of radial substeps, \(\Delta R\) is the substep width,
and \(\Delta R_{\rm S}\), \(N_{\rm S,min}\), and \(N_{\rm S,max}\) are fixed
transport controls.

For a substep from \(R_L\) to \(R_R=R_L+\Delta R\), non-uniform media are sampled
at
\begin{equation}
R_m=\frac{R_L+R_R}{2},\qquad n_m=n(R_m),\qquad n_R=n(R_R),
\end{equation}
while a uniform medium uses the shell density.  The log-\(\gamma\) source and
cooling face speed are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{x,j}^{\rm S}&=
\frac{q_R(R_m,\Delta R)a_p(R_m)}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}
\psi_x(x)\,dx,\\
\psi_x(x)&=\gamma_e^{1-p}\mathcal C_{\max}(\gamma_e),\\
\gamma_e&=\exp(x),\\
f_{j+1/2,m}&=
\frac{D_{j,m}+D_{j+1,m}}{2}+\frac{1}{R_m},\\
D_{j,m}&=D_{j,i-1}\frac{n_m}{n_{i-1}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\psi_x\) gives the nonthermal source per unit \(x\), and the cooling array
is rescaled only by the local density sample.

The semi-Lagrangian update treats cooling as motion across the log-energy grid.
One substep adds half of \(Q_{x,j}^{\rm S}\), traces cell faces backward along the
cooling characteristics, remaps the conserved content between the two departure
faces, and then adds the remaining half of the source.  The detailed prefix
integral and characteristic-face formulas are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The output of the substep is a new
conservative log-\(\gamma\) spectrum \(N_{x,j}^{n+1}\).

After the last substep, the code converts the transported \(dN_e/dx\) cells to a
centered \(dN_e/d\gamma_e\) spectrum with the same conservative exponential
cell-shape conversion used by the other solvers.  The \(\nu_m\), \(\nu_c\), and
\(\nu_a\) arrays are shell-left diagnostics for the active output slots
\(1,\ldots,N_R-1\).  The self-absorption frequency \(\nu_a\) comes from the same
synchrotron optical-depth grid used for the emitted spectrum.

\subsection{Three-level log-\texorpdfstring{\(\gamma\)}{gamma} solver}

The \texttt{t2g1\_1d} branch keeps the same log-\(\gamma\) state, source shape,
and shell-left diagnostics as \texttt{slc1\_1d}.  Its difference is the radial
discretization: it uses a three-level implicit upwind solve instead of a
semi-Lagrangian remap.  Each output shell uses the same fixed substep count
\(L_i\), and each substep advances the source radius to
\(R_\ell=R_{\ell-1}+\Delta R\).  The source, density-scaled cooling speed, and
signed face step are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{x,j}^{\rm T}&=
\frac{q_R(R_\ell,\Delta R)a_p(R_\ell)}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}
\psi_x(x)\,dx,\\
f_{j+1/2,\ell}&=
\frac{D_{j,\ell}+D_{j+1,\ell}}{2}+\frac{1}{R_\ell},\\
D_{j,\ell}&=D_{j,i-1}\frac{n(R_\ell)}{n_{i-1}},\\
\lambda_{j+1/2}&=-\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta x}f_{j+1/2,\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(q_R\), \(a_p\), and the density correction are all evaluated at
\(R_\ell\).

The first substeps do not yet have two previous spectra.  The branch therefore
starts with backward-Euler sweeps using the same high-to-low triangular primitive
as the fixed-grid \texttt{fullhide} update.  The output of this startup stage is
the pair of history spectra needed by the second-order radial solve.

The three-level radial solve uses a second-order BDF2 balance after two history
states are available:
\begin{equation}
\frac{3N_j^{n+1}-4N_j^n+N_j^{n-1}}{2\Delta R}
+\mathcal{L}_j(N^{n+1})=Q_{x,j}^{\rm T},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}\) is the one-sided cooling-advection operator encoded by the
face coefficients.  Both the startup and BDF2 phases use the shared high-to-low
sweep in Equation~\eqref{eq:triangular-cooling-sweep}.  The corresponding
diagonal, right-hand-side, and coupling coefficients are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The step output is the updated
log-\(\gamma\) spectrum and its shifted one-step history.

After each accepted substep, the pair \((N^n,N^{n-1})\) is shifted forward.  At
the final substep of each output shell, the code converts \(dN_e/dx\) to a
centered \(dN_e/d\gamma_e\) spectrum with the same conservative exponential
cell-shape reconstruction used by the other log-\(\gamma\) drivers.

\subsection{WENO finite-volume solver}

The \texttt{weno5\_1d} branch moves the electron spectrum with an explicit
finite-volume flux on the same uniform log-\(\gamma\) state as the other
one-dimensional branches:
\begin{equation}
x=\ln\gamma_e,\qquad
N_{x,j}(R)=\frac{1}{\Delta x}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}\frac{dN_e}{dx}\,dx .
\end{equation}
For the output shell \(R_{i-1}\rightarrow R_i\), the shell-left shock state sets
the magnetic field, acceleration cutoff, cooling break, and synchrotron drift:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm loc}&=R_{i-1},\qquad
\bar\Gamma_i=\frac{\Gamma_i+\Gamma_{i-1}}{2},\\
B'_i&=\left[32\pi\epsilon_B n(R_{\rm loc})m_pc^2
\bar\Gamma_i(\bar\Gamma_i-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{\max,i}&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8B'_ie^3)^{1/2}},\\
a_{c,i}&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i'^2\bar\Gamma_i t_{{\rm obs},i}}
{6\pi m_ec(1+z)},\\
\gamma_{c,i}&=a_{c,i}^{-1},\\
\beta_i&=(1-\bar\Gamma_i^{-2})^{1/2},\\
a_i&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i'^2}
{6\pi m_ec^2\beta_i\bar\Gamma_i},\qquad
\frac{d\gamma_j}{dR}=-D_j\gamma_j .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(a_i\gamma_e\) is the synchrotron part of the energy drift per unit radius,
and \(D_j\) stores the total radiative drift used as the WENO face velocity.

The WENO update solves the conservative log-energy equation with an explicit
face flux,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial N_x}{\partial R}
&=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(v_xN_x\right)+Q_x,\\
v_{x,j+1/2}
&=
\frac{D_j+D_{j+1}}{2}+R^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(Q_x\) is the injected source per unit radius and the high-energy outer
face is extrapolated from the last interior cooling face.  The first trial
radial step and the final explicit substep count are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_{W,0}&=
\frac{\eta_W}
{a_i\gamma_{\max,i}+\eta_{\rm ad}/(R_i+R_{i-1})},\\
v_W&=\max_j|v_{x,j+1/2}|,\\
N_W^{\rm d}&=\left\lfloor
\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{\Delta R_{W,0}}
\right\rfloor+N_W^+,\\
N_W^{\rm f}&=\left\lceil
\frac{(R_i-R_{i-1})v_W}
{\eta_{\rm CFL,W}\,\Delta x}
\right\rceil,\\
L_i&=\max\left(N_W^{\rm d},N_W^{\rm f}\right),\\
\Delta R&=\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{L_i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The fixed controls \(\eta_W\), \(\eta_{\rm ad}\), \(N_W^+\), and
\(\eta_{\rm CFL,W}\) set the drift and flux limits.

The explicit source uses the same finite-shell swept-particle count as the other
electron solvers.  At the substep source radius \(R_s\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{x,j}^{\rm W}&=
\frac{q_R(R_s,\Delta R)a_{p,i}}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}
\psi_x(x)\,dx,\\
q_R(R_s,\Delta R)&\equiv
n(R_s)\frac{V(R_s+\Delta R)-V(R_s)}{\Delta R},\\
a_{p,i}&\equiv
f_e\frac{1-p}{\gamma_{\max,i}^{1-p}-\gamma_{m,i}^{1-p}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(V(R)=4\pi R^3/3\), \(q_R\) gives the swept upstream electrons per unit
radius, and \(a_{p,i}\) normalizes the injected power law.  In this explicit
branch, \(\gamma_m\) and \(\gamma_{\max}\) stay fixed at the shell-level shock
values during the output shell.

The WENO-Z step reconstructs the face flux from neighboring cell fluxes.  The
flux variable is \(F=v_xN_x\).  Because the equation contains
\(+\partial F/\partial x\), positive \(v_x\) moves particles toward lower \(x\).
Thus positive \(v_x\) uses the right-biased stencil, and negative \(v_x\) uses
the left-biased stencil.  For either bias, the face flux is
\begin{equation}
F_{i+1/2}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\omega_k\hat f_k .
\end{equation}
With linear weights \(d=(1/10,3/5,3/10)\), the WENO-Z weights are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_5&=|\beta_0-\beta_2|,\\
\alpha_k&=d_k\left[1+
\left(\frac{\tau_5}{\beta_k+\epsilon_W}\right)^2\right],
\\
\omega_k&=\frac{\alpha_k}{\sum_m\alpha_m}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\hat f_k\) are the three fifth-order candidate fluxes, \(\beta_k\) are the
Jiang--Shu smoothness indicators, and \(\epsilon_W\) is the smoothness-weight
floor.  Their explicit forms and boundary stencil bookkeeping are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The reconstructed \(F_{i+1/2}\) is the
flux used by the explicit radial update.

The explicit radial step applies this flux with a third-order
strong-stability-preserving Runge--Kutta update.  With
\begin{equation}
\lambda_W=\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta x},
\qquad
\mathcal{D}_j(N)=F_{j+1/2}(N)-F_{j-1/2}(N),
\end{equation}
the three Runge--Kutta stages use the same substep source \(Q_{x,j}^{\rm W}\);
their coefficients are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The step
output is the post-substep conservative spectrum \(N_{x,j}^{n+1}\).

After each substep, the stored conservative spectrum is projected to
\(N_x\ge0\).  This projection acts only on transported electron cell averages
and does not smooth neighboring bins or alter a light curve after transport.  At
the last substep in an output shell, the same exponential cell-shape
reconstruction used by the log-\(\gamma\) solvers gives
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\right|_j
=\frac{N_{x,j}^{\rm exp}}{\gamma_j}.
\end{equation}
Here \(N_{x,j}^{\rm exp}\) is the reconstructed log-energy cell content.  The
radiation arrays written during shell preparation are shell-left fields used to
build the cooling seed; the final output point has no following shell, so the
driver refreshes \(L_\nu^{\rm syn}\) and \(n_\nu^{\rm syn}\) from the final
transported spectrum and final dynamics-grid field.

\subsection{Direct characteristic-integration solver}

The \texttt{charint\_1d} solver follows electron cooling by tracing
characteristics backward in energy.  It evolves the same conservative
log-\(\gamma\) cell averages as the other one-dimensional solvers,
\begin{equation}
N_{x,j}(R)=\frac{1}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}\frac{dN_e}{dx}\,dx,
\qquad x=\ln\gamma_e,
\end{equation}
and obtains the content of each arrival cell from the departure interval reached
by the backward characteristic.

For an output shell \(R_{i-1}\rightarrow R_i\), the local shock state and cooling
scales are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm loc}&=R_{i-1},\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm sh}=\frac{\Gamma_i+\Gamma_{i-1}}{2},\qquad
n_{\rm ext}=n(R_{\rm loc}),\\
B'&=\left[32\pi\epsilon_B n_{\rm ext}m_pc^2
\Gamma_{\rm sh}(\Gamma_{\rm sh}-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{\max}&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8B'e^3)^{1/2}},\\
a_c&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B'^2\Gamma_{\rm sh}t_{{\rm obs},i}}
{6\pi m_ec(1+z)},\\
\gamma_c&=a_c^{-1},\\
\beta_{\rm sh}&=(1-\Gamma_{\rm sh}^{-2})^{1/2},\\
\Xi_e&=\frac{\epsilon_e}{f_e}\frac{m_p}{m_e}(\Gamma_{\rm sh}-1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The minimum injection Lorentz factor is the same branch solution
\(\gamma_m(\Xi_e,\gamma_{\max},p)\) used by the other forward-shock solvers.  In
a non-uniform density profile, the same \(B'\), \(\gamma_{\max}\), and
\(\gamma_m\) formulae are re-evaluated at
\begin{equation}
R_m=\frac{R_i+R_{i-1}}{2},
\qquad n_m=n(R_m) .
\end{equation}

The characteristic radial lag must resolve the fastest cooling.  Without
adaptive substeps, the shell is split according to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{\rm syn}&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B'^2}{6\pi m_ec^2\beta_{\rm sh}\Gamma_{\rm sh}},\\
\Delta R_{\rm ch}&=\frac{\eta_{\rm CFL}}
{a_{\rm syn}\gamma_{\max}+\eta_{\rm ad}/R_m},\\
N_i^{\rm ch}&=\left\lceil\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{\Delta R_{\rm ch}}\right\rceil,\\
L_i&=\max\left[
N_{\rm char,min},
\min\left(N_{\rm char,max},N_i^{\rm ch}\right)
\right],\\
\Delta R&=\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{L_i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(a_{\rm syn}\gamma_{\max}\) estimates the fastest synchrotron drift and
\(\eta_{\rm ad}/R_m\) estimates the adiabatic drift.

Adaptive characteristic substeps choose the radial lag from a local density and
radius-change measure.  The allowed interval and acceptance metric are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_{\min}&=\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{N_{\rm char,max}},\\
\Delta R_{\max}&=\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{N_{\rm char,min}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The acceptance metric is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_g&=\Delta R/R_s,\\
\epsilon_n&=\frac{|n(R_s+\Delta R)-n(R_s)|}{n(R_s)} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The comparison variable is then
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_R=
\begin{cases}
\epsilon_g, & \text{uniform medium},\\
\max\left(\epsilon_g,\epsilon_n\right), & \text{density jump}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A trial is accepted when \(\epsilon_R\le\eta_{\rm tol}\epsilon_{\rm sub}\), or
when the lower bound has been reached.  This criterion selects the radial
quadrature of the characteristic map and does not change the density profile.

The characteristic source is normalized by the finite shell swept at the substep
radius.  At evaluation radius \(R_s\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n_s&\equiv n(R_s),\\
V(R)&=\frac{4\pi R^3}{3},\\
\Delta V_s&\equiv V(R_s+\Delta R)-V(R_s),\\
q_R(R_s,\Delta R)&=n_s\frac{\Delta V_s}{\Delta R},\\
I_p(R_s)&=\gamma_{\max}^{1-p}(R_s)-\gamma_m^{1-p}(R_s),\\
a_p(R_s)&=f_e\frac{1-p}{I_p(R_s)},\\
A_R^{\rm C}&=q_R(R_s,\Delta R)a_p(R_s).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The source cell is
\begin{equation}
Q_{x,j}^{\rm C}=\frac{A_R^{\rm C}}{\Delta x_j}
\int_{x_{j-1/2}}^{x_{j+1/2}}
\psi_{\rm C}(x)\,dx ,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\psi_{\rm C}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\gamma_e^{1-p}\mathcal C_{\max}(\gamma_e),
&\gamma_e\ge\gamma_m,\\
0,&\gamma_e<\gamma_m,
\end{cases}
\qquad \gamma_e=\exp(x),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\max}(\gamma_e)=
\begin{cases}
1, & \gamma_e\le\gamma_{\max},\\
\exp(1-\gamma_e/\gamma_{\max}), & \gamma_e>\gamma_{\max}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The source is zero below \(\gamma_m\), follows \(\gamma_e^{-p}\) above
\(\gamma_m\), and has a continuous high-energy turnover above \(\gamma_{\max}\).

The characteristic map is simple in \(u=1/\gamma_e\).  In the synchrotron-only
branch, one substep obeys
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\gamma_e}{dR}&=-a_{\rm syn}\gamma_e^2-b\gamma_e,\\
b&=R_s^{-1},\\
\frac{du}{dR}&=a_{\rm syn}+bu .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a face arriving at \(u\) after radial lag \(\ell\), the departure point is
\begin{equation}
u_{\rm back}=
\begin{cases}
u-a_{\rm syn}\ell, & b=0,\\
(u+a_{\rm syn}/b)e^{-b\ell}-a_{\rm syn}/b, & {\rm otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The general cooling branch uses the same idea after the total loss array has
been assembled from synchrotron, inverse-Compton, and self-absorption terms.  In
a non-uniform external medium, the shell loss array is rescaled by
\(n_s/n_{\rm ext}\).  The code then applies a piecewise affine map in
\(u=1/\gamma_e\), with the detailed cell-edge tracing given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The output of the map is the pair of
departure faces for each arrival cell.

The remap conserves electron number by integrating between the two traced faces.
Old electrons are integrated over the full radial lag \(\Delta R\).  Newly
injected electrons are integrated along the same characteristic with a clustered
Gauss rule.  The prefix-integral formula and quadrature weights are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The remap output is the next conservative
log-\(\gamma\) spectrum \(N_{x,j}^{n+1}\).

After each output shell, the solver converts the stored log-energy cells back to
a centered physical spectrum:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\bigg|_j
=\frac{N_{x,j}^{\rm exp}}{\gamma_j}.
\end{equation}
Here \(N_{x,j}^{\rm exp}\) is the local exponential reconstruction of
\(dN_e/dx\).  The reported break and self-absorption diagnostics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_m&=\frac{3eB'\gamma_m^2}
{4\pi m_ec\Gamma_{\rm sh}(1-\beta_{\rm sh})(1+z)},\\
\nu_c&=\frac{3eB'\gamma_c^2}
{4\pi m_ec\Gamma_{\rm sh}(1-\beta_{\rm sh})(1+z)},\\
\tau_\nu(\nu_a)&=1,\\
\nu_a^{\rm obs}&=\frac{\nu_a}
{\Gamma_{\rm sh}(1-\beta_{\rm sh})(1+z)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Discontinuous-Galerkin solver}

The \texttt{dg\_1d} solver represents the electron spectrum with local
polynomials instead of one value per cell.  It uses the same \(y\) coordinate as
the finite-volume branch and evolves
\begin{equation}
N_y(y,R)=\frac{dN_e}{dy}
=\frac{dN_e}{dx_g}\frac{dx_g}{dy}.
\end{equation}
Here \(x_g\) is the log-energy coordinate used for radiation diagnostics.  On
element \(K_a=[y_{a,L},y_{a,R}]\), the local coordinate
\(r\in[-1,1]\) is defined by \(y=y_a+\Delta y_ar/2\), and
\begin{equation}
N_y^h(y,R)\big|_{K_a}=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{p_{\rm DG}}N_{a\ell}(R)L_\ell(r),
\end{equation}
where \(L_\ell\) are Lagrange polynomials on Legendre--Gauss--Lobatto nodes.
The fixed diagnostic grid is the shared four-velocity grid with scale
\(\gamma_{\rm sc}\), whose high-energy edge is estimated from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\max}^{\rm grid}
&=\eta_{\rm tail}
\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8B'_{\rm ref}e^3)^{1/2}},\\
B'_{\rm ref}&=(8\pi\epsilon_B e'_{\rm ref})^{1/2},\\
e'_{\rm ref}
&=\kappa_{\rm FS}\Gamma_{\rm end}(\Gamma_{\rm end}-1)n(R_1)m_pc^2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\Gamma_{\rm end}\) is the last dynamics-grid Lorentz factor,
\(\eta_{\rm tail}\) sets the high-energy coverage, and \(\kappa_{\rm FS}\) is the
forward-shock energy-density factor used for this diagnostic grid estimate.
For each output shell, the moving active grid is rebuilt around the active
breaks allowed by the electron domain and mesh-separation tolerance.  The current
shell quantities are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar\Gamma_i&=\frac{\Gamma_i+\Gamma_{i-1}}{2},\\
B_i'&=\left[32\pi\epsilon_B n(R_{i-1})m_pc^2
\bar\Gamma_i(\bar\Gamma_i-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{\max,i}&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8B_i'e^3)^{1/2}},\\
\gamma_{m,i}&=\gamma_m\!\left[
\frac{\epsilon_e}{f_e}\frac{m_p}{m_e}(\bar\Gamma_i-\!1),
\gamma_{\max,i},p\right],\\
a_{c,i}&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i'^2\bar\Gamma_i t_{{\rm obs},i}}
{6\pi m_ec(1+z)},\\
\gamma_{c,i}&=a_{c,i}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The active intervals around \(\ln\gamma_{m,i}\), \(\ln\gamma_{c,i}\), and
\(\ln\gamma_{\max,i}\) are split into \(N_{\rm seg}\) elements.

The active DG mesh tracks the part of the electron spectrum that contains source
or surviving high-energy electrons.  It always includes the current injection,
cooling, and cutoff breaks when those breaks lie inside the electron domain.  It
also keeps a high-energy tail when the old DG state still carries the moment
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_2(x_0)\equiv
\int_{\gamma_e\ge\exp(x_0)}N_y\gamma_e^2\,dy .
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal{M}_2\) is a high-energy electron moment used only to choose the
active DG mesh.  The active-edge threshold formula is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The output is a DG element set built around
\(\gamma_{m,i}\), \(\gamma_{c,i}\), and \(\gamma_{\max,i}\).

The radial grid uses \(N_{\rm DG}\) regular substeps away from density jumps.
Near a Gaussian density structure, candidate steps are cut at the declared jump
markers and limited by the jump width and density-gradient scale.  These rules,
given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}, produce radial substeps that do
not cross a sharp density change in one DG solve.

At substep radius \(R_s\), the source is normalized to the same finite-shell
electron number as the finite-volume branches:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_y^{\rm D}(R_s)&=
q_R(R_s,\Delta R)a_p(R_s)\,\widetilde Q_y^{\rm D},\\
D_j(R_s)&=
D_j(R_{i-1})\frac{n(R_s)}{n(R_{i-1})}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\widetilde Q_y^{\rm D}\) is a conservative DG source shape, and the local
density scaling is used only for non-uniform media.  The DG substep solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial N_y}{\partial R}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
\left[
-\frac{D(R_s)+R_s^{-1}}
{dx_g/dy}N_y
\right]
=Q_y^{\rm D}(R_s),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the velocity term contains both radiative cooling and the adiabatic drift.

After each output radius, the moving DG polynomial is projected back to the fixed
diagnostic grid.  The projection uses cell-overlap quadrature:
\begin{equation}
\bar N_{y,j}^{\rm out}=
\frac{1}{\Delta y_j}
\sum_a\int_{[y_{j-1/2},y_{j+1/2}]\cap K_a}
N_y^h(y)\,dy .
\end{equation}
The reported electron spectrum is then
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\right|_j=
\frac{N_{y,j}^{\rm exp}}
{\gamma_j(dx_g/dy)_j},
\end{equation}
where \(N_{y,j}^{\rm exp}\) is the center value of the local exponential
reconstruction of the fixed-grid cell average.  The solver requires both the DG
integral and the projected fixed-grid integral to remain positive:
\begin{equation}
\int N_y^h\,dy>0,\qquad
\sum_j\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\bigg|_j
\gamma_j\Delta x_j>0,
\end{equation}
as state-domain conditions for the DG output.  The final output radius is
refreshed through the same synchrotron optical-depth grid used for \(\nu_a\), so
the returned \(\nu_m\), \(\nu_c\), and \(\nu_a\) arrays describe the final
electron spectrum.

DG source projection puts the finite-shell electron source on the polynomial
mesh.  A natural-log source \(Q_{x_g}\) is first converted to the DG coordinate,
\begin{equation}
Q_y(y)=Q_{x_g}[x_g(y)]\frac{dx_g}{dy}.
\end{equation}
so \(Q_y\,dy\) and \(Q_{x_g}\,dx_g\) carry the same injected electron number.
The forward source uses the shared cutoff power law, while the reverse-shock
kinetic source uses the normalized \((\gamma_e-1)^{-p}\) shape.  Both are
projected onto the local Legendre basis and evaluated at LGL nodes; the
projection integrals are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

The implicit DG step moves electrons through the \(y\) coordinate by cooling.
It solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial N_y}{\partial R}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(v_yN_y\right)&=Q_y,\\
v_y&=-\frac{D_{\rm rad}+D_{\rm ad}}
{dx_g/dy}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(D_{\rm rad}\) and \(D_{\rm ad}\) are positive radiative and adiabatic
loss coefficients.  The forward path uses \(D_{\rm ad}=R^{-1}\), while the
reverse path uses \(R^{-1}\) before crossing and the stored post-crossing volume
loss \((d\ln V_3/dR)/3\) after crossing.  In the public \texttt{dg\_1d} path,
each element advances the equation with a fully implicit nodal block,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_a N_a^{n+1}
=N_a^n+\Delta R\,Q_a+\mathcal F_a^{\rm up},
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal A_a\) is a \((p_{\rm DG}+1)\times(p_{\rm DG}+1)\) DG transport
block and \(\mathcal F_a^{\rm up}\) is the upwind face contribution.  Radiative
and adiabatic losses usually give \(v_y<0\), so the blocks are solved from high
energy to low energy.  If any node has \(v_y>0\), the same flux operator is
assembled as one global implicit system.  In four-velocity DG coordinates the
first low-energy face is closed, so cooled particles that leave the finite
energy range are kept in the first element.  The output is a conservative nodal
DG spectrum at the next radius; the block entries and face terms are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

DG remapping changes the polynomial mesh while preserving electron number.  If a
new coordinate \(y_{\rm n}\) and an old coordinate \(y_{\rm o}\) represent the
same \(\gamma_e\), the remap enforces
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm n}(y_{\rm n})\,dy_{\rm n}
=N_{\rm o}(y_{\rm o})\,dy_{\rm o},
\end{equation}
and checks the conserved total
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm tot}=\sum_a\frac{\Delta y_a}{2}
\sum_i w_iN_{a,i}.
\end{equation}
After projection or a radial solve, an element-local positivity operator keeps
the element average fixed while reducing only the high-order departure from that
average when a node becomes negative.  The optional Jackson or Fejer modal
operation acts only on high-order modal coefficients in flagged DG elements.  It
changes the DG representation of the electron spectrum, not the observer
light-curve assembly.  The output is a non-negative conservative DG state; the
local formulas are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

The DG characteristic projector maps conserved electron content between old and
new elements with the local affine loss law
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\gamma_e}{dR}=-a_{\rm rad}\gamma_e^2-b_{\rm ad}\gamma_e,
\end{equation}
where \(a_{\rm rad}\) and \(b_{\rm ad}\) are the radiative and adiabatic
coefficients.  The inverse map gives the departure energy for a chosen arrival
energy, then projects the upstream DG polynomial onto the arrival element.
Content whose characteristic exits below the first four-velocity element is
stored as low-energy mass and written back into the first element.  The public
\texttt{dg\_1d} forward light-curve path uses the fully implicit block solve
above for shell-local transport; the affine maps are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

\subsection{Cooling assembler}

The cooling assembler converts local radiative kernels into the radial loss
coefficient read by the electron transport solvers.  It combines synchrotron
cooling, inverse-Compton cooling, and synchrotron-self-absorption heating before
the transport solver adds adiabatic drift.  With \(x=\ln\gamma_e\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\gamma_i}{dR}&=-D_i\gamma_i,\\
v_{x,i+1/2}
&=\frac{D_i+D_{i+1}}{2}+R^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(D_i>0\) means cooling and positive \(v_x\) moves the distribution toward
lower \(\gamma_e\).  The synchrotron coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{\rm syn}&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es} B'^2}{6\pi m_e c^2\,\beta\Gamma},\\
\left.\frac{d\gamma_i}{dR}\right|_{\rm syn}
&=-a_{\rm syn}\gamma_i^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(B'\) is the comoving magnetic field and \(\beta\Gamma c\) converts
comoving cooling rates to radial rates.

The four cooling branches differ only in how they supply the Compton term.  Let
\(A_i^{\rm IC}\) be the tabulated inverse-Compton/Klein--Nishina coefficient,
\(A_i^{\rm abs}\) the synchrotron-self-absorption heating coefficient, and
\(Y_i\) an analytic Compton factor.  Photon-mediated terms use
\(dt'/dR=(\beta\Gamma c)^{-1}\), so
\begin{equation}
D_i=
\begin{cases}
a_{\rm syn}\gamma_i, & \mathrm{synchrotron\ only},\\[0.6ex]
\left[a_{\rm syn}+
\dfrac{A_i^{\rm IC}-A_i^{\rm abs}}{\beta\Gamma c}
\right]\gamma_i, & \mathrm{tabulated\ IC/KN},\\[2ex]
\left[a_{\rm syn}(1+Y_i^{\rm N})
-\dfrac{A_i^{\rm abs}}{\beta\Gamma c}
\right]\gamma_i, & \mathrm{Nakar},\\[2ex]
\left[a_{\rm syn}(1+Y_i^{\rm F})
-\dfrac{A_i^{\rm abs}}{\beta\Gamma c}
\right]\gamma_i, & \mathrm{Fan}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:electron-forward-cooling-assembly}
\end{equation}
The tabulated branch uses the local photon field directly.  The Nakar and Fan
branches replace that photon integral by analytic estimates of the Compton
factor.

The Nakar branch estimates inverse-Compton cooling from the synchrotron
luminosity below the Klein--Nishina threshold \citep{NakarAndoSari2009}.  For
electron node \(i\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat\nu_i&=\frac{m_e c^2}{h\gamma_i},\\
L_i^{<}&=
\int_{\nu_{\min}}^{\min(\hat\nu_i,\nu_{\max})}
L_\nu^{\rm syn}\,d\nu,\\
Y_i^{\rm N}&=
\frac{L_i^{<}}
{(4\pi R^2 c)(4\Gamma^2 n m_p c^2)},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the denominator is the shocked-shell energy flux scale.  The seed-frequency
integral uses the fixed logarithmic Gauss order \(N_{\rm G}\) in every frequency
cell.

The Fan branch estimates the Klein--Nishina correction from the fraction of
synchrotron photons below the electron-dependent threshold
\citep{FanPiran2008}.  For each electron node,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\eta_{\rm rad}&=\min\left[1,\left(\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma_c}\right)^{p-2}\right],\\
\hat\gamma_i&=
\left(\frac{m_e c^2}{hC_\nu B'\gamma_i}\right)^{1/2},\\
\gamma_M&=\gamma_{\max},\\
C_\nu&=\frac{3e}{4\pi m_e c}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:synch-frequency-coeff}
\end{equation}
Here \(\eta_{\rm rad}\) is the radiative electron-energy fraction,
\(\hat\gamma_i\) is the electron Lorentz factor whose synchrotron photons enter
the Klein--Nishina threshold for node \(i\), and \(C_\nu B'\gamma^2\) is the
synchrotron characteristic frequency.  With
\(\eta_i^{\rm KN}\) denoting the synchrotron photon-energy fraction below this
threshold, the analytic Compton factor is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_i&=\eta_{\rm rad}\eta_i^{\rm KN}\frac{\epsilon_e}{\epsilon_B},\\
Y_i^{\rm F}(1+Y_i^{\rm F})&=\Xi_i,\\
Y_i^{\rm F}=
\frac{-1+\sqrt{1+4\Xi_i}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The fraction \(\eta_i^{\rm KN}\) is evaluated from the fast- or slow-cooling
broken power-law spectrum.  It is zero below the lowest relevant break, unity
above \(\gamma_M\), and otherwise follows the analytic broken-spectrum integrals
listed in Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.  Synchrotron-self-absorption has the
opposite sign in Equation~\eqref{eq:electron-forward-cooling-assembly} because
absorbed synchrotron photons heat the same electron grid.

The cooling branches have a hard physics boundary.  Joint hadronic feedback uses
only the tabulated IC/KN path, because the secondary source
\(Q_{e,R}^{\rm sec}\) and the attenuated photon field must be evaluated against
the same local \(N_e,n_\gamma\) state.  An analytic \(Y\) closure would separate
these two objects.  The public Python cooling modes expose the synchrotron-only,
tabulated IC/KN, and Nakar-style branches.  The Fan-style branch is an internal
Fortran closure.

\subsection{Synchrotron-self-absorption heating kernel}

The SSA heating kernel returns \(A_i^{\rm abs}\) in
Equation~\eqref{eq:electron-forward-cooling-assembly} from the local seed-photon
density \(n_\nu\).  For an electron with Lorentz factor \(\gamma_i\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_B&=\frac{eB'}{2\pi m_e c},&
\nu_{\min,i}&=\frac{\nu_B}{\gamma_i},\\
\nu_{u,i}&=\frac{3eB'\gamma_i^2}{4\pi m_e c},&
B_Q&=\frac{m_e^2 c^3}{e\hbar},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ssa-frequency-scales}
\end{equation}
where \(\nu_B\) is the gyrofrequency, \(\nu_{\min,i}\) is the low-frequency
cutoff of the fit, \(\nu_{u,i}\) is the synchrotron turnover scale for that
electron, and \(B_Q=m_e^2c^3/(e\hbar)\) with \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\).

The adopted single-particle absorption fit is split at \(\nu_{u,i}\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_{L,i}&=\kappa_1\frac{B_Q}{B'}
(\chi_{\rm L}\nu_{\min,i})^{5/3},\\
S_{H,i}&=\kappa_2\frac{B_Q}{B'}\gamma_i^{-3},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_i(\nu)=
\begin{cases}
S_{L,i}\nu^{-5/3},
& \nu_{\min,i}<\nu\le\nu_{u,i},\\[2ex]
S_{H,i}\dfrac{\nu_B}{\nu}\exp(-\nu/\nu_{u,i}),
& \nu>\nu_{u,i},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where \(\kappa_1\), \(\kappa_2\), and \(\chi_{\rm L}\) are the fixed coefficients
of the adopted synchrotron-absorption asymptotic fit.  They belong to the
single-particle kernel and are not additional physical parameters.  The returned
heating coefficient is
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\rm abs}
=c\int_{\nu_{\min,i}}^{\nu_{\max}}
h\nu\,n_\nu\,\Sigma_i(\nu)\,d\nu .
\end{equation}
This integral counts the photon energy absorbed per unit time by electrons at
\(\gamma_i\).  The quadrature separates the low-frequency power-law and
high-frequency exponential pieces, clips only the boundary cells at
\(\nu_{\min,i}\) and \(\nu_{u,i}\), and uses the shared radiation interpolation
for \(n_\nu\).  Prefix-sum and high-frequency quadrature formulas are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsection{Grid-level inverse-Compton cooling kernel}

The grid-level inverse-Compton kernel computes \(A_i^{\rm IC}\) from the local
seed photon spectrum.  For an electron at \(\gamma_i\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_0&=\frac{h\nu_0}{m_e c^2},\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{h\nu}{m_e c^2},\\
\xi&=4\gamma_i\epsilon_0,\qquad
\epsilon_{\max}=\frac{4\gamma_i^2\epsilon_0}
{1+4\gamma_i\epsilon_0},\\
q&=\frac{\epsilon}{\xi(\gamma_i-\epsilon)} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Allowed low-frequency Thomson scatterings,
\(\nu_0/(4\gamma_i^2)<\nu\le\nu_0\), use
\begin{equation}
G_{\rm L}=\frac{\nu}{\nu_0}-\frac{1}{4\gamma_i^2},
\end{equation}
while the remaining allowed scatterings use the Jones kernel
\(G_{\rm J}(q,\xi)\) from Equation~\eqref{eq:ssc-kernel}.  The loss coefficient
passed to the cooling assembler is
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\rm IC}=
\frac{3h\sigma_{\rm es}}{4m_e c\gamma_i^3}
\int d\nu_0\,\frac{n_{\nu_0}}{\nu_0}
\int d\nu\,\nu\,G_{\rm loss}(\nu,\nu_0,\gamma_i),
\end{equation}
where \(G_{\rm loss}\) is the branch-selected kernel above.  This coefficient is
positive for inverse-Compton cooling, and it is the quantity inserted into
Equation~\eqref{eq:electron-forward-cooling-assembly}.

The coupled electron--photon budget path evaluates the same IC kernel on the
log-spaced seed and scattered-frequency grids.  It returns the auxiliary
coefficient \(A_i^{\rm IC,b}\) used by the implicit cooling solve in the joint
feedback calculation.  This path changes the numerical accumulation of the same
physical IC loss kernel; it does not define a separate cooling law.  The
log-frequency seed grid and Simpson-weighted budget coefficient are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsection{Synchrotron emissivity quadrature}

The synchrotron emissivity is computed from the transported electron spectrum.
Using the standard single-particle kernel,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_\nu^{\rm syn}(R)&=
\frac{\sqrt{3}e^3B'}{m_e c^2}
\int N_e(\gamma_e,R)
F\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_c}\right)d\gamma_e,\\
\nu_c&=C_\nu B'\gamma_e^2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(C_\nu\) is defined in Eq.~(\ref{eq:synch-frequency-coeff}).  Self-absorption
is applied after the emitted luminosity has been assembled
\citep{RybickiLightman1979,Sari1998,Granot2002}.

The fixed-grid branch integrates over the stored electron grid.  Fast paths use
the fit \(\Phi(x)\) from Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.  On
\(y_i=\ln\gamma_i\), with spacing \(\Delta y\), Simpson weights \(w_i^{\rm S}\),
and \(N_i=dN_e/d\gamma_e(\gamma_i)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu,{\rm em}}&=
C_{\rm syn}B'\frac{\Delta y}{3}
\sum_i w_i^{\rm S}N_i\gamma_i\\
&\quad\times
\Phi\!\left(\frac{\nu}{C_\nu B'\gamma_i^2}\right),\\
C_{\rm syn}&=\frac{\sqrt{3}e^3}{m_e c^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The factor \(N_i\gamma_i\,dy\) is the electron number in a logarithmic energy
interval.  The output is the unabsorbed luminosity passed to self-absorption and
observer projection.

\subsection{Synchrotron self-absorption shell transfer}
\label{sec:syn-shell-transfer}

The self-absorption depth is the optical depth of one shocked shell to its own
synchrotron photons.  The shell already stores its total electron spectrum, so
the calculation uses an electron column rather than a volume emissivity.  For
adjacent electron points,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_i=\frac{N_i}{\gamma_i^2},\qquad
\bar\gamma_{i+1/2}=\frac{\gamma_i+\gamma_{i+1}}{2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(N_i=dN_e/d\gamma_e\), \(q_i\) is the derivative variable used by the
absorption operator, and \(\bar\gamma_{i+1/2}\) is the midpoint Lorentz factor of
the cell face.  The shell optical depth is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_\nu&=
\frac{C_\tau B'}{4\pi R^2\nu^2}
\sum_i
\bar\gamma_{i+1/2}^2
\left(q_i-q_{i+1}\right)\\
&\quad\times
\Phi\!\left[
\frac{\nu}{C_\nu B'\bar\gamma_{i+1/2}^2}
\right],\\
C_\tau&=\frac{\sqrt{3}e^3}{8\pi m_e^2 c^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(R\) is the shock radius, \(B'\) is the comoving magnetic field, and
\(\Phi\) is the same synchrotron kernel used for emission.  The difference
\(q_i-q_{i+1}\) is the finite-volume form of the electron-spectrum derivative in
the self-absorption coefficient.  The output \(\tau_\nu(R)\) sets both the
escaping synchrotron luminosity and the self-absorption frequency.

The finite-cell transfer factor converts emitted synchrotron power into escaping
power.  For one uniform emitting and absorbing shell,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\psi(\tau_\nu)&=
\begin{cases}
1,&\tau_\nu=0,\\
\dfrac{1-\exp(-\tau_\nu)}{\tau_\nu},&\tau_\nu>0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:finite-cell-transfer}\\
L_\nu^{\rm syn}&=L_{\nu,{\rm em}}\psi(\tau_\nu),\\
n_\nu^{\rm syn}&=\frac{L_\nu^{\rm syn}}{4\pi R^2 c h\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(L_{\nu,{\rm em}}\) is the luminosity before absorption, \(L_\nu^{\rm syn}\)
is the escaping synchrotron luminosity, and \(n_\nu^{\rm syn}\) is the photon
number density used by the inverse-Compton kernel.  The optically thin limit is
the explicit value \(\psi(0)=1\).  The adaptive solution for \(\nu_a\) below uses
the same \(\tau_\nu\) grid, so the transfer output and the absorption-break
diagnostic come from the same shell state.

\subsection{Cyclotron-bin synchrotron extension}

The cyclotron-bin extension supplies the low-frequency radiation from mildly
relativistic electrons.  Electrons above the transition \(\gamma_{\rm cyc}\)
still use the synchrotron quadrature.  Electrons below it keep their stored
number in the same electron grid, but their radiated power is deposited into the
frequency bin that contains the fundamental cyclotron frequency.  For one
electron segment,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta N_i&=\frac{N_i+N_{i+1}}{2}(\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_i),\\
\bar\gamma_i&=\frac{\gamma_i+\gamma_{i+1}}{2},\qquad
\nu_0=\frac{\nu_B}{\bar\gamma_i},\\
\Delta L_{\nu_k,{\rm em}}&=
\frac{\Delta N_i}{\Delta\nu_k}
\frac{4}{3}\sigma_{\rm es}cU'_B
\bar\gamma_i^2(1-\bar\gamma_i^{-2}),\\
\nu_B&=\frac{eB'}{2\pi m_e c},\qquad
U'_B=\frac{B'^2}{8\pi}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\Delta N_i\) is the number of electrons in the segment, \(\bar\gamma_i\)
is its midpoint Lorentz factor, and \(\Delta\nu_k\) is the selected bin width.
This added power is a shell radiation output, not a change to the evolved
electron spectrum.  The same factor \(\psi(\tau_{\nu_k})\) converts it into the
escaping luminosity and the local seed photon density.

\subsection{Radiation interpolation and quadrature helpers}
\label{sec:positive-field-interpolation}

Radiation interpolation reads tabulated shell radiation fields between frequency
bins.  The same rule is used for emissivity, luminosity, and photon density:
positive adjacent endpoints define a local power law, while a segment with a
nonpositive endpoint is read linearly.  The logarithmic quadrature helper then
chooses support points for SSA heating, IC cooling, and SSC integrals without
changing the shell state.

The self-absorption heating segment adds absorbed photon energy back to electrons
in one frequency interval.  For local photon density \(n_\nu\),
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm SSA}=
\int_{\nu_0}^{\nu_1}\sigma_{\rm SSA}(\nu)n_\nu h\nu c\,d\nu .
\end{equation}
Here \(\sigma_{\rm SSA}\) is the self-absorption cross section used by the same
electron segment.  The output \(H_{\rm SSA}\) is the local energy return that
enters the electron energy-space update.  The interpolation formula, reduced
grid, cross-section branches, cutoff shape, and logarithmic quadrature rule are
given in Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsection{Self-absorption frequency solver}

The self-absorption frequency is the shell frequency where the synchrotron
optical depth reaches unity:
\begin{equation}
\tau_\nu(\nu_a)=1 .
\end{equation}
Because \(\nu_a\) does not usually sit on the stored radiation grid, the solver
evaluates \(\tau_\nu\) off-grid from the same electron spectrum and absorption
kernel used for the shell transfer.  The output \(\nu_a(R)\) is a diagnostic of
the local spectral break.  If the evaluated interval does not contain
\(\tau_\nu=1\), the reported value is the nearest interval boundary and should be
read as a limit.  The cell reconstruction, adaptive quadrature, search bounds,
and log-frequency root update are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsection{Synchrotron polarization kernel}

The synchrotron polarization kernel is a local emissivity diagnostic.  It splits
the single-electron synchrotron power into two linear-polarization components,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_\perp&\propto\frac{F(x)+G(x)}{2},\\
P_\parallel&\propto\frac{F(x)-G(x)}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(P_\perp\) and \(P_\parallel\) are the two linear-polarization emissivity
components and \(x\) is the usual synchrotron frequency ratio.  The functions
\(F(x)\), \(G(x)\), and their quadrature branches are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.  Weighting by the local electron distribution
gives
\begin{equation}
\Pi_\nu=
\frac{\int N_e(\gamma_e)[P_\perp(x)-P_\parallel(x)]d\gamma_e}
{\int N_e(\gamma_e)[P_\perp(x)+P_\parallel(x)]d\gamma_e},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu,\perp}&=\frac{1+\Pi_\nu}{2}L_\nu^{\rm syn},\\
L_{\nu,\parallel}&=\frac{1-\Pi_\nu}{2}L_\nu^{\rm syn}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These components are local shell diagnostics.  The present public observer
interface does not provide a full polarization projection product.

The \(\chi\)-resolved diagnostic tracks how absorption accumulates through
ordered downstream zones:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\nu,\le b}&=\sum_{a=1}^b\tau_{\nu,a},\\
\tau_{\nu,\le b}(\nu_{{\rm a},b})&=1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The shell-reduced spectra are log-\(\chi\) integrals,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu,{\rm sh}}&=\sum_a \chi_a\Delta\eta\,L_{\nu,a},\\
n_{\nu,{\rm sh}}&=\sum_a \chi_a\Delta\eta\,n_{\nu,a}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The output remains a shell-level record of intrinsic polarization and absorption;
it is not a sky-image or Stokes-parameter observer calculation.

\subsection{Adaptive radiation-grid resampling}

The adaptive radiation-grid module selects support points for a synchrotron
quadrature.  It does not evolve, renormalize, or change the transported electron
spectrum \(N_e\).  Its input is the already computed list
\((\gamma_i,N_{e,i})\), and its output is a smaller list used only by the
emissivity integral.  The direct wrapper
\texttt{electron\_radiation\_grid} is compiled with the electron common kernels
but is not called by the inspected production light-curve kernels, so it is
documented as a standalone helper.

The selected grid preserves the ends of the positive emitting support and adds
quadrature points near curvature in the electron spectrum.  Power-law portions
need little support, while cooling breaks, injection turnovers, and cutoffs need
denser sampling.  The helper uses logarithmic coordinates only to choose support
points; it returns selected indices and never replaces the physical electron
spectrum.  The active-support scan, curvature estimator, and point-selection
rule are given in Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\chi\)}{chi}-resolved downstream electron state}

The \(\chi\)-resolved forward-electron branch stores the electron population on
a finite downstream mass coordinate \(q\); \(\chi\) is only the derived diagnostic
coordinate used for display and projection.  For gas with adiabatic index
\(\hat\gamma\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_{\rm sh}&=\frac{\hat\gamma+1}{\hat\gamma-1},\\
q_\ast&=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{\rm sh}}\right)^{r_{\rm sh}},\\
\Delta q&=\frac{q_\ast}{N_\chi},\qquad
q_{a+1/2}=a\Delta q .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(a\) labels downstream cells and \(q=0\) is the shock front.  The default
two-dimensional finite-volume branch evolves
\begin{equation}
U_{ia}(R)=\frac{1}{\Delta y_i\Delta q}
\int_{\Delta y_i}\int_{\Delta q_a}
\frac{\partial^2 N_e}{\partial y\,\partial q}\,dq\,dy ,
\end{equation}
where \(y\) is the four-velocity coordinate defined above, and recovers the
shell spectrum as \(N_i(R_j)=\sum_a U_{ia}(R_j)\Delta q\).  The characteristic
and optional pulsar-wind branches use \(x=\ln\gamma_e\) on the same \(q\) domain;
their initialization and coordinate maps are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

Before cooling and emission are evaluated, the geometry module assigns each
\(q\) cell \((r_a,\beta_a,\Gamma_a,t'_a)\) through the finite-speed bridge between
the Blandford--McKee and planar strong-shock limits.  The transported electron
content remains on \(q\), while the local magnetic fraction is either constant or
decays with the comoving age:
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{B,a}=\epsilon_{B,\min}
 +(\epsilon_B-\epsilon_{B,\min})
\left(1+\frac{t'_a}{t_B}\right)^{\alpha_t}.
\end{equation}
When magnetic decay is inactive, \(\epsilon_{B,a}=\epsilon_B\).  The local field
is then set by the post-shock energy density,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e'_a&=r_{\rm sh}\Gamma_f(\Gamma_f-1)n(R)m_p c^2,\\
B'_a&=\left(8\pi\epsilon_{B,a}e'_a\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(n(R)\) is the upstream external density.  The output
\((r_a,\Gamma_a,B'_a)\) fixes the downstream cooling, synchrotron,
self-absorption, and diffusion coefficients.

Each downstream cell then carries its own synchrotron luminosity and
self-absorption depth.  For adjacent electron bins,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar\gamma_i&=\frac{\gamma_i+\gamma_{i+1}}{2},\\
E_{ia}&=\frac{N_{ia}+N_{i+1,a}}{2}(\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_i),\\
A_{ia}&=\bar\gamma_i^2
\left(\frac{N_{ia}}{\gamma_i^2}
-\frac{N_{i+1,a}}{\gamma_{i+1}^2}\right),\\
\nu_{ia}&=C_\nu B'_a\bar\gamma_i^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(E_{ia}\), \(A_{ia}\), and \(\nu_{ia}\) are the segment population, SSA
difference, and characteristic synchrotron frequency.  With \(w_a=\Delta q\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu,a}^{\rm em}&=C_{\rm syn}B'_a
\sum_iE_{ia}\Phi\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_{ia}}\right),\\
\tau_{\nu,a}&=
\frac{C_\tau B'_a w_a}{4\pi R^2\nu^2}
\sum_iA_{ia}\Phi\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_{ia}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite-cell transfer factor \(\psi\), defined in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:finite-cell-transfer}), gives
\[
L_{\nu,a}=L_{\nu,a}^{\rm em}\psi(\tau_{\nu,a}),\qquad
n_{\nu,a}=\frac{L_{\nu,a}}{4\pi R^2 c h\nu}.
\]
This local radiation state is the input to photon-history cooling and later
observer projection.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\chi\)}{chi}-resolved photon-history cooling}

The photon-history field is a causal comoving light-cone cache on the finite
\(q\) grid.  Over a radial interval \([R_{j-1},R_j]\), a source cell
\(s=(m,a)\) contributes to a target cell \(t=(j,b)\) only when it is behind the
target and inside the target light cone.  The resulting photon field supplies
the cooling assembler in Eq.~(\ref{eq:electron-forward-cooling-assembly}).

The relative cell motion sets the source frequency sampled by a target frequency:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{st}&=\frac{\beta_t-\beta_s}{1-\beta_t\beta_s},\\
{\cal D}_{st}&=\frac{1+\beta_{st}}{(1-\beta_{st}^2)^{1/2}},
\qquad
\nu'_s=\frac{\nu'}{{\cal D}_{st}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The source field is read from positive stored luminosity and photon-density bins.
The survival factor \({\cal A}_{\nu'}\) carries the SSA and \(\gamma\gamma\)
path opacity on the reduced cooling grid; overlap, interpolation, and path-product
details are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

For target cell \(t\), the attenuated history contribution is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L_{\nu',t}^{\rm hist}
&=w_s{\cal D}_{st}^3
{\cal A}_{\nu'} Q_{L,s}\!\left(\frac{\nu'}{{\cal D}_{st}}\right),\\
\Delta n_{\nu',t}^{\rm hist}
&=w_s{\cal D}_{st}^2
{\cal A}_{\nu'} Q_{n,s}\!\left(\frac{\nu'}{{\cal D}_{st}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(w_s\) is the source-light-cone overlap and \(Q_{L,s},Q_{n,s}\) are the
stored source luminosity and photon-density fields.  The powers
\({\cal D}_{st}^3\) and \({\cal D}_{st}^2\) transform luminosity and photon
density.  Adding the streamed field to the local synchrotron seed gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu',b}^{\rm cool}(R_j)
&=L_{\nu',b}^{\rm local}(R_{j-1})
+P_{\nu',b}^{\rm stream}(R_j),\\
n_{\nu',b}^{\rm cool}(R_j)
&=n_{\nu',b}^{\rm local}(R_{j-1})
+n_{\nu',b}^{\rm stream}(R_j).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
on the reduced cooling-frequency grid.  The assembler returns \(D_{ib}\) in
\(d\gamma_i/dR=-D_{ib}\gamma_i\), using the same synchrotron, IC/KN, Nakar, Fan,
and self-absorption terms as the one-dimensional solver.  These history arrays
enter only local cooling; observer-time projection uses the stored synchrotron
luminosity, optical depth, radius, Lorentz factor, and volume-weight arrays.

\subsection{Downstream \texorpdfstring{\(q\)}{q}-space advection--diffusion}

The \(q\)-space transport step moves electrons through the finite downstream
layer before the energy update.  For one electron-energy bin \(i\), the cell
average \(U_i(q,R)\) obeys
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial R}
+\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\!\left[A_q(q,R)U_i\right]
&=
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\!\left[
\mathcal K_i(q,R)\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial q}
\right]
+S_i(q,R),\\
\mathcal K_i(q,R)
&=\frac{\kappa_i(q,R)}{\beta_{\rm sh}c}
\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial s}\right)^2,\\
A_q(q,R)&=\frac{(3-k)(q_\ast-q)}{R}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_q\) is the expansion drift, \(\mathcal K_i\) is the Bohm-diffusion
coefficient after the coordinate change from shock-frame distance \(s\) to \(q\),
\(k\) is the external density index in \(n\propto R^{-k}\), and
\(\beta_{\rm sh}\) is the shock speed.  Because \(A_q(q_\ast,R)=0\), the
transport domain is the resolved downstream layer.

The shock-front source places newly accelerated electrons in the first downstream
cell,
\begin{equation}
U_{ia}^{\rm src}-U_{ia}^{n}
=\frac{Q_i(R,\Delta R)}{\Delta q}\,\delta_{a1},
\end{equation}
where \(Q_i(R,\Delta R)\) is the finite radial source in electron-energy bin
\(i\).  Summing over \(q\) gives the same electron number as the one-zone
forward-shock source.

The conservative \(q\)-transport update combines drift, diffusion, and the
shock-front source before the energy update.  On a face,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{a+1/2}^{+}&=\max(A_{a+1/2},0),\\
A_{a+1/2}^{-}&=\min(A_{a+1/2},0),\\
{\cal K}_{i,a+1/2}
&=\frac{\kappa_{i,a+1/2}}{\beta_{\rm sh}c}
\left(\frac{\partial q}{\partial s}\right)^2_{a+1/2},\\
{\cal H}_{i,a+1/2}
&=A_{a+1/2}^{+}U_{ia}^{n+1}
+A_{a+1/2}^{-}U_{i,a+1}^{n+1}\\
&\quad -{\cal K}_{i,a+1/2}
\frac{U_{i,a+1}^{n+1}-U_{ia}^{n+1}}{\Delta q}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The face flux is positive toward increasing \(q\), and the implicit cell equation
is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U_{ia}^{n+1}
&=U_{ia}^{n}+\frac{Q_i(R,\Delta R)}{\Delta q}\delta_{a1}\\
&\quad -\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta q}
\left({\cal H}_{i,a+1/2}-{\cal H}_{i,a-1/2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This tridiagonal solve conserves the downstream electron content except for the
explicit shock-front source and any supported outer escape boundary.  The direct
implicit branch applies this joint \(q\) solve before the energy solve; split
finite-width and characteristic diagnostic branches use the same conservative
remap and diffusion operator around the energy update.  Face tracing, metric
construction, and matrix-boundary forms are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  The default outer diffusive flux is closed;
the optional pulsar-wind research branch may use an absorbing outer boundary.

\subsection{Downstream energy-space update}

The energy-space update cools each downstream column after \(q\)-transport.  It
uses the cell average \(U_{ia}\) and the local cooling coefficient \(D_{ia}\)
from synchrotron, IC, SSA, and history photon fields in cell \(a\).  In the
default four-velocity branch, radiative cooling and adiabatic drift give the face
displacement
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_j&=R_j-R_{j-1},\\
J_{i+1/2}&=(dx_g/dy)_{i+1/2},\\
\bar D_{i+1/2,a}&=\frac{D_{ia}+D_{i+1,a}}{2},\\
\eta_{i+1/2,a}
&=\Delta R_j\bar D_{i+1/2,a}
+\ln\!\left(\frac{R_j}{R_{j-1}}\right),\\
{\cal F}_{i+1/2,a}
&=\frac{\eta_{i+1/2,a}}{J_{i+1/2}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(J_{i+1/2}\) is the grid Jacobian and \({\cal F}_{i+1/2,a}\) is the signed
energy-coordinate displacement.  The logarithmic term integrates
\(d\ln\gamma_e/dR=-1/R\).  The same conservative tridiagonal face-flux system as
the one-dimensional \texttt{fullhide} kernel is applied independently in each
column \(a\), producing the cooled \(U_{ia}\) used by radiation and observer
arrays.

The log-gamma branch uses \(x=\ln\gamma_e\).  In each downstream column,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\xi_{i+1/2,a}
&=D_{i+1/2,a}+\frac{1}{R},\\
\lambda_{i+1/2,a}
&=-\frac{\Delta R}{\Delta x}\xi_{i+1/2,a}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(D_{i+1/2,a}\) contains radiative cooling and \(1/R\) is the adiabatic
term.  The resulting one-sided implicit system is swept from high to low energy.
The non-negative projection \(\Pi_+(X)=\max(X,0)\) acts only on solved cell
averages and does not mix neighboring bins.  Backward-sweep coefficients are in
Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.

The characteristic energy branch follows cooling trajectories in
\(x=\ln\gamma_e\).  Each \(q\) column uses the one-dimensional piecewise cooling
map, with the source supplied only at the shock front:
\begin{equation}
S_{ia}^{x}=S_i^x\delta_{a1}.
\end{equation}
When affine synchrotron cooling is selected, the characteristic obeys
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\gamma_e}{dR}
&=-a_{{\rm syn},a}\gamma_e^2-\frac{\gamma_e}{R},\\
a_{{\rm syn},a}
&=\frac{\sigma_{\rm es} B_a'^2}{6\pi m_e c^2\beta_{\rm sh}\Gamma_{\rm sh}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first term is cell-local synchrotron cooling and the second is the global
adiabatic loss; \(\sigma_{\rm es}\) is the electron-scattering cross section.  If
the loss is not affine, the tabulated \(D_{ia}\) supplies the piecewise map.

The optional stochastic-transport branch changes the adiabatic term from a
global shell value to a local downstream value,
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{d\ln\gamma_e}{dR}\right|_{\rm ad}
=
\frac{1}{3\beta_f}
\left(\frac{2\beta_a}{r_a}
+\left.\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial r}\right|_{a}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(r_a\) and \(\beta_a\) come from the bridged downstream profile.  A
positive stochastic-acceleration normalization adds a zero-flux diffusion step in
\(x\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U_a}{\partial R}
=
\frac{\alpha_{\rm st}}{R}
\frac{\partial^2 U_a}{\partial x^2},
\end{equation}
applied half before and half after the cooling solve.  The implicit matrix and
edge conditions are given in Appendix~\ref{app:electron-solvers}.  This branch is
a research option for the \(\chi\)-resolved electron path, not a general
pulsar-wind-nebula or cosmic-ray propagation model.

\subsubsection{Returned \texorpdfstring{\(\chi\)}{chi}-resolved state}

The returned \(\chi\)-resolved state is the electron state used by observer
projection.  At each stored radius, the wrapper converts \(U_{ia}\) to
\(dN_e/d\gamma_e\) with the coordinate Jacobian and also sums over \(q\) to
recover the shell spectrum.  The stored cell arrays are \(r_a\), \(\Gamma_a\),
\(\Delta q\), \(B'_a\), unabsorbed synchrotron luminosity, local seed field, and
cell optical depth.

The scope is explicit.  A one-dimensional shell request recomputes shell
synchrotron from the summed spectrum, whereas direct \(\chi\)-resolved projection
keeps local luminosities and optical depths for foreground absorption.  SSC,
hadronic transport, pair-cascade feedback, and reverse-shock coupling remain
shell-level or one-dimensional; this wrapper does not make those channels
\(q\)-local.

Thermal electrons have a narrow solver boundary.  They are implemented only in
\texttt{fullhide\_1d} and \texttt{fullhide\_1d\_hz}; other electron solvers
reject thermal-electron transport.  Adaptive electron substeps are implemented
only for selected one-dimensional separated paths, where one full radial step is
compared with two half steps.  Joint electron-photon coupling uses fixed substeps
because the secondary source and cooling seed are iterated with the hadronic
stage.

\subsection{Photon Fields and Absorption} \label{sec:photon}

Photon fields are local comoving targets for cooling, SSC, absorption, and
hadronic interactions.  The electron solver supplies synchrotron emissivity and,
when enabled, SSC emissivity; the photon module stores these fields before any
observer projection mixes radii or angles.

\subsubsection{Comoving target photon state}

The comoving target state is the photon density read by the next local radiation
process.  From the transported electron spectrum and local emissivity, the module
returns a seed-frequency grid, synchrotron and optional SSC photon fields, a
hadronic target field, and the seed arrays used by self-absorption.  In a
separated electron-photon run,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n_{\nu'}^{\rm tar}
&=n_{\nu'}^{\rm syn}
+I_{\rm SSC}\,n_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC},\\
I_{\rm SSC}
&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{SSC target active},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(n_{\nu'}^{\rm syn}\) and \(n_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}\) are comoving photon number
densities per unit frequency, and \(I_{\rm SSC}\) selects whether forward SSC
photons enter the target field.  In the joint branch, \(p\gamma\),
Bethe--Heitler, and \(\gamma\gamma\) survival factors act on
\(n_{\nu'}^{\rm tar}\) before the next electron solve.  The stored target field is
therefore the local photon input to cooling and feedback, not an observer-frame
spectrum.

\subsubsection{Radiation-kernel boundary}

The radiation-kernel boundary evaluates local kernels on the stored photon state;
it is not a separate physical model.  Its public products are
\(L_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}\), the local pair-absorption factor
\(\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\gamma}\), and the external EBL factor
\(\mathcal{A}_{\rm EBL}\).  The equations below define the scattering kernel,
pair-production cross section, EBL interpolation, and photon normalization;
compiled binding names are ABI details listed in Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.

\subsubsection{Radiation interpolation reuse}

All photon quadratures use the same local interpolation between frequency nodes.
With \(X=\ln\nu'\) and \(Y=\ln n_{\nu'}\), positive endpoints are reconstructed
linearly in \((X,Y)\), i.e. as a local power law.  Cells with a non-positive
endpoint are reconstructed linearly in \(n_{\nu'}\).  Non-increasing frequency
intervals are outside the supported grid domain.

\subsubsection{Composite Simpson radiation quadrature}

Composite Simpson quadrature supplies the weights for uniform SSC integrals in
logarithmic variables, \(x=\ln\nu_s'\) for seed photons or \(x=\ln\gamma\) for
electrons.  On an odd uniform grid \(x_i=x_1+(i-1)\Delta x\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{x_1}^{x_N} f(x)\,dx
&\simeq
\frac{\Delta x}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_i^{\rm S} f_i,\\
w_i^{\rm S}
&=
\begin{cases}
1, & i=1 \ \text{or}\ i=N,\\
4, & i \ \text{even},\\
2, & i \ \text{odd and}\ 1<i<N .
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(f_i=f(x_i)\), and \(w_i^{\rm S}\) is only a quadrature weight; it does not
modify the electron spectrum or seed photon density.  Grids outside this Simpson
layout are outside the uniform-grid SSC branch.

\subsection{Uniform-grid SSC emissivity}

\subsubsection{Jones--Klein--Nishina variables}

Forward SSC emission is local electron scattering of the local synchrotron
photon field.  The uniform-grid branch uses the same logarithmic electron and
seed-frequency grids.  For emitted frequency \(\nu'\) and seed frequency
\(\nu_s'\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon&=\frac{h\nu'}{m_ec^2},&
\epsilon_s&=\frac{h\nu_s'}{m_ec^2},\\
b&=4\gamma\epsilon_s,&
q&=\frac{\epsilon}{b(\gamma-\epsilon)},\\
\eta&=bq=\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma-\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\epsilon\) and \(\epsilon_s\) are photon energies in units of \(m_ec^2\),
and \(q,\eta\) locate the event in the Klein--Nishina kernel.  For
\(\nu_s'<\nu'\), the allowed electron energies satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma&>\gamma_{\rm IC},\\
\gamma_{\rm IC}
&\equiv
\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon+
\left(\epsilon^2+\frac{\nu'}{\nu_s'}\right)^{1/2}\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first line is the kinematic lower limit on \(\gamma\).  The
Jones--Klein--Nishina factor for the low-seed-frequency integral is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G(q,\eta)
&=2q\ln q+(1+2q)(1-q)\\
&\quad+\frac{\eta^2(1-q)}{2(1+\eta)} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ssc-kernel}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Uniform-grid Simpson integration}

The uniform SSC luminosity splits the seed-frequency integral at \(\nu_s'=\nu'\).
For \(\nu_s'<\nu'\), with \(n_s\equiv n_{\nu_s'}^{\rm tar}\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\rm IC}(\nu')&=\frac{3}{4}ch\sigma_{\rm es}\nu',\\
L_{\nu'}^{{\rm SSC},<}
&=C_{\rm IC}
\int_{\nu_s'<\nu'} d\ln\nu_s'\,n_s
\int d\ln\gamma\,\frac{N_e(\gamma)}{\gamma}
G(q,\eta).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\sigma_{\rm es}\) is the electron-scattering cross section and
\(N_e(\gamma)\) is the transported electron spectrum.  For
\(\nu_s'\ge\nu'\), high-energy electron tail moments evaluate the elastic
scattering part,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\rm es}(\nu_s')
&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\nu_s'}{\nu'}\right)^{1/2},\\
\Phi_{\rm es}(\gamma,\nu_s')
&=\frac{\nu'}{\nu_s'}-\frac{1}{4\gamma^2},\\
L_{\nu'}^{{\rm SSC},>}
&=C_{\rm IC}
\int_{\nu_s'\ge\nu'}d\ln\nu_s'\,n_s
\int_{\gamma>\gamma_{\rm es}}d\ln\gamma\,
\frac{N_e(\gamma)}{\gamma}
\Phi_{\rm es}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both logarithmic integrals use the Simpson weights above.  Their sum gives the
local SSC luminosity and the photon density stored for later local processes,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}
&=L_{\nu'}^{{\rm SSC},<}+L_{\nu'}^{{\rm SSC},>},\\
n_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}
&=\frac{L_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}}{4\pi R^2ch\nu'}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The module returns the emitted SSC spectrum and comoving SSC photon density; SSC
cooling of electrons remains part of the transport step.

\subsection{Nonuniform-grid SSC emissivity}

The nonuniform-grid SSC branch uses the same scattering kernel on unequal
\(\ln\gamma\) cells.  With \(x=\ln\gamma\) and \(N_x=dN_e/dx\), cell \(i\)
reconstructs
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_x(x)&\simeq N_{x,i}+s_i(x-x_i),\\
x_i&\equiv \frac{x_{i-1/2}+x_{i+1/2}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(s_i\) is the local minmod-limited slope.  This reconstruction samples the
SSC quadrature and does not modify the transported electron state.

The nonuniform branch changes quadrature support, not Compton physics.  For
\(\nu_s'<\nu'\), each electron cell above the kinematic limit contributes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_i^{<}(\nu',\nu_s')
&=\int_{x_{\rm L}}^{x_{i+1/2}}
N_x(x)\,\frac{G[q(x),\eta(x)]}{\gamma(x)^2}\,dx,\\
x_{\rm L}&=\max[x_{i-1/2},\ln\gamma_{\rm IC}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The seed-frequency and cell integrals use the fixed Gauss rule in
Appendix~\ref{app:photon-kernels}.  For \(\nu_s'\ge\nu'\), the branch uses
precomputed high-energy moments,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_a(>x_{\rm L})&=\int_{x_{\rm L}}^\infty N_x(x)\gamma(x)^{-a}\,dx,\\
I^{>}(\nu',\nu_s')
&=\frac{\nu'}{\nu_s'}M_2(>x_{\rm L})-\frac{1}{4}M_4(>x_{\rm L}) .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The returned products remain \(L_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}\) and
\(n_{\nu'}^{\rm SSC}\).  The branch requires strictly increasing electron cell
edges and the same target field \(n_{\nu'}^{\rm tar}\); it does not define a
different SSC model \citep{Jones1968,BlumenthalGould1970}.

\subsection{Finite-cell photon transfer}

\subsubsection{Shell transfer factor}

Finite-cell photon transfer converts a local optical depth into escaping
luminosity.  Because a photon born inside an absorbing cell samples less material
than a photon entering from outside, \asgard\ uses the cell-average factor
\(\psi(\tau)\) from Eq.~(\ref{eq:finite-cell-transfer}), with \(\psi(0)=1\).  For
synchrotron self-absorption,
\[
\tau_\nu^{\rm loc}=\alpha_\nu'\Delta r',
\]
where \(\alpha_\nu'\) is the comoving absorption coefficient and \(\Delta r'\) is
the comoving shell width.  For local \(\gamma\gamma\) absorption,
\[
\tau_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm loc}(\nu')
=\Delta r'\int \sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(\nu',\tilde\nu')
n_{\tilde\nu'}^{\rm tar}\,d\tilde\nu' .
\]
The transfer step changes only escaping luminosity and stored photon density; it
does not change the photon-production kernel or transported electron spectrum.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\gamma\gamma\)}{gamma-gamma} shell absorption}

\subsubsection{Soft-photon cell measure}

Pair absorption removes a high-energy photon through the local target photon
field.  For a photon of comoving frequency \(\nu_1'\) and a target-frequency cell
\((\nu_j',\nu_{j+1}')\), the kernel first defines
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_1&=\frac{h\nu_1'}{m_ec^2},\\
\nu_{2,j}'&=(\nu_j'\nu_{j+1}')^{1/2},\\
\epsilon_{2,j}&=\frac{h\nu_{2,j}'}{m_ec^2},\\
\Delta\nu_j'&=\nu_{2,j}'\Delta\ln\nu' .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\epsilon_1\) and \(\epsilon_{2,j}\) are photon energies in units of
\(m_ec^2\), \(\Delta\nu_j'\) is the logarithmic cell measure, and the sampled
target density is \(n_{\nu_{2,j}'}^{\rm tar}\).

\subsubsection{Isotropic angular moment}

For the local comoving isotropic target, define
\(x_j\equiv\epsilon_1\epsilon_{2,j}\) and
\(z\equiv(1-\mu)/2\).  The angular average, including the photon relative-speed
weight, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K(x)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\mu)
\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}\!\left[x\frac{1-\mu}{2}\right]d\mu\\
&=2\int_{1/x}^{1}z\,\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(xz)\,dz,
\qquad x>1,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with \(K(x)=0\) for \(x\le1\).  Thus the physical pair threshold fixes the
integration support; no upper cutoff in \(s=xz\) is introduced.

The Breit--Wheeler cross section is evaluated with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_s&=s^{-1},\qquad \beta_s=(1-u_s)^{1/2},\\
L_s&=\ln\frac{1+\beta_s}{1-\beta_s}
=\ln s+2\ln(1+\beta_s),\\
\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(s)&=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3\sigma_{\rm es}}{16}u_s
\left[(3-\beta_s^4)L_s-2\beta_s(2-\beta_s^2)\right], & s>1,\\
0, & s\le1 .
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Near threshold the implementation forms \(\beta_s^2=(s-1)u_s\) and evaluates
the logarithms through equivalent \(\operatorname{atanh}\) identities.  At large
\(s\), the \(u_s\) and \(L_s\) forms avoid subtracting nearly equal quantities.
For each \(x>1\), the change of variable
\(s=1+(x-1)t^2\) gives
\begin{equation}
K(x)=\frac{4(x-1)}{x^2}\int_0^1
t\,s(t)\,\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}[s(t)]\,dt .
\end{equation}
The implementation evaluates this smooth integral with a 16-point
Gauss--Legendre rule on \(0<t<1\).

\subsubsection{Shell optical-depth assembly}

The angular moment is independent of shell radius and is reused when the target
field is assembled.  For a shell at \(R_i,\Gamma_i\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta'_{\gamma\gamma,i}&=\frac{R_i}{12\Gamma_i},\\
n_j&=n_{\nu_{2,j}'}^{\rm tar}(R_i),\\
\tau_{\gamma\gamma}(\nu_1',R_i)
&=\Delta'_{\gamma\gamma,i}
\sum_j K(x_j)n_j\Delta\nu_j'
+\tau_{\rm ex}(\nu_1',R_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There is no additional factor of \(1/2\): the isotropic angular normalization is
already contained in \(K\).  The path length is only the current \(k=0\)
homogeneous-shell approximation.  Indeed,
\(N_{\rm sw}=4\pi nR^3/3=(4\pi R^2\Delta')(4\Gamma n)\) gives
\(\Delta'=R/(12\Gamma)\).  This homogeneous-shell estimate uses
\(n_2'=4\Gamma n\); it is neither an exact compression law at arbitrary
\(\Gamma\) nor a general Blandford--McKee shell width.  Transrelativistic
compression and path lengths, a \(k=2\) wind, tabulated density jumps, prompt
internal shocks, and cascade zones remain registered boundaries of the current
implementation.  The term \(\tau_{\rm ex}\)
adds optical depth from another local module.  The finite-cell survival factor is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\gamma}(\nu_1',R_i)
&=\psi[\tau_{\gamma\gamma}(\nu_1',R_i)] .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This factor attenuates photons emitted throughout the absorbing cell, not a
foreground screen \(e^{-\tau}\).  It is the local \(\gamma\gamma\) attenuation
used by the photon assembly \citep{GouldSchreder1967,Svensson1987}.

\subsection{Head-on path-opacity helper}

The head-on helper supplies a path optical depth for photon-history transport,
not the shell-emission attenuation above.  With
\(s_j^{\rm ho}\equiv\epsilon_1\epsilon_{2,j}\) and comoving path length
\(\Delta x'\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm ho}(\nu_1')=
\Delta x'
\sum_j \sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(s_j^{\rm ho})
n_{\nu_{2,j}'}^{\rm tar}\Delta\nu_j' .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is a ray-segment opacity and does not replace the angle-averaged finite-cell
survival calculation for shell emission.

\subsection{External EBL attenuation}

\subsubsection{Table boundary}

External EBL attenuation acts after photons leave the blast-wave shells.  It is
therefore not part of the shell photon density, electron cooling, or local
\(\gamma\gamma\) feedback.  The default Domínguez et al. table
\citep{Dominguez2011EBL} stores optical depth by photon energy \(E_i\) and source
redshift \(z_j\); the corresponding observed frequencies are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_i=\frac{E_i}{h}, \qquad
\tau_{\rm EBL}(\nu_i,0)=0 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second relation is the zero-distance boundary for this external table.

\subsubsection{Redshift and frequency interpolation}

For \(z_j\le z\le z_{j+1}\), the redshift interpolation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_z&=\frac{z-z_j}{z_{j+1}-z_j},\\
\tau_i(z)&=(1-\lambda_z)\tau_{ij}
+\lambda_z\tau_{i,j+1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_{ij}\) is the tabulated value at \((\nu_i,z_j)\).  The observed
frequency interpolation for \(\nu_i\le\nu_{\rm obs}\le\nu_{i+1}\) is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_\nu&=\frac{\nu_{\rm obs}-\nu_i}{\nu_{i+1}-\nu_i},\\
\tau_{\rm EBL}(\nu_{\rm obs},z)=
(1-\lambda_\nu)\tau_i(z)+\lambda_\nu\tau_{i+1}(z).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Below the lowest tabulated gamma-ray energy, the returned optical depth is zero.
Negative redshift, redshift above the table, or frequency above the table is
outside the selected EBL model.

\subsubsection{External attenuation factor}

The EBL output is the observer-side multiplicative factor
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}_{\rm EBL}(\nu_{\rm obs},z)
=\exp[-\tau_{\rm EBL}(\nu_{\rm obs},z)].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
It multiplies projected flux after local transfer and does not change any local
particle or photon state.

\subsection{Observer-side photon component assembly}

\subsubsection{Component ordering}

Observer-side photon assembly accounts for components after local radiation
modules finish.  Forward synchrotron is present in standard forward-shock runs;
SSC, reverse-shock, cross-zone inverse-Compton, hadronic, Bethe--Heitler, and
pair-production components enter only when their upstream branch supplies a
luminosity.  Thus
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
{\cal C}_{\rm act}\subset
\{&{\rm FS\,syn},{\rm FS\,SSC},{\rm FS\,had},{\rm FS\,BH},
{\rm FS\,HIC},{\rm FS\,pair},\\
&{\rm RS\,syn},{\rm RS\,had},{\rm RS\,SSC},{\rm cross\,IC}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Absent components are not reconstructed during observer assembly.

\subsubsection{Distance and attenuation prefactor}

The distance and attenuation prefactor converts each active local luminosity into
a radial flux grid before equal-arrival-time projection.  For component
\(c\in{\cal C}_{\rm act}\), let \(\widetilde L_{\nu'}^c(R)\) be the local
spectral luminosity after that component's shell transfer.  The assembly uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{P}_{\rm loc}(\nu,R)&=
\frac{1+z}{4\pi d_L^2}\,
\mathcal{A}_{\rm loc}(\nu,R),\\
\Delta F_\nu^c(R)&=
\mathcal{P}_{\rm loc}(\nu,R)\,\widetilde L_{\nu'}^c(R),\\
F_\nu^{\rm rad}(R)&=
\sum_{c\in{\cal C}_{\rm act}}\Delta F_\nu^c(R).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(d_L\) is the luminosity distance and \(\mathcal{A}_{\rm loc}\) is the
local photon-survival factor returned by the pair-absorption kernel, including
active extra optical depths such as Bethe--Heitler photon loss and pair-cascade
opacity.  The EBL table factor is external to this local prefactor.  The
projection module then maps each \(\Delta F_\nu^c(R)\) and the summed
\(F_\nu^{\rm rad}(R)\) onto observer time.

\section{Reverse Shocks and Density-Jump Secondary Shocks} \label{sec:reverse-shock}

\subsection{Primary ejecta state and activation}

\subsubsection{Physical role}

The primary reverse shock heats the original ejecta shell.  It is distinct from
the forward shock, which heats the external medium.  This branch therefore needs
a finite shell width, a region-4 upstream density, and a rule for deciding when a
magnetized upstream flow can form a shock.  Its outputs are the region-3 mass,
thermal energy, volume, magnetic field, and electron-injection scale.

\subsubsection{Activation boundary}

The reverse-shock branch is active only when the input model supplies an engine
duration.  That duration fixes the initial shell width, so without it the code has
no upstream region-4 density for the reverse shock.  We write the initial ejecta
Lorentz factor as \(\Gamma_0\); the runtime field \(\eta_0\) stores the same
quantity.

\subsubsection{Ejecta shell state}

The ejecta shell state gives the baryon density and inertia seen by the reverse
shock.  For an engine duration \(\Delta t_{\rm eng}\), \asgard\ uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_0&\equiv c\Delta t_{\rm eng},\\
M_{\rm ej}&\equiv \frac{E_{\rm iso}}{(1+\sigma)\Gamma_0c^2},\\
\Delta(R)&\equiv \max\left(\Delta_0,\frac{R}{\Gamma_0^2}\right),\\
n_4(R)&\equiv \frac{M_{\rm ej}}
{4\pi m_pR^2\Gamma_0\Delta(R)} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\Delta_0\) is the initial shell width, \(\Delta(R)\) includes radial
spreading, \(M_{\rm ej}\) is the baryonic ejecta mass, and \(n_4\) is the
upstream baryon density in region 4.  The total ejecta energy is \(E_{\rm iso}\).
When \(\sigma>0\), only the fraction \((1+\sigma)^{-1}\) is carried by baryonic
rest mass.  The unshocked shell still has matter plus ordered-field inertia, so
\asgard\ uses \(n_4\) for shock heating and electron injection, but uses
\(\tilde n_4\equiv(1+\sigma)n_4\) when the unprocessed magnetized ejecta enters
the inertia budget.

\subsubsection{Shock-front relative Lorentz factor}

The reverse-shock electron source is set by the local relative Lorentz factor at
the shock front.  If \(\Gamma_2\) is the shocked-shell Lorentz factor and
\(\Gamma_0\) is the unshocked ejecta Lorentz factor, then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_2&=(\Gamma_2^2-1)^{1/2},\\
u_0&=(\Gamma_0^2-1)^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{34}&=\Gamma_2\Gamma_0-u_2u_0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(u_2\) and \(u_0\) are the four-speeds of the shocked shell and unshocked
ejecta.  The output \(\gamma_{34}(R)\) sets the reverse-shock injection energy
scale for region-3 electrons.

\subsubsection{Magnetized activation criteria}

Magnetized ejecta form a reverse shock only after two local conditions are true.
The region-2 pressure must be high enough to push against the upstream ordered
field, and the upstream flow must be super-fast-magnetosonic in the
reverse-shock frame.  The pressure condition is written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_2&\equiv
\frac{(4\Gamma_2+3)(\Gamma_2-1)n_1m_pc^2}{3},\\
\sigma_{\rm cr}&\equiv \frac{2P_2}{n_4m_pc^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(P_2\) is the region-2 pressure estimate and \(n_1\) is the external
upstream density.  The active condition is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm cr}\ge\sigma,\qquad u_{4s}^2>\sigma ,
\end{equation}
where \(u_{4s}\) is the region-4 upstream four-speed in the reverse-shock frame.
The first inequality is a pressure criterion, while the second is the
fast-magnetosonic criterion.  For \(\sigma\le0\), these magnetized activation
checks do not delay the hydrodynamic reverse shock.

\subsubsection{Waiting-state inertia}

Before the activation checks pass, magnetized ejecta can add inertia but not
reverse-shock heat.  Thus \(dM_3/dR=0\), no reverse-shock electrons are injected,
and \(B'_3=0\), while the contact still feels the causally connected unshocked
shell,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_{\rm w}&\equiv
M_{\rm ej}(1+\sigma)\frac{\Gamma_0}{\Gamma_0-1}f_{\rm f},\\
\beta_{\rm f}&\equiv \left(\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{1/2},\\
f_{\rm f}&\equiv \min\left[
1,\,
\frac{R\beta_{\rm f}}
{\Gamma_0^2(1-\beta_4\beta_{\rm f})\beta_4\Delta(R)}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\beta_{\rm f}\) is the fast-magnetosonic speed, \(f_{\rm f}\) is the
connected shell fraction, and \(\beta_4\) is the region-4 speed.  The
waiting-stage Lorentz factor obeys
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Gamma_2}{dR}
=-\frac{u_2^2\,dM_2/dR}
{M_{\rm w}+[\epsilon_2+2(1-\epsilon_2)\Gamma_2]M_2}.
\end{equation}
This waiting branch contains no reverse-shock mass or heat source.  When both
activation conditions first hold inside a radial step, \asgard\ splits the step
and starts the active reverse shock at that event.  The setup follows
thin-shell/reverse-shock treatments and Zhang--Kobayashi magnetized-ejecta
notation, with \vegas\ used only as a jump-condition comparison backend
\citep{SariPiran1999,Kobayashi2000,ZhangKobayashi2005,PeerWijers2006,Giannios2008,Wang2025VegasAfterglow}.

\subsection{Finite-strength MHD jump solver}

\subsubsection{Cold upstream variables}

The MHD jump solver returns the downstream speed, compression, and heat for a
cold transverse-field upstream.  With \(g\equiv\gamma_{34}\) and
\(\hat{\gamma}\equiv4/3+1/(3g)\), the \(\sigma>\sigma_{\rm cut}\) downstream
four-speed is found from \(y=u_d^2\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_3y^3+a_2y^2+a_1y+a_0&=0,\\
u_d&=y^{1/2},\\
u_u&=\left[(1+u_d^2)(g^2-1)\right]^{1/2}+u_dg,\\
r_\sigma&=\frac{u_u}{u_d}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite positive root is used; the fixed coefficients
\(a_i(g,\hat{\gamma},\sigma)\) and depressed-cubic variables are listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:reverse-shock-technical}.  Here \(u_u\) is the shock-frame
upstream four-speed, and \(r_\sigma\) compresses the ordered field and sets the
active primary reverse-shock mass flux.  The threshold \(\sigma_{\rm cut}\)
selects the algebraic branch only; it is not a physical magnetization boundary.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Low-\(\sigma\)}{Low-sigma} hydrodynamic branch}

The low-\(\sigma\) branch uses the finite-strength hydrodynamic limit.  For
\(\sigma\le\sigma_{\rm cut}\), \asgard\ uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y_{\rm h}&=
\frac{(g-1)(\hat{\gamma}-1)^2}
{-\hat{\gamma}(\hat{\gamma}-2)(g-1)+2},\\
u_d&=y_{\rm h}^{1/2},\\
r_0&=4g .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The heat helper returns \(g-1\) only for exactly unmagnetized ejecta; for
\(0<\sigma\le\sigma_{\rm cut}\) it still evaluates the finite-\(\sigma\) enthalpy
and approaches \(g-1\) continuously.  The active dynamics do not use the
ultra-relativistic \(4g+3\) compression approximation.  This MHD jump applies only
to the primary ejecta reverse shock; density-jump secondary branches remain
hydrodynamic diagnostics without ordered-field compression or magnetic enthalpy
feedback.

\subsection{Comoving region-3 field and heat state}

\subsubsection{Comoving field components}

All magnetic fields in this subsection are comoving.  The unshocked ejecta carries
the ordered region-4 field
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho_4&\equiv m_pn_4,\\
B'_4&\equiv (4\pi\sigma\rho_4c^2)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which vanishes for \(\sigma=0\).  During active pre-crossing, the region-3 field
combines a turbulent heat-generated part and the compressed ordered component,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_{\rm t}^{\prime 2}&\equiv 8\pi\epsilon_{B,r}\frac{U_3}{V_3},\\
B'_{\rm o}&=r_\sigma B'_4 \qquad \hbox{before crossing},\\
B'_3&=\left(B_{\rm t}^{\prime 2}+B_{\rm o}^{\prime 2}\right)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(U_3\) and \(V_3\) are the region-3 internal energy and comoving volume.
The total \(B'_3\) sets reverse-shock synchrotron cooling, whereas only
\(B'_{\rm o}\) enters the magnetic enthalpy in the bulk dynamics.  In the waiting
state \(B'_3=0\).

\subsubsection{Post-crossing ordered-field advection}

The post-crossing ordered field is carried after the reverse shock has crossed
the ejecta shell.  The stored crossing value evolves with the region-3 volume and
radius as
\begin{equation}
B'_{\rm o}(R)=B'_{{\rm o},\times}
\frac{V_{3,\times}}{V_3(R)}
\frac{R}{R_\times}.
\end{equation}
Here \(R_\times\), \(V_{3,\times}\), and \(B'_{{\rm o},\times}\) are the radius,
volume, and ordered field stored at shell crossing.  The crossing field is an
event-register state.  It is not fitted after the light curve is computed.

\subsubsection{Magnetic enthalpy inertia}

The ordered component adds magnetic enthalpy inertia to the shell dynamics:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p_B&\equiv \frac{B_{\rm o}^{\prime 2}}{8\pi},\\
E_B&\equiv p_BV_3 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\[
M_B=\frac{E_B+p_BV_3}{c^2}+\frac{p_BV_3}{\Gamma_2^2c^2}.
\]
The first term is magnetic enthalpy; the second is the finite-\(\Gamma_2\)
pressure contribution retained by the shell equation.

\subsubsection{Heat state from the jump}

The jump heat is stored as the dimensionless region-3 thermal state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\zeta_3&\equiv\frac{U_3}{M_3c^2},\\
\gamma_u&\equiv(1+u_u^2)^{1/2},\\
\gamma_d&\equiv(1+u_d^2)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(M_3\) is the shocked ejecta mass.  During active pre-crossing,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\zeta_3&=\frac{h_d-1}{\hat{\gamma}},\\
h_d&\equiv (1+\sigma)\frac{\gamma_u}{\gamma_d}-r_\sigma\sigma .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\zeta_3\), not the unmagnetized estimate \(\gamma_{34}-1\), sets the heat
when magnetization is active.  The Lorentz equation uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_3=
\begin{cases}
1, & \sigma=0,\\
\zeta_3/(\gamma_{34}-1), & \sigma>0,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The MHD jump therefore enters through \(M_B\) and through the heat--bulk coupling
\(\mathcal R_3\).  After shell crossing, the code uses the evolved ratio
\(U_3/(M_3c^2)\) and sets the pre-crossing response term to zero.

\subsection{Primary reverse-shock ODE}

\subsubsection{State vector and normalization}

The primary reverse-shock ODE evolves one contact discontinuity shared by the
forward-shocked medium and the reverse-shocked ejecta.  The state vector stores
the bulk motion and the region-3 thermodynamic state,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y_{\rm R}&\equiv
\left(\Gamma_2,R,M_2,\frac{M_3}{M_{\rm ej}},
\frac{U_3}{M_{\rm ej}c^2},\frac{V_3}{V_0},\ldots\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the ellipsis denotes density-jump secondary-shock registers.  The forward
swept mass and volume scale are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dM_2}{dR}&=4\pi R^2m_pn_1,\\
V_0&\equiv \frac{M_{\rm ej}}{n_{3,0}m_p}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(n_1\) is the external density.  The integrator stores physical rates for
\(\Gamma_2,R,M_2\) and normalized rates for \(M_3,U_3,V_3\) using
\(M_{\rm ej}\), \(M_{\rm ej}c^2\), and \(V_0\); exported arrays undo this
normalization.

\subsubsection{Pre-crossing mass flux and shock speed}

Before shell crossing, the reverse shock sweeps baryons from \(n_4\) into region
3:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dM_3}{dt}&=
4\pi R^2m_p\,\Gamma_0 n_4\,\Delta\beta_{\rm rs}
\frac{dR}{dt},\\
\frac{dR}{dt}&=\frac{\beta_2c}{1-\beta_2},\\
\Delta\beta_{\rm rs}
&=\frac{u_u}{\Gamma_0(\Gamma_0\gamma_u-u_0u_u)},\\
\beta_{\rm rs}&=\beta_4-\Delta\beta_{\rm rs}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(u_0=(\Gamma_0^2-1)^{1/2}\), while \(u_u\) and \(\gamma_u\) are supplied by
the MHD jump solution.  This source is zero in the waiting state and after shell
crossing.

\subsubsection{Active pre-crossing Lorentz equation}

The active pre-crossing Lorentz equation balances forward swept mass, reverse
shock loading, thermal inertia, magnetic inertia, and active secondary-shock
inertia.  In this branch,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{\rm pre}
&\equiv(\Gamma_2^2-1)n_1+\mathcal B_{\rm pre},\\
\mathcal B_{\rm pre}
&\equiv(\Gamma_2\gamma_{34}-\Gamma_0)\Delta\beta_{\rm rs}
\Gamma_0\tilde n_4,\\
\frac{d\Gamma_2}{dR}
=-\frac{4\pi R^2m_p\mathcal A_{\rm pre}}{\mathcal I_{\rm pre}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\tilde n_4=(1+\sigma)n_4\) is the inertial density of unprocessed
magnetized ejecta.  The first term in \(\mathcal A_{\rm pre}\) is forward-shock
deceleration, while \(\mathcal B_{\rm pre}\) is reverse-shock loading.  The
inertia denominator is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal I_{\rm pre}={}&
[\epsilon_2+2(1-\epsilon_2)\Gamma_2]M_2
+M_3+M_B+M_s \\
& +(1-\epsilon_3)\zeta_3M_3
+(1-\epsilon_3)\Gamma_2\mathcal R_3M_3\mathcal G_{34}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here \(M_s\) is the effective inertia of active hydrodynamic secondary
reverse-shock branches and is zero otherwise.  The last factor is
\(\partial\gamma_{34}/\partial\Gamma_2\),
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{34}=
\Gamma_0(1-\beta_4\beta_2)
-\frac{\Gamma_0\beta_4}{\Gamma_2^2\beta_2}.
\end{equation}
This equation is used only during active pre-crossing; waiting and post-crossing
states use the separate branches above and below.

\subsubsection{Reverse radiative fraction}

The reverse radiative fraction controls how much shocked-ejecta heat remains in
the inertia term.  With the same cooling diagnostic as the forward dynamics,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{c,3}&=\frac{6\pi m_ec}
{\sigma_{\rm es} B_3^{\prime 2}\Gamma_2t},\\
\gamma_{m,3}&=1+
\frac{\epsilon_{e,r}}{f_{e,r}}
\frac{p_r-2}{p_r-1}
\frac{m_p}{m_e}\zeta_3,\\
\epsilon_3&=\epsilon_{e,r}\min\left[
1,\left(\frac{\gamma_{m,3}}{\gamma_{c,3}}\right)^{p_r-2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(t\) is the source-frame time used by the dynamics, \(B'_3\) is the total
region-3 field returned by the jump and heat state, and \(p_r\), \(f_{e,r}\), and
\(\epsilon_{e,r}\) are the reverse-shock electron parameters.

\subsubsection{Thermal and volume update}

The thermal and volume updates add newly shocked material and expand the existing
region 3:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dU_3}{dt}&=\zeta_3c^2\frac{dM_3}{dt}
-(\hat{\gamma}_{3}-1)\frac{U_3}{V_3}q_{V,3},\\
\gamma_{{\rm th},3}&=1+\zeta_3,\\
\hat{\gamma}_{3}&=
\frac{4\gamma_{{\rm th},3}+1}{3\gamma_{{\rm th},3}},\\
\frac{dV_3}{dt}&=\frac{1}{r_\sigma n_4m_p}\frac{dM_3}{dt}
+q_{V,3},\\
q_{V,3}&\equiv V_3\left(\frac{3}{R}\frac{dR}{dt}
-\frac{1}{\Gamma_2}\frac{d\Gamma_2}{dt}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In the waiting state both source terms are zero; after crossing, only the
expansion term remains.

\subsubsection{Post-crossing bulk update}

The post-crossing bulk update removes the active reverse-shock source.  After
crossing, \(dM_3/dt=0\), no new reverse-shock heat is added, and the stored
\(U_3/V_3\) thermal state controls post-crossing electron cooling.  The bulk
Lorentz factor follows
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\Gamma_2}{dR}
=-\frac{u_2^2\,dM_2/dR}
{[\epsilon_2+2(1-\epsilon_2)\Gamma_2]M_2+M_3+M_B+M_s}.
\end{equation}
This equation keeps the swept forward mass, shocked ejecta mass, magnetic
inertia, and active secondary-shock inertia in the denominator.  It does not add
new region-3 mass.

\subsection{Crossing and activation event splitting}

\subsubsection{Physical events}

The reverse-shock integrator stops at branch changes.  Magnetized ejecta may
first wait, then activate when the pressure and fast-mode criteria hold, and
finally enter the post-crossing branch at \(M_3=M_{\rm ej}\).  The split prevents
one Runge--Kutta step from mixing waiting, active pre-crossing, and post-crossing
source terms.

\subsubsection{Mass-fraction independent variable}

During active pre-crossing, \asgard\ uses \(q\equiv M_3/M_{\rm ej}\) as the step
variable, so shell crossing is the fixed endpoint \(q=1\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dY}{dq}
&=\frac{dY/dt}{d(M_3/M_{\rm ej})/dt},\\
\frac{dt}{dq}
&=\left[\frac{d(M_3/M_{\rm ej})}{dt}\right]^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(Y\) is the reverse-shock state and \(t\) is source-frame time.  The
post-crossing branch starts from the \(q=1\) state and receives no new shocked
ejecta mass.

\subsubsection{Log-time single-branch stages}

Away from an event, a single-branch interval uses \(s=\ln(t/t_0)\):
\begin{equation}
\frac{dY}{ds}=t\frac{dY}{dt}.
\end{equation}
where \(t_0\) is the stage start time.  This form applies to waiting, active, and
post-crossing intervals only when no event lies inside the step.

\subsubsection{Event-state register}

The event-state register is algorithmic, not a new blast-wave variable.  It
selects waiting, active pre-crossing, or post-crossing equations, and carries
\(B'_3\) and the frozen crossing vector \(\mathbf C_\times\).  It is not fitted
or projected as an observer component.

\subsubsection{Crossing-state write}

The crossing-state vector is empty before shell crossing.  At \(q=1\), the first
post-crossing right-hand-side evaluation writes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf C_\times={}&(t_\times,R_\times,e_{3,\times},\Gamma_{2,\times},
U_{3,\times},V_{3,\times},\\
&M_{3,\times},\gamma_{m,\times},B'_{{\rm o},\times})\\
={}&\left(t,R,\frac{U_3}{V_3},\Gamma_2,U_3,V_3,
M_3,\gamma_{m,3},B'_{\rm o}\right),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(B'_{{\rm o},\times}\) is the ordered field at crossing.  Later
post-crossing steps read this vector without rewriting it.

\subsubsection{Crossing and activation split}

If a pre-crossing log-time interval reaches \(q=1\), \asgard\ locates the event,
evolves to it with active equations, writes \(\mathbf C_\times\) once, and evolves
the remainder with post-crossing equations.  The waiting-to-active split uses the
same event-location logic before any active reverse-shock source term is applied.

\subsubsection{Single-branch refinement}

The RK refinement check is applied only within one branch:
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rm RK,RS}=\max_a\frac{2|a_N-a_{2N}|}{a_N+a_{2N}},
\end{equation}
with \(a\in\{\Gamma_2,R,M_2\}\) for log-time steps and
\(a\in\{\Gamma_2,R,M_2,t\}\) for the \(q\)-step.  Event points are excluded
because the equations change there.

\subsection{Reverse electron injection contract}

The reverse-electron source is a radial newly shocked-ejecta source, not a
source-frame time rate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_R^{\rm r}(R)&\equiv
f_{e,r}\frac{1}{m_p}\frac{dM_3}{dR},\\
\gamma_m^{\rm r}(R)&=1+
\frac{\epsilon_{e,r}}{f_{e,r}}
\frac{p_r-2}{p_r-1}
\frac{m_p}{m_e}\left[\gamma_{34}(R)-1\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(Q_R^{\rm r}\) is the radial injection rate and \(f_{e,r}\) is the
nonthermal electron fraction.  The stored heat \(\zeta_3=U_3/(M_3c^2)\) controls
thermal evolution, while the transported source uses the shock-front
\(\gamma_{34}\) scale.

On the natural-log grid \(x=\ln\gamma_e\), the reverse source uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_{\max}^{\rm r}(\gamma_e)&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \gamma_e\le\gamma_{\max}^{\rm r},\\
\exp(1-\gamma_e/\gamma_{\max}^{\rm r}),&
\gamma_e>\gamma_{\max}^{\rm r},
\end{cases}\\
Q_x^{\rm r}(x;R)&=
\begin{cases}
A_r(R)\,\gamma_e(\gamma_e-1)^{-p_r}
\mathcal C_{\max}^{\rm r}(\gamma_e),
& \gamma_e>\gamma_m^{\rm r},\\
0, & \gamma_e\le\gamma_m^{\rm r}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The normalization \(A_r\) is fixed by
\(\int Q_x^{\rm r}\,dx=Q_R^{\rm r}\).  This form keeps the kinetic factor
\(\gamma_e-1\) at mildly relativistic energies and includes the Jacobian
\(d\gamma_e/dx=\gamma_e\).  If a log-four-velocity transport coordinate is
selected internally, the source is transformed with the corresponding
\(dx/dy\).  Reverse electron transport may use either \texttt{fullhide\_1d} or
\texttt{dg\_1d}; both paths use the same radial source.  The reverse
synchrotron luminosity is computed with the same selected synchrotron kernel as
the forward shock.  Optional reverse SSC and cross-zone IC are added only during
observer assembly.  Reverse hadronic full-chain flags reuse the formal
one-dimensional hadronic kernels with the reverse magnetic field, reverse seed
photons, reverse shell energy, and reverse baryon target density.

\subsection{Reverse radius-output sweep}

The reverse radius-output sweep inserts the electron source on each saved radial
interval.  For \([R_{i-1},R_i]\), \asgard\ evaluates the local shock state at the
midpoint,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar\Gamma_{2,i}&=\frac{\Gamma_{2,i}+\Gamma_{2,i-1}}{2},\\
\bar B'_{3,i}&=\frac{B'_{3,i}+B'_{3,i-1}}{2},\\
\bar u_{2,i}&=(\bar\Gamma_{2,i}^2-1)^{1/2},\\
u_0&=(\Gamma_0^2-1)^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{34,i}
&=\frac{\bar\Gamma_{2,i}^2+\Gamma_0^2-1}
{\Gamma_0\bar\Gamma_{2,i}+\bar u_{2,i}u_0},\\
\gamma_{m,i}^{\rm r}&=
1+\frac{\epsilon_{e,r}}{f_{e,r}}
\frac{p_r-2}{p_r-1}
\frac{m_p}{m_e}(\gamma_{34,i}-1),\\
\gamma_{\max,i}^{\rm r}
&=\frac{3m_ec^2}{(8e^3\bar B'_{3,i})^{1/2}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The midpoint \(\bar\Gamma_{2,i}\) sets the shock energy scale, and
\(\bar B'_{3,i}\) sets the local synchrotron-loss cutoff.

If shell crossing lies inside the output interval, only the pre-crossing part
receives a source:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta R_{\rm inj}&=\min(R_i,R_\times)-R_{i-1},\\
\mathcal Q^{\rm inj}_{R,i}
&=f_{e,r}\frac{M_{3,{\rm hi}}-M_{3,i-1}}
{m_p\Delta R_{\rm inj}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(M_{3,{\rm hi}}=M_{3,\times}\) for \(R_i>R_\times\) and
\(M_{3,{\rm hi}}=M_{3,i}\) otherwise.  Intervals entirely after crossing set
\(\mathcal Q^{\rm inj}_{R,i}=0\); post-crossing transport cools and expands the
stored spectrum without fresh injection.

\subsection{Reverse cooling assembly}

The reverse cooling assembly converts local radiation losses into the positive
radial coefficient \(D_i\equiv-\gamma_i^{-1}(d\gamma_i/dR)_{\rm rad}\).  For
synchrotron-only cooling,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{\rm syn}(R)&=
\frac{\sigma_{\rm es} B_3^{\prime 2}}{6\pi m_ec^2\,\beta_2\Gamma_2},\\
D_i^{\rm syn}&=a_{\rm syn}\gamma_i,\qquad
\left.\frac{d\gamma_i}{dR}\right|_{\rm syn}
=-D_i^{\rm syn}\gamma_i .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(B'_3\) is the total reverse-shock field and \(\beta_2\Gamma_2\) converts
the comoving cooling rate to a radial rate.

The local IC/KN mode adds the comoving reverse-seed loss coefficient
\(A_i^{\rm IC}\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D_i^{\rm IC}&=
\left(a_{\rm syn}+\frac{A_i^{\rm IC}}{\beta_2\Gamma_2c}\right)\gamma_i,\\
\left.\frac{d\gamma_i}{dR}\right|_{\rm rad}
&=-D_i^{\rm IC}\gamma_i .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This changes the cooling rate before either the finite-volume or DG update is
called.

The Nakar-style Compton mode writes IC cooling as a multiplicative synchrotron
factor,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e_{\rm N}'&=4\Gamma_2^2 n_4m_pc^2,\\
Y_i^{\rm N}&=\frac{A_i^{\rm N}}{4\pi R^2c\,e_{\rm N}'},\\
D_i^{\rm N}&=a_{\rm syn}(1+Y_i^{\rm N})\gamma_i .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(A_i^{\rm N}\) is the seed-synchrotron power integrated over the Thomson
part of the spectrum.  The same factor reduces the local injection cutoff to
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\max,{\rm IC}}^{\rm r}
\equiv \gamma_{\max}^{\rm r}(1+Y_{\max}^{\rm N})^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
All three modes return a positive \(D_i\).  The next transport step uses that
coefficient together with the reverse-shell expansion rate.

\subsection{Reverse finite-volume transport and substeps}

The reverse finite-volume step moves the electron spectrum through energy space
as the shell expands and radiates.  The adiabatic coefficient is \(a_{\rm ad}=1/R\)
before shell crossing.  After crossing, it is measured from the stored region-3
volume:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{\rm ad}&=\frac{1}{3}\frac{d\ln V_3}{dR},\\
a_i^{\rm ad}&\simeq
\frac{\ln(V_{3,i}/V_{3,i-1})}{3(R_i-R_{i-1})}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with \(V_{3,\times}\) used when the output interval contains the crossing point.
Here \(a_{\rm ad}\) gives the radial adiabatic cooling rate of the stored
reverse-electron spectrum.

The conservative coordinate \(y\) is the log-four-velocity coordinate used by the
\texttt{fullhide\_1d} branch.  If \(x=\ln\gamma_e\) and
\(j_y=dx/dy\), the face speed is
\begin{equation}
v_{y,i+1/2}=
\frac{D_{i+1/2}+a_{\rm ad}}{j_{y,i+1/2}},
\end{equation}
and the finite-volume update advances
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial N_y}{\partial R}
+
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
\left[-v_yN_y\right]
=Q_y ,
\end{equation}
where \(N_y\) is the cell density in the conservative energy coordinate,
\(Q_y\) is the reverse-shock source in that coordinate, and \(D_{i+1/2}\) is the
face value of the selected radiative cooling coefficient.  This is the same
face-flux equation as the forward shell solver, with the forward source replaced
by the reverse radial source.

The radial substep size is set by the fastest local cooling and expansion rate.
For the \texttt{fullhide\_1d} branch,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{m,i}&=\frac{R_i+R_{i-1}}{2},\\
a_i^\ell&=
\begin{cases}
2/(3R_{m,i}), & \hbox{primary RS},\\
1/R_{i-1}, & \hbox{secondary/reacceleration},
\end{cases}\\
\Delta R_{\rm cand}
&=\frac{C_R}{a_{\rm syn}\gamma_{\max}+a_i^\ell},\\
N_c&=\left\lfloor\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{\Delta R_{\rm cand}}\right\rfloor,\\
N_{\rm lim}&=\min(N_{\max},N_c),\\
N_{\rm sub}&=\max(N_{\min},N_{\rm lim}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The \texttt{dg\_1d} branch instead uses \(N_{\rm DG}\) substeps and the DG
transport formulas above.  The controls \(C_R\), \(N_{\min}\), \(N_{\max}\), and
\(N_{\rm DG}\) are fixed transport settings, not fitted physical parameters.

\subsection{Post-crossing characteristic remap}

The post-crossing characteristic remap evolves only the electrons that were
already shocked before shell crossing.  After \(R_\times\), the reverse-shock
source is set to zero.  The code stores the spectrum as
\(N_x=dN_e/dx\) with \(x=\ln\gamma_e\), traces each current cell edge back to
the crossing-time map, and conservatively places the old electron content into
the current grid.  This step changes electron energies by cooling and expansion;
it does not add new particles.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron affine map}

The synchrotron characteristic map is analytic in inverse energy.  For
\(u\equiv1/\gamma_e\), synchrotron cooling plus expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{du}{dR}=a_{\rm syn}+a_{\rm ad}u .
\end{equation}
If \(u_{\rm now}\) is a current cell edge and the radial step is \(\Delta R\),
the edge at the previous radius is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\eta_u&=\frac{a_{\rm syn}}{a_{\rm ad}},\qquad
E_u=\exp(-a_{\rm ad}\Delta R)
\quad(a_{\rm ad}\ne0),\\
u_{\rm prev}&=
\begin{cases}
u_{\rm now}-a_{\rm syn}\Delta R, & a_{\rm ad}=0,\\
(u_{\rm now}+\eta_u)E_u-\eta_u, & a_{\rm ad}\ne0 .
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The code converts \(u_{\rm prev}\) back to \(x_{\rm prev}=-\ln u_{\rm prev}\).
It then composes this edge with the map stored at crossing.  The output is the
old \(x\)-interval that feeds the current cell.

\subsubsection{IC/KN piecewise-affine map}

The IC/KN characteristic map allows the cooling coefficient to vary with electron
energy.  In one energy cell \(h\), the code fits the inverse-energy speed with
\begin{equation}
\frac{du}{dR}=\alpha_h+\beta_hu .
\end{equation}
The same affine solution is then applied until the traced edge reaches a cell
boundary; after that point the next cell coefficients are used.  This is a
piecewise characteristic map for radiative cooling, not a new emission model.

The conservative remap uses the traced edges to integrate the old log-energy
content over the old interval:
\begin{equation}
N_{x,i}^{\rm new}
=\frac{1}{\Delta x_i}
\int_{x_{{\rm old},i-1/2}}^{x_{{\rm old},i+1/2}}
N_x^{\rm old}(x)\,dx .
\end{equation}
The low-energy boundary is closed during this post-crossing remap.  The final
diagnostic \(dN_e/d\gamma_e\) is reconstructed from \(N_x\) using
\(dN_e/d\gamma_e=N_x/\gamma_e\).  Thus the post-crossing branch preserves the
stored electron content subject only to cooling, expansion, and the finite
transport grid.

\subsection{Secondary-reservoir electron transport}

Each density-jump reverse-shock branch \(j\) carries a diagnostic electron
reservoir with \(M_j,V_j,B'_j,\gamma_{m,j}\), and its own spectrum.  The transport
machinery is the same as for the primary reverse shock; the branch is not an
independent emission model.

\subsubsection{Reservoir source and volume loss}

On \([R_{i-1},R_i]\), branch \(j\) receives electrons only from newly processed
mass:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q^{s}_{R,j}
&=f_{e,r}\frac{M_{j,i}-M_{j,i-1}}
{m_p(R_i-R_{i-1})},\\
a_{V,j}&=
\frac{\ln(V_{j,i}/V_{j,i-1})}
{3(R_i-R_{i-1})}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(a_{V,j}\) is applied only when both endpoint volumes are positive.  A
branch with no positive processed mass leaves its spectrum and luminosity at zero.

\subsubsection{Reservoir radiation product}

Each active reservoir uses the same synchrotron and SSA kernels as the other
electron branches.  The reported observer-frame absorption break is
\begin{equation}
\nu_a^{\rm obs}=
\frac{\nu_a'}
{\Gamma_2(1-\beta_2)(1+z)} .
\end{equation}
where \(\nu_a'\) is the comoving SSA frequency of one reservoir.  The
branch-summed secondary luminosity is
\begin{equation}
L_{\nu'}^{\prime,s}=\sum_jL_{\nu',j}^{\prime,s}.
\end{equation}
This is a synchrotron luminosity history for observer assembly, with no separate
secondary-reservoir SSC, hadronic, or pair-feedback branch.

\subsection{Reacceleration diagnostic}

The reacceleration diagnostic follows old electrons when a later density-jump
branch shocks material from an earlier reservoir.  It transfers only available
parent mass, boosts that seed spectrum, applies a diffusive-shock acceleration
remap, and injects only the remaining fresh mass.

\subsubsection{Parent mass transfer}

If branch \(j\) has parent label \(\pi_j\), the transferred mass and seed
spectrum are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta M_j^{\rm tr}&=\min(\Delta M_j,M_{\pi_j}^{\rm av}),\\
f_j^{\rm tr}&=\frac{\Delta M_j^{\rm tr}}{M_{\pi_j}^{\rm av}},\\
N_{\log,j}^{s}(\gamma)&=f_j^{\rm tr}N_{\log,\pi_j}(\gamma).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Transfer requires a dissipative branch, \(\gamma_{m,j}>1\) and
\(\Gamma_{43,j}>1\), and positive available parent mass.  Otherwise
\(N_{\log,j}^{s}=0\).

\subsubsection{Compression boost}

The branch boost maps the transferred spectrum to the new shock scale:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_j^{c}&=
\begin{cases}
r_j, & r_j>1,\\
1, & r_j\le1,
\end{cases}\\
g_j&=\Gamma_{43,j}(r_j^{c})^{1/3},\\
N_{\log,j}^{b}(\gamma)
&=N_{\log,j}^{s}(\gamma/g_j).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(r_j\) is the stored compression factor and \(\Gamma_{43,j}\) is the
secondary-shock relative Lorentz factor.  The shift conserves particle number in
log-energy space.

\subsubsection{Diffusive-shock acceleration remap}

For \(p_r>2\), the diffusive-shock acceleration remap is
\begin{equation}
\frac{dN_{j}^{a}}{d\gamma}
=(p_r-1)\gamma^{-p_r}
\int_1^\gamma
\frac{dN_j^{b}}{d\tilde\gamma}
\tilde\gamma^{p_r-1}\,d\tilde\gamma,
\qquad p_r>2 .
\end{equation}
Here \(N_j^b\) is the boosted seed spectrum and \(N_j^a\) is the reaccelerated
spectrum; \(p_r>2\) is a hard boundary of this diagnostic branch.

\subsubsection{Energy accounting}

The diagnostic records the seed and reaccelerated electron energies,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_j^{s}&=\int(\gamma-1)m_ec^2N_{\log,j}^{s}\,d\ln\gamma,\\
E_j^{a}&=\int(\gamma-1)m_ec^2N_{\log,j}^{a}\,d\ln\gamma .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The parent loses \(\Delta M_j^{\rm tr}\), the new branch gains that mass, and the
remaining \(\Delta M_j-\Delta M_j^{\rm tr}\) enters through the fresh kinetic
source.  \(E_j^s\) and \(E_j^a\) are reprocessing-budget diagnostics, not extra
radiative components.

\subsection{Density-jump secondary-shock solver}

Secondary reverse shocks are diagnostic branches tied to density-jump events, not
independent emission components.  A branch opens only if the rising side of an
external-density bump drives a reverse shock that produces more heat than
adiabatic compression of the same upstream reservoir.

\subsubsection{Event-window density}

For a Gaussian jump \(j\), \asgard\ defines \(\Delta R_j=w_jR_j\) and uses only
the rising side:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_j-\eta_J\Delta R_j&\le R<R_j,\\
x_j&=\frac{R-R_j}{\Delta R_j},\\
n_j^{\rm ex}(R)&=n_{\rm b}(R)(f_j-1)\exp(-x_j^2/2),\\
n_1&=n_{\rm b}+\sum_{\ell}n_\ell^{\rm ex}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(n_1\) is the total upstream density in the contact problem, while
\(n_j^{\rm ex}\) is the selected excess density for branch \(j\).  Both are
number densities, not dimensionless multipliers.

\subsubsection{Secondary inertia contribution}

Active density-jump branches add the effective inertia
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p'_s&=(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1)\frac{U_s}{V_s},\\
M_s&=\frac{M_s^bc^2+U_s+p'_sV_s}{c^2}
+\frac{p'_sV_s}{\Gamma_2^2c^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}=4/3\), and \((M_s^b,U_s,V_s)\) are the summed baryonic
mass, thermal energy, and volume.  If no branch has positive mass, heat, and
volume, \(M_s=0\).

\subsubsection{Region-4 upstream state}

For the first secondary branch, region 4 is the pre-bump forward-shock downstream
state:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n_{\rm pre}&=n_1-n_j^{\rm ex},\qquad r_2=4\Gamma_2,\\
n'_4&=r_2 n_{\rm pre},\\
e'_4&=(\Gamma_2-1)n'_4m_pc^2,\\
p'_4&=(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1)e'_4 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
If a previous secondary branch has finite state, that parent supplies the local
upstream instead:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
n'_4&=\frac{M_p}{m_pV_p},\\
e'_4&=\frac{U_p}{V_p},\qquad
p'_4=(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1)e'_4 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This chained upstream state is used only when \(M_p,U_p,V_p>0\).

\subsubsection{Contact pressure root}

For a trial secondary contact Lorentz factor \(g_c\), pressure balance uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p'_2(g_c)&=
\hat\gamma_{\rm h}(g_c^2-1)n_1m_pc^2,\\
p'_3(g_c)&=
p'_4+\hat\gamma_{\rm h}(\Gamma_{43}^2-1)(e'_4+p'_4),\\
\beta_c&=(1-g_c^{-2})^{1/2},\\
\Gamma_{43}&=g_c\Gamma_4(1-\beta_c\beta_4).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The solver searches \(1<g_c<\Gamma_4\) for \(p'_2(g_c)-p'_3(g_c)=0\), where
\((\Gamma_4,\beta_4)\) describe region 4.

The branch starts only when the contact solution is compressive and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi&=\frac{n'_3}{n'_4},\\
e'_{3,{\rm hot}}&=3p'_3,\\
e'_{\rm ad}&=e'_4\chi^{4/3},\\
S_j&=e'_{3,{\rm hot}}-e'_{\rm ad}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
has \(S_j>0\).  A sign change of \(S_j\) opens or closes branch \(j\) inside the
selected rising side of the bump.

\subsubsection{Shock-frame compression}

For a trial laboratory shock speed \(\beta_s\), the shock-frame speeds and
compression are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{4s}&=\frac{\beta_4-\beta_s}{1-\beta_4\beta_s},
\qquad
\beta_{3s}=\frac{\beta_c-\beta_s}{1-\beta_c\beta_s},\\
u_{is}&=\Gamma_{is}|\beta_{is}|\quad (i=3,4),\\
\chi&\equiv\frac{n'_3}{n'_4}=\frac{u_{4s}}{u_{3s}} .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The stored diagnostic \(\beta_{\rm rs}\) is this laboratory-frame root
\(\beta_s\).  The solver accepts only \(\chi>1\) states that satisfy
\begin{equation}
w'_4u_{4s}^2+p'_4=w'_3u_{3s}^2+p'_3,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w'_4&=n'_4m_pc^2+e'_4+p'_4,\\
e'_3&=\frac{p'_3}{\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1},
\qquad
w'_3=\chi n'_4m_pc^2+e'_3+p'_3 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This relativistically hot closure is not a general finite-temperature Taub solve.
Once contact balance fixes \(p'_3\), mass and momentum flux determine only the
shock speed and compression.

\subsubsection{Dissipated-energy source}

If a compressive momentum root exists, new heat is deposited only when
\begin{equation}
\Delta e'_j=e'_{3,{\rm hot}}-e'_4\chi^{\hat\gamma_{\rm h}}>0,
\end{equation}
which subtracts the adiabatically compressed upstream energy from the newly
shocked hot-gas state.  Otherwise the branch only advects its stored material.

An active branch then adds mass, heat, and volume through
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dV_{q,j}}{dR}&=\frac{1}{n'_3m_p}\frac{dM_j}{dR},\\
\frac{dV_{e,j}}{dR}
&=V_j\frac{d}{dR}\ln\left(\frac{R^3}{\Gamma_2}\right),\\
\frac{dM_j}{dR}
&=4\pi R^2n'_4m_p\Gamma_2
\frac{\beta_2-\beta_{\rm rs}}{\beta_c},\\
\frac{dU_j}{dR}
&=\Delta e'_j\frac{dV_{q,j}}{dR}
-(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1)\frac{U_j}{V_j}
\frac{dV_{e,j}}{dR},\\
\frac{dV_j}{dR}
&=\frac{dV_{q,j}}{dR}+\frac{dV_{e,j}}{dR}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(q\) denotes newly shocked volume and \(e\) expansion of stored branch
material.

For \(p_r>2\), the newly heated secondary electrons have
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{m,j}=1+
\frac{\epsilon_{e,r}}{f_{e,r}}
\frac{p_r-2}{p_r-1}
\frac{\Delta e'_j}{n'_3m_ec^2}.
\end{equation}
The branch is accepted only below the local synchrotron cutoff.  The ODE stores
cumulative branch mass, heat, volume, dissipated energy, and
\(\gamma_{m,j}\)-weighted dissipated energy; these are diagnostics and inputs to
the branch electron source, not an inertia term beyond \(M_s\).

\subsection{Secondary event windows and observer diagnostics}

The observer layer opens a secondary density-jump branch only where the hot
post-shock state exceeds adiabatic compression of the same upstream gas:
\begin{equation}
S_j(R)=e'_{3,j}-e'_{4,j}\chi_j^{\hat\gamma_{\rm h}}.
\end{equation}
Here \(e'_{3,j}\) is the shocked hot-gas energy density and
\(e'_{4,j}\chi_j^{\hat\gamma_{\rm h}}\) is the compressed upstream value.

\subsubsection{Event-root scanner}

The scanner converts the sign of \(S_j\) on the rising bump
\([R_j-\eta_J\Delta R_j,R_j)\) into
\((R_{j,\rm start},R_{j,\rm end},t_{j,\rm start},t_{j,\rm end})\).  It brackets
with steps no wider than \(\Delta R_j/N_J^{\rm scan}\), linearly interpolates
\((Y,\Gamma_2,t_{\rm obs})\) inside each bracket, and bisects sign changes in
radius.  An open branch at the final sampled state is closed at that last
physical state.

\subsubsection{Dimensional branch state}

The dimensionless branch entries are restored as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_j&=Y_{m,j}M_{\rm ej},\\
U_j&=Y_{u,j}M_{\rm ej}c^2,\\
V_j&=Y_{v,j}V_0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These public diagnostics also set the secondary-reservoir electron source when
that reservoir is enabled.

\subsubsection{Dissipation-weighted diagnostics}

Shock diagnostics use newly dissipated energy rather than old inactive mass.  If
\(E^q_{j,i}\) is the cumulative shocked energy in branch \(j\) at radius \(R_i\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_{j,i}&=E^q_{j,i}-E^q_{j,i-1},
\qquad
\langle Q\rangle_i&=
\frac{\sum_j \Delta E_{j,i}Q_{j,i}}
{\sum_j \Delta E_{j,i}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(Q_{j,i}\) denotes the contact Lorentz factor, shock Lorentz factor,
compression ratio, or injection Lorentz factor.  If no branch dissipates new
heat at \(R_i\), the diagnostic keeps its initialized value.

\subsubsection{Combined secondary state}

The one-dimensional output layer receives the reservoir sums
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_s^b&=\sum_j M_j,\\
U_s&=\sum_j U_j,\\
V_s&=\sum_j V_j,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and, when \(V_s>0\), the effective field, pressure, and enthalpy density
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B'_s&=\left(8\pi\epsilon_{B,r}\frac{U_s}{V_s}\right)^{1/2},\\
p'_s&=(\hat\gamma_{\rm h}-1)\frac{U_s}{V_s},\\
w'_s&=\frac{M_s^b c^2+U_s}{V_s}+p'_s .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
No combined secondary state is reported where \(V_s\le0\).

\subsubsection{Break-frequency diagnostics}

The density-jump synchrotron diagnostics use the same dissipation weights for
the injection break,
\begin{equation}
\bar{\gamma}_{m,s}=
\frac{\sum_j \Delta E_{j,i}\gamma_{m,j,i}}
{\sum_j \Delta E_{j,i}},
\end{equation}
where \(\bar{\gamma}_{m,s}\) is the energy-weighted injection Lorentz factor.
With
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_\nu&=\frac{3e}{4\pi m_ec},\\
\bar\beta_c&=(1-\langle g_c\rangle^{-2})^{1/2},
\delta_c^{-1}&=\langle g_c\rangle(1-\bar\beta_c),\\
\beta_2&=(1-\Gamma_2^{-2})^{1/2},\\
\delta_2^{-1}&=\Gamma_2(1-\beta_2),\\
\gamma_{c,s}&=
\frac{6\pi m_ec(1+z)}
{\sigma_{\rm es} B_s^{\prime 2}\Gamma_2 t_{\rm obs}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the diagnostic break frequencies are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{m,s}=
\frac{C_\nu B'_s\bar{\gamma}_{m,s}^2\delta_c}{1+z},\\
\nu_{c,s}=
\frac{C_\nu B'_s\gamma_{c,s}^2\delta_2}{1+z}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The injection break uses the energy-weighted contact Doppler factor, whereas
the cooling break uses the region-2 Doppler factor and current \(t_{\rm obs}\).
These frequencies are diagnostics, not a new multi-zone radiation calculation.

\subsubsection{Supported branch boundary}

This diagnostic path supports \(p_r>2\), the one-dimensional
\texttt{fullhide\_1d} or \texttt{dg\_1d} electron solver, and the direct 1D
observer path.  It excludes hadronic processes, forward SSC cooling, reverse
SSC, cross-zone IC, and coupled electron-photon feedback.  Event windows are
roots from linearly interpolated adjacent outputs, while adaptive RK refinement
controls only the primary variables \((\Gamma_2,R,M_2)\).  The secondary
reverse-shock output is therefore a density-jump diagnostic, not a general
reverse-shock model
\citep{DaiLu2002,NakarGranot2007,UhmZhang2014,Laskar2016GRB160509A,Alexander2017GRB160625B,Laskar2019GRB181201A,PangDai2024OffaxisRS}.

\section{Hadronic and Pair-Feedback Boundary} \label{sec:hadronic}

\subsection{Activation, state, and grid}

Hadronic transport is a one-dimensional shell branch activated only when the
hadronic module is enabled and \(\epsilon_p>0\).  The legacy branch transports
protons and proton synchrotron only, whereas the formal AM3/Hummer branch can add
\(p\gamma\), Bethe--Heitler, \(pp\), secondary species, neutrinos, hadronic IC,
and pair-feedback operators
\citep{Murase2007,Hummer2010,KelnerAharonian2008,Chodorowski1992,GouldSchreder1967,IceCube2017GRBNeutrinos,Sahu2022GRB190829AHadronic,Sahu2024GRB221009AHadronic}.
Inactive switches remove their operators before observer assembly.  Non-1D
forward-electron solvers are rejected because \asgard\ has no \(\chi\)-local
target photon field, hadron density, or secondary feedback state for this branch.
The package binding only selects compiled handles,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_{\rm FS}&\equiv\texttt{hadronic\_forward\_1d},\\
H_{\rm RS}&\equiv\texttt{hadronic\_reverse\_1d},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and does not alter the particle equations.

The shell-local returned state is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
{\cal Y}_h=\{&
N_p,N_n,N_{\pi^0},N_{\pi^\pm},N_{\mu^\pm},\\
&N_\nu,Q_e^{\rm BH},Q_e^{pp},Q_e^{\gamma\gamma}\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with all spectra stored on a logarithmic Lorentz-factor grid,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\frac{x_{\max}-x_{\min}}{N_\gamma-1},\\
x_i&=x_{\min}+(i-1)\Delta x,\\
\gamma_i&=10^{x_i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Source and interaction support is represented by the first and last grid indices
that satisfy the requested Lorentz-factor bounds.  Shell timing is inherited from
the radius-ordered state through \(\Delta t_i'=t_i'-t_{i-1}'\) and
\(t'_{\rm dyn}=R/(\Gamma c)\); the hadronic kernel does not introduce a fitted
observer-time grid.

\subsection{Injection and acceleration}

The shell energy \(E_{s,i}\) fixes the comoving source luminosity
\(L'_{s,i}=E_{s,i}/\Delta t'_i\), and
\(\Delta V_i=4\pi(R_i^3-R_{i-1}^3)/3\) converts particle content to local density.
For species \(s\), the unnormalized source shape is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_s(\gamma)&=
\gamma^{-p_s}\mathcal C_s(\gamma)\,
\mathbf{1}_{\gamma_{\min}\le\gamma\le\gamma_{\rm inj}},\\
\mathcal C_s&=
\begin{cases}
\exp(-\gamma/\gamma_{\rm cut}),&{\rm cutoff\ active},\\
1,&{\rm otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The formal shell source uses the relativistic energy moment,
\begin{equation}
A_s=\int S_s(\gamma)\gamma\,d\gamma,\qquad
Q_s(\gamma)=\frac{L'_{s,i}S_s(\gamma)}{m_sc^2A_s},
\end{equation}
so \(m_sc^2\int Q_s\gamma\,d\gamma=L'_{s,i}\).  The legacy proton-only branch
uses the kinetic-energy moment instead.

Charged species use
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(m_s,Z_s)&\in\{(m_p,1),(m_n,0),
(m_\pi,\pm1),(m_\mu,\pm1)\},\\
|q_s|&=|Z_s|e .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Their maximum Lorentz factor is set by acceleration, dynamical, synchrotron, and
optional tabulated cooling limits:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\rm dyn}
&=\frac{|q_s|B'R}{\eta_{\rm acc}\Gamma m_sc^2},\\
\gamma_{\rm syn}
&=\left[\frac{6\pi |q_s|m_s^2}
{\eta_{\rm acc}\sigma_{\rm es}m_e^2B'}\right]^{1/2},\\
\gamma_{\max}&=\min(\gamma_{\rm dyn},\gamma_{\rm syn},\gamma_{\rm ext})
\quad(\gamma_{\rm ext}\ {\rm omitted\ if\ absent}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The optional quantum-synchrotron correction multiplies classical synchrotron
losses by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi&=\gamma\,\frac{B'}{B_Q}\frac{m_e}{m_s},\\
\mathcal Q(\chi)&=
\begin{cases}
1, & \chi\le\chi_Q,\\
\left[1+\sqrt{2}\,\chi^{2/3}\right]^{-2},&\chi>\chi_Q,
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with \(B_Q=m_e^2c^3/(e\hbar)\) \citep{Klinger2024AM3}.  Neutral species skip the
acceleration-limit calculation.

\subsection{Hadronic process operators}

The proton update collects injection, continuous losses, interaction losses, and
reinjection:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial N_p}{\partial R}
=Q_{p,R}+\mathcal{R}_{p\gamma}
-\mathcal{L}_{p\gamma}-\mathcal{L}_{\rm BH}
-\mathcal{L}_{pp}-\mathcal{L}_{p,{\rm c}} .
\end{equation}
Continuous proton losses are written as positive cooling rates,
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm ad}=\frac{\gamma_p}{t'_{\rm dyn}},\qquad
C_{\rm syn}=
\frac{\sigma_{\rm es} B'^2}{6\pi m_ec}
\left(\frac{m_e}{m_p}\right)^3\gamma_p^2,
\end{equation}
with Bethe--Heitler and \(pp\) terms added only when active.  The \(p\gamma\)
interaction is applied as a finite-step sink plus same-step reinjection,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{p,i}^{\rm new}
&=N_{p,i}^{\rm c}e^{-\lambda_{p\gamma,i}\Delta t'}
+t'_{{\rm eff},i}V_i\mathcal{R}_{p\gamma,i},\\
t'_{{\rm eff},i}
&=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{p\gamma,i}\Delta t'}}{\lambda_{p\gamma,i}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the zero-rate limit \(t'_{\rm eff}=\Delta t'\).

Bethe--Heitler pair production uses
\(\gamma_p=E_p/(m_pc^2)\), \(x_\gamma=\epsilon/(m_ec^2)\), and
\(x_e=E_e/(m_ec^2)\).  Cells with \(\gamma_px_\gamma\le1\) return no pair source,
photon sink, or proton loss.  For allowed cells, \asgard\ evaluates the
Blumenthal--Gould differential kernel
\citep{BlumenthalGould1970},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}_{\rm BH}
=\frac{x_e}{2\gamma_p^3x_\gamma^2}
\int d\omega\int d\bar E\,
\frac{\omega\,\sigma_{\rm BH}(\omega,\bar E,\xi)}{\bar p},
\end{equation}
inside the finite fit domain.  On log-uniform proton and photon grids this kernel
returns
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_e^{\rm BH}(E_{e,j})
&=\frac{k_{\rm BH}\Delta\ln E_p\,\Delta\ln\epsilon}{E_{e,j}}
\sum_{i,k}Y_iZ_kK_{jik},\\
\Lambda_{\gamma}^{\rm BH}(\epsilon_k)
&=\frac{k_{\rm BH}\Delta\ln E_p\,\Delta\ln E_e}{\epsilon_kn_\gamma(\epsilon_k)}
\sum_{i,j}Y_iZ_kK_{jik},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(Y_i=E_{p,i}N_p(E_{p,i})\), \(Z_k=\epsilon_kn_\gamma(\epsilon_k)\), and
\(k_{\rm BH}=3c\sigma_{\rm es}\alpha_{\rm f}/(16\pi)\).  Proton cooling uses the
positive Chodorowski et al. rate \(\mathcal C_p^{\rm BH}\) built from the
published piecewise loss fit \(\phi_{\rm C}\) \citep{Chodorowski1992}.  The
Bethe--Heitler branch therefore returns one secondary \(e^\pm\) source, one
target-photon loss rate, and one proton cooling rate.

\subsection{Photon survival, secondaries, and feedback}

The \(p\gamma\) response uses two shell-local passes.  The first pass computes the
photon-loss depth; the second pass sees the finite-cell survived photon density
\begin{equation}
S_\gamma(\nu',R)=\frac{1-e^{-\tau_\gamma(\nu',R)}}{\tau_\gamma(\nu',R)},
\qquad S_\gamma\to1\quad(\tau_\gamma\to0).
\end{equation}
The survived field generates neutral-pion photons, charged pions, muons,
neutrinos, neutron and proton reinjection, and local secondary-pair sources.
The \(pp\) branch uses the fixed delta-approximation parameters
\((\kappa_{pp},\xi_\pi,f_{\pi^0})\).  Bethe--Heitler and \(pp\) pairs are passed
to the electron layer; when they are merged into the forward electron
distribution, no separate Bethe--Heitler luminosity is assembled.

The joint electron--photon--hadron loop is local to one shell:
\begin{equation}
N_e^{(r)}
\rightarrow n_\gamma^{(r)}
\rightarrow \{Q_e^{{\rm sec},(r)},\tau_{\gamma\gamma}^{(r)}\}
\rightarrow N_e^{(r+1)}.
\end{equation}
Observer projection reads only the completed local sequence.  For
\(N_{\rm cas}>1\), \asgard\ uses the shell-sequence
\(\gamma\gamma\) pair/synchrotron cascade; the single-shell path is a diagnostic
mode, and IC-mediated electromagnetic cascades are unsupported.

\subsection{Shell-sequence pair cascade}

The cascade carries pairs from shell to shell, updates the local photon density,
and returns pair density, pair synchrotron luminosity, pair seed photons, and
\(\gamma\gamma\) optical depth.  Pair energies share the photon logarithmic
spacing,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
j_0&=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(m_ec^2/E_{\gamma,1})}{\Delta\ln E}\right\rceil,
\\
E_{e,j}&=E_{\gamma,1}\exp[(j-1+j_0)\Delta\ln E],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and photons reside in shell \(i\) for
\(t'_{{\rm esc},i}=R_i/(\eta_{\rm esc}\Gamma_i c)\).  At cascade substep \(m\),
the current photon density returns
\(\{\lambda_{\gamma\gamma},q_{\pm,\gamma\gamma},P_{\rm abs},P_{\rm inj}\}\), and
the pair source added to the grid is
\begin{equation}
S_{\pm,j}^{(m)}=
\Delta V_i\,m_ec^2q_{\pm,\gamma\gamma}(E_{e,j})\Delta t'_{\rm sub},
\qquad
\Delta t'_{\rm sub}=\frac{\Delta t'_i}{N_{\rm sub}} .
\end{equation}
Pairs then cool by synchrotron plus adiabatic losses,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\pm,i}(\gamma)=
\frac{\sigma_{\rm es}B_i^{\prime2}}{6\pi m_ec}\gamma^2
+\frac{\gamma}{t'_{{\rm dyn},i}},
\end{equation}
through the same finite-grid cooled-content remap used by the electron transport.
The photon field closes the local loop with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dn_\epsilon}{dt'}&=S_\epsilon-\Lambda_\epsilon n_\epsilon,
\\
\Lambda_\epsilon&=\lambda_{\gamma\gamma}+\frac{1}{t'_{\rm esc}},\\
S_\epsilon&=\frac{n_{\epsilon,{\rm prim}}+n_{\epsilon,\pm}}{t'_{\rm esc}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The stored powers \(P_{\rm abs}\) and \(P_{\rm inj}\) are local energy-closure
diagnostics, not observer-frame fluxes.  The single-shell pair-synchrotron mode
keeps only the local \(\gamma\gamma\) absorption and synchrotron cooling track; it
does not carry pairs or update photons in the next shell.


\section{Observer Projection and Component Assembly} \label{sec:projection}

\subsection{Equal-arrival-time projection}

Observer projection reads the radius-ordered local spectra and absorption
factors and maps them to \(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\).  For an angular cell
\((\theta,\phi)\) viewed at \(\theta_v\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mu&=\cos\theta_v\cos\theta+\sin\theta_v\sin\theta\cos\phi,\\
t_{\rm arr}(R,\theta,\phi)
&=t_0(R)+\frac{(1+z)R(1-\mu)}{c},\\
\delta^{-1}&=\Gamma(1-\beta\mu).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The observer frequency and angular weight are
\begin{equation}
\nu'=\nu_{\rm obs}(1+z)\delta^{-1},\qquad
dW=\delta^3\,\frac{d\Omega}{4\pi}.
\end{equation}
The code brackets each observer time between adjacent arrival radii, interpolates
the local SED on that radial segment, and then interpolates in frequency on the
stored logarithmic grid.  Positive neighboring SED bins use log--log
interpolation; otherwise the linear-amplitude rule is used.  Projection changes
only how local photons are observed.

\subsection{Structured-jet aggregation boundary}

Structured jets are projected by solving active angular patches and summing their
equal-arrival-time contributions.  A direct top-hat model with a supported medium
uses the direct kernel-backed path.  The Fortran structured backend supports
uniform patch sampling with the one-dimensional \texttt{fullhide\_1d} or
\texttt{dg\_1d} electron solvers, forward synchrotron/SSC, reverse synchrotron,
and the supported structured \(p\gamma\)/neutrino path.  It excludes reverse SSC,
cross-zone IC, reverse hadronic branches, Bethe--Heitler, \(pp\), hadronic IC,
and pair-cascade branches.  Dominant-region samplers use the Python patch
backend.

For patch \((a,b)\), the active register is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E_{ab}&=E_{\rm iso}(\phi_b,\theta_a),\qquad
\Gamma_{0,ab}=\Gamma_0(\phi_b,\theta_a),\\
A_{ab}&=\mathbf{1}(E_{ab}>0)\,
\mathbf{1}(\Gamma_{0,ab}\ge\Gamma_{\min}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
An active patch runs the same local one-dimensional solve as a direct top-hat
model after the substitutions
\(\Gamma_0\leftarrow\Gamma_{0,ab}\) and \(E_{\rm iso}\leftarrow E_{ab}\).  The
aggregate changes angular weights and state reuse; it does not change the local
shock, particle, or synchrotron equations.

Before projection, each local component luminosity receives the common shell
prefactor
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{P}_{\nu'}(R)&=
\frac{1+z}{4\pi d_L^2}\,
\mathcal{A}_{\rm abs}(\nu',R),\\
\widetilde L_{\nu'}^{\,c}(R,q)&=
\mathcal{P}_{\nu'}(R)L_{\nu'}^{\prime c}(R,q).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Active component labels can include FS synchrotron, FS SSC, FS hadronic photons,
Bethe--Heitler photons, hadronic IC, pair-cascade photons, RS synchrotron, RS
hadronic photons, RS SSC, and cross-zone IC.  The returned total is the algebraic
sum of the active projected components; inactive components remain absent.

\subsection{Projection kernels and direct top-hat path}

The \texttt{src.Interpolation} binding only exposes compiled projection kernels.
It maps local arrays
\begin{equation}
\{\widetilde L_{\nu'}(R),\tau_{\nu'}(R),R,\Gamma,t_{\rm obs},\nu_{\rm obs}\}
\longrightarrow F_{\nu_{\rm obs}}(t_{\rm obs}),
\end{equation}
leaving the radiation process, optical depth, Doppler factor, arrival-time map,
and interpolation weights inside the selected Fortran kernel.

The direct top-hat kernel divides the emitting surface into midpoint angular
cells with
\begin{equation}
\Delta\Omega_a=
(\cos\theta_{a,-}-\cos\theta_{a,+})\,\Delta\phi .
\end{equation}
For an observer-time bracket between \(R_i\) and \(R_{i+1}\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_R&=\frac{t_{\rm obs}-t_{\rm arr}(R_i)}
{t_{\rm arr}(R_{i+1})-t_{\rm arr}(R_i)},\\
\Gamma_R&=\exp[(1-\lambda_R)\ln\Gamma_i+\lambda_R\ln\Gamma_{i+1}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The cell contribution is
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\nu_{\rm obs}}=
\frac{\Delta\Omega_a}{4\pi}\,
\delta_R^3\,\widetilde L_{\nu'}(R_R),
\qquad
\nu'=\frac{1+z}{\delta_R}\nu_{\rm obs}.
\end{equation}
Adaptive angular quadrature changes only polar sampling.  It accepts a trial
interval when
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\nu,t}|F_{\rm fine}-F_{\rm coarse}|
\le \epsilon_{\rm ad}\sum_{\nu,t}|F_{\rm fine}|,
\end{equation}
or when the requested recursion depth is reached.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\chi\)}{chi}-resolved and ray-traced projection}

The \(\chi\)-resolved projection treats each downstream cell as a separate
emitting zone.  Its arrival time uses the shock-front radius \(R_f\) and the
downstream-cell radius \(R_\chi\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
t_\chi(R)&=t_0(R)+\frac{1+z}{c}
\left[R_f(R)-\mu R_\chi(R)\right],
\\
\delta_\chi&=\left[\Gamma_\chi(1-\beta_\chi\mu)\right]^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite-cell transfer factor is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_\chi(\nu)=
\exp[-\tau_{\rm front}(\nu)]\,\psi[\tau_\chi(\nu)],
\qquad
\psi(x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x},
\end{equation}
and the projected cell contribution is
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\nu_{\rm obs},\chi}=
\frac{\Delta\Omega}{4\pi}\,
\delta_\chi^3 W_\chi
\widetilde L_{\nu',\chi}\mathcal{E}_\chi(\nu').
\end{equation}
If a cell arrival-time sequence is nonmonotonic, the search tests each adjacent
radial segment separately; the arrival-time equation and local spectrum are not
changed.

Structured ring and ray-list variants reuse this \(\chi\)-resolved projection on
precomputed rings.  The batch-luminosity path first applies
\(\mathcal P_d=(1+z)/(4\pi d_L^2)\) and then sums projected ring luminosities.
The ray-traced SSA path builds emitting events \(e=(a,\chi,i,b)\), aberrates the
observer direction into each event frame, and converts stored radial SSA depths
into line-of-sight depths.  A foreground event contributes only if its sky patch
contains the emitting point and it lies in front of that point.  The event flux is
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\nu_{\rm obs}}=
\mathcal{P}_d\,\frac{\Delta\Omega_e}{4\pi}\,\delta_e^3
\widetilde L_{\nu'}\,
\exp[-\tau_{\rm front}]\,\psi(\tau_{r,e}\ell_e),
\end{equation}
where \(\ell_e=\Delta s'_e/\Delta r'_e\).  This is a geometric opacity
calculation over stored luminosity and SSA grids, not a new radiation solver.

\subsection{Legacy structured projection and frequency accumulator}

The legacy structured entries use the same local arrival-time and Doppler map as
the direct kernel, but with patch-dependent histories:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{ab}=\cos\theta_v\cos\bar\theta_a
+\sin\theta_v\sin\bar\theta_a\cos\phi_b,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta F_{\nu_{\rm obs}}=
S_\phi\frac{\Delta\Omega_a}{4\pi}\,
\delta_R^3\,\widetilde L_{\nu'_R,R},
\qquad
\nu'_R=\frac{(1+z)\nu_{\rm obs}}{\delta_R}.
\end{equation}
Axisymmetric half-plane quadratures use the mirror factor \(S_\phi\); explicit
two-dimensional patch grids use \(S_\phi=1\).  Thus the structured file changes
angular weights and patch histories, not the radiative transformation.

The shared frequency accumulator is the final remap to the observer grid.  For
\(x_i=\ln\nu_i\), \(X_j=\ln\nu_{{\rm obs},j}\), and
\(q=(X_j-x_i)/(x_{i+1}-x_i)\), positive neighboring source values use
\begin{equation}
F(X_j)\leftarrow F(X_j)+
\exp[(1-q)\ln S_i+q\ln S_{i+1}],
\end{equation}
whereas intervals touching an empty endpoint use
\begin{equation}
F(X_j)\leftarrow F(X_j)+(1-q)S_i+qS_{i+1}.
\end{equation}
The shifted-source helper applies the same rule after adding the scalar
Doppler/redshift shift.  These helpers change only the frequency grid.

\subsection{Public projection boundaries}

Public light curves return total and component-resolved flux matrices assembled
from already computed component arrays.  Accepted geometry kernels are
\texttt{sed\_legacy}, \texttt{sed\_adaptive\_theta}, and
\texttt{chi\_eats\_2d}.  The \(\chi\)-resolved projection requires a
two-dimensional electron solver and stored \(\chi\)-resolved luminosity, optical
depth, radius, bulk Lorentz factor, and volume weights.  It replaces only the
forward-shock synchrotron plus SSA projection; SSC, hadronic, and pair-cascade
components remain shell-level components.  Off-axis top-hat projection requires
\(N_\phi\ge2\), while the collapsed single-azimuth grid is valid only on axis.
These are projection boundaries, not physical claims about the source.

Ray-traced SSA is nonlocal in angle because one angular cell can absorb emission
from another angular cell.  Its convergence must therefore be checked by global
resolution changes in \(\theta\), \(\phi\), \(\chi\), and radius; local additivity
between two angular pieces is not a valid convergence test for this branch.

\section{Fitting, Runtime, and Reproducibility} \label{sec:fitting}

\subsection{Active-variable map}

The active-variable map is the only place where the fitting wrapper changes a
model parameter.  The sampler proposes a coordinate \(u_j\), and the afterglow
calculation receives the physical parameter \(\vartheta_j\).  Before any radius
step is solved, the code applies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\vartheta_j&=
\begin{cases}
10^{u_j}, & j\in{\cal P}_{\log},\\
u_j, & j\in{\cal P}_{\rm lin},\\
\vartheta_{j,0}, & j\in{\cal P}_{\rm fix},
\end{cases}\\
a_j&\le u_j\le b_j .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \({\cal P}_{\log}\), \({\cal P}_{\rm lin}\), and \({\cal P}_{\rm fix}\)
are the log, linear, and fixed parameter sets.  The numbers \(a_j\) and \(b_j\)
are the sampling bounds.  After this map, the same public model path builds the
runtime configuration, solves the radius sequence, and projects the local state
to the requested observer grid.  The fitting wrapper does not change the
dynamics, particle transport, or projection kernels.

\subsection{Observation-block residuals}

The observation block groups data points that can be evaluated on one observer
grid.  A block may contain flux densities, spectra, or band fluxes.  After one
model solve for that block, the residual code reads the required entries from
the total-flux matrix.  For flux-density and spectral data it uses
\begin{equation}
\ln\mathcal{L}
=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_i
\left[
\frac{F_i^{\rm obs}-F_i^{\rm mod}}{\sigma_i}
\right]^2 ,
\end{equation}
where \(F_i^{\rm obs}\) is the measured flux, \(F_i^{\rm mod}\) is the model
flux, and \(\sigma_i\) is the reported uncertainty.  The fixed normalization is
omitted because the implemented use compares parameter choices with fixed
uncertainties.  The likelihood changes only the score assigned to observer
products.  It does not change the hydrodynamics, electron transport, or
projection kernels.

\subsection{Band-flux quadrature}

The band-flux product is the model spectrum integrated over an observed
frequency band.  For a band observation at observer time \(t_b\), the model value
is
\begin{equation}
F_b^{\rm mod}
=\int_{\nu_1}^{\nu_2}F_\nu(t_b,\nu;\boldsymbol{\vartheta})\,d\nu
\simeq
\sum_q w_q^{\rm trap}
F_\nu(t_b,\nu_q;\boldsymbol{\vartheta}) ,
\end{equation}
where \(\nu_1\) and \(\nu_2\) are the band edges, \(\nu_q\) are the declared
logarithmic band points, and \(w_q^{\rm trap}\) are trapezoid weights.  The
residual formula above is then applied to \(F_b^{\rm mod}\).  The quadrature
therefore defines the observed product; it is not a new radiation calculation.

\subsection{Exposure quadrature}

The exposure product is the model flux averaged over a finite observing time.
For an exposure centered at \(t_e\) with duration \(\Delta t_e\), the model value
is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar F_{\nu,e}
&=\frac{1}{\Delta t_e}
\int_{t_e-\Delta t_e/2}^{t_e+\Delta t_e/2}
F_\nu(t,\nu_e;\boldsymbol{\vartheta})\,dt\\
&\simeq
\sum_q w_q^{\rm G}
F_\nu(t_{e,q},\nu_e;\boldsymbol{\vartheta}) .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(\nu_e\) is the observed frequency, \(t_{e,q}\) are the mapped
Gauss--Legendre nodes, and \(w_q^{\rm G}\) are normalized by
\(\sum_q w_q^{\rm G}=1\).  Repeated \((t_{e,q},\nu_e)\) pairs are queried once
and then read from a cache.  The pair cache changes runtime cost only; it does
not change the physical model value.

\subsection{Runtime query cache and public products}

The runtime query cache stores observer products that have already been solved.
Its keys are the observer grid, projection kind, observer geometry, and runtime
configuration.  The cached objects are flux matrices and diagnostic tracks.  They
are read back for flux-density, spectrum, band-flux, exposure-averaged flux, and
component queries.  The cache does not evolve, renormalize, or reinterpret any
physical state.

\section{Discussion} \label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Degeneracy}

Afterglow light curves are degenerate because several local processes can change
the same observer flux.  A break in \(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\) may trace blast-wave
deceleration, density structure, a cooling transition, angular projection, an RS
component, SSC cooling, local absorption, or secondary particles.  ASGARD
addresses this degeneracy by storing the radius-local state before observer
projection mixes angular and radial contributions.  Figures~\ref{fig:radius-state}
--\ref{fig:reverse-shock} and Figure~\ref{fig:complex-state} show the
local records that can be inspected before a light-curve feature is interpreted.
Figures~\ref{fig:benchmark-fs} and \ref{fig:rs-ssc-geometry} then test
only the public-output subsets whose assumptions can be matched across codes.
This evidence supports diagnostic decomposition, not a claim that one light curve
has a unique physical cause.

\subsection{State}

The radius-ordered state is the object that makes the benchmark interpretable.
It links the dynamics, electron distribution, photon field, absorption factors,
optional RS state, optional hadronic and pair products, and observer components
at the same shock radius.  The state figures show this link inside ASGARD, and
the cross-code figures show which projected outputs can be compared to public
codes.  This separation matters because a public flux ratio does not expose the
local electron spectrum, photon field, or pair source that produced the flux.
Figure~\ref{fig:complex-state} therefore complements
Figures~\ref{fig:benchmark-fs} and \ref{fig:rs-ssc-geometry}: it checks
state completeness where a fair public-code ratio is not available.

\subsection{Boundaries}

The supported-state record also defines where the method stops.  A coupled
physical branch is supported only when ASGARD has a local state, a projection
rule, and validation evidence for that branch.  Table~\ref{tab:cross-code-capabilities}
and Section~\ref{sec:limits} make this rule explicit for comparison codes,
runtime configurations, and coupled-physics branches.  Unsupported combinations
are listed as unsupported rather than inferred from partial fields.  The boundary
list is therefore part of the method, not a software footnote.

\section{Model Boundaries} \label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Runtime configuration boundaries}

The runtime configuration boundary states which inputs have a complete
radius-ordered state sequence and observer projection.  Let \(N_{\rho,\max}\) be
the tabulated-medium capacity, and let \(N_{J,\max}\) be the density-jump
register length.  A setup outside the following list is not a supported physical
model in this manuscript.
\begin{itemize}
\item External media are supported as uniform media, \(k=2\) stellar winds, or
tabulated radial profiles.  User-defined medium kernels are unsupported.
\item A tabulated medium requires \(2\le N_\rho\le N_{\rho,\max}\) positive
radius-density points, ordered by increasing radius.
\item Density jumps require \(N_J\le N_{J,\max}\) Gaussian jumps on an analytic
medium.
\item Jet spreading is unsupported.  The angular elements keep the opening
angles specified by the initial jet profile.
\item Adaptive observer-time grids are supported only for direct top-hat
kernel-backed models.
\item The structured Fortran backend requires uniform patch sampling and either
\texttt{fullhide\_1d} or \texttt{dg\_1d}.
\item Non-uniform dominant-region sampling is a patch-backend option; it is not
supported by the structured Fortran backend.
\item Sky-image and synchrotron-Stokes products are unsupported by the current
manuscript evidence set.
\item Thermal electrons are supported only by the one-dimensional implicit
electron solvers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coupled-physics boundaries}

The coupled-physics boundary states which extra radiation branches have both a
local state and an observer projection.  A branch without both objects is not
used as a model prediction.
\begin{itemize}
\item Formal hadronic transport requires a one-dimensional forward-electron
solver.
\item The legacy hadronic branch does not support \(p\gamma\) interactions or
neutrino channels.
\item Joint electron-photon coupling requires the one-dimensional implicit
electron solver, the AM3-based one-dimensional hadronic solver, the
Hummer--2010 photohadronic response, Bethe-Heitler physics, numeric IC/KN
cooling, no reverse shock, no \(\chi\)-resolved transport, and fixed electron
substeps.
\item Multi-density reverse-shock v1 requires \(p_r>2\).  It does not support
hadronic processes, forward SSC, reverse SSC, cross-zone IC, or joint
electron-photon coupling.
\item The current formal hadronic path is shell-averaged; it is not
\(\chi\)-resolved.
\item Pair-cascade iterations support shell-sequence \(\gamma\gamma\) pair/synch
feedback.  IC-mediated electromagnetic cascades are unsupported.
\item The \(\chi\)-resolved projection replaces only forward-shock synchrotron
plus SSA projection.  It does not make SSC, hadronic emission, or pair cascades
\(\chi\)-local.
\end{itemize}
These limits are part of the published method.  A coupled case outside this list
has no manuscript-level support unless it has its own local state, projection
operator, and validation evidence.

\section{Summary} \label{sec:summary}

\asgard\ is a bounded GRB afterglow method for tracing observer features back to
local physical state.  At each radius, the state \(\mathcal{S}(R)\) records the
blast-wave dynamics, electron distribution, photon fields, optional hadronic
species, reverse-shock variables, absorption factors, and observer components.
The code updates these objects before applying the equal-arrival-time projection.
This ordering is the main contribution of the paper: a complex light curve can be
read as local dynamics, particles, photons, absorption, components, and
projection rather than only as a final \(F_\nu(t_{\rm obs})\).

The evidence for this method is reproducible from tracked source data.  The
figures test the radius state, forward-shock API products, electron and photon
transport, \(\chi\)-resolved projection, reverse-shock diagnostics,
hadronic/pair activation checks, the bounded validation dashboard, matched
public-output subsets, and the ASGARD-only state dashboard.  The capability table
states which entries have numerical rows and which entries are explicit
boundaries.  The PyBlastAfterglowMag source is pinned, its repo-local
\texttt{pba.out} is compiled, and its numerical rows are quoted only for the
matched FS and Gaussian-geometry subsets.

The supported use cases are exactly the cases with state, projection, and
validation evidence.  ASGARD supports the runtime and coupled-physics domains
listed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.  It does not support jet spreading,
user-defined medium kernels, wind profiles with \(k\ne2\), \(\chi\)-resolved
hadronic transport, IC-mediated electromagnetic cascades, or coupled branches
without a validated observer projection.  These boundaries are part of the
method because they state which physical interpretations can be drawn from the
exported state.

\section{Electron Solvers} \label{app:electron-solvers}

This appendix records the solver mechanics that are too detailed for the main
method flow.  The main text gives the physical continuity equation, source and
cooling terms, and public boundaries.  Here we keep only the storage maps and
numerical contracts needed to reproduce the one-dimensional and \(\chi\)-resolved
electron states.

\subsection{Coordinate maps and cell averages}

All electron solvers represent the same physical spectrum.  The reference
coordinate is \(x_g=\ln\gamma_e\), with \(N_x=dN_e/dx_g\).  If a solver stores a
coordinate \(y\), the initial cell average is
\begin{equation}
N_{y,j}^{\rm init}=\frac{1}{\Delta y_j}
\int_{y_{j-1/2}}^{y_{j+1/2}}
N_x[x_g(y)]\,\frac{dx_g}{dy}\,dy .
\end{equation}
The log-\(\gamma\) branch uses \(y=x_g\).  The four-velocity branch uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y&=\ln\left[1+\frac{\gamma_e^2-1}{\gamma_s^2-1}\right],\\
\gamma_e(y)&=\left[1+(\gamma_s^2-1)(e^y-1)\right]^{1/2},\\
\frac{dx_g}{dy}&=
\frac{(\gamma_s^2-1)e^y}{2\gamma_e^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The grid is uniform in \(y\), and diagnostic arrays are returned as
\((x_{g,i+1/2},\gamma_i)\).  These are storage coordinates, not physical cutoffs.

Diagnostics that need \(dN_e/d\gamma_e\) reconstruct a positive exponential
profile in each cell,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dN_e}{dy}=A_j\exp[\sigma_j(y-y_j)],\qquad
\sigma_j=\operatorname{minmod}(\ell_j^-,\ell_j^+),
\end{equation}
with \(\sigma_j=0\) when positive neighbors are absent and \(A_j\) fixed by the
cell average.  The centered diagnostic value is
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{dN_e}{d\gamma_e}\right|_j
=\frac{A_j}{\gamma_j(dx_g/dy)_j}.
\end{equation}
This conversion does not change transported cell averages.

\subsection{Conservative cooling and remap primitives}

The implicit finite-volume cooling primitive moves content from high to low
electron energy.  With face coefficient \(\lambda_{j+1/2}\), diagonal weight
\(b_0\), and right-hand side \(r_j\),
\begin{equation}
p_j=b_0-\lambda_{j-1/2},\qquad
c_j=\frac{2\lambda_{j+1/2}}{p_j+p_{j+1}},
\end{equation}
and the high-to-low sweep is
\begin{equation}
N_N^{n+1}=r_N,\qquad
N_j^{n+1}=\Pi_+\!\left(r_j-c_jN_{j+1}^{n+1}\right).
\label{eq:triangular-cooling-sweep}
\end{equation}
The projection \(\Pi_+\) acts on the solved cell content of this conservative
electron solver.

Remaps move conserved cell content between old and new energy coordinates.  The
transported spectrum is reconstructed with a monotone parabolic cell profile
whose cell integral defines the prefix function \(M(x)=\int Q(x')\,dx'\).  An
arrival cell receives
\begin{equation}
\bar Q_j^{\,{\rm new}}=
\frac{M(x_{j+1/2}^{\rm dep})-M(x_{j-1/2}^{\rm dep})}{\Delta x_j}.
\end{equation}
Analytic source terms use the same prefix-integral idea but are reconstructed
separately from the transported spectrum, so a broad injected power law is not
forced to inherit old-spectral curvature.

\subsection{One-dimensional solver variants}

The characteristic solver traces \(u=1/\gamma_e\) backward through a piecewise
affine cooling field.  Source quadrature uses mapped Gauss points
\begin{equation}
s_m=t_m^{q_{\rm C}},\qquad
w_m=q_{\rm C}W_mt_m^{q_{\rm C}-1},
\end{equation}
and the updated natural-log cell average is
\begin{equation}
N_{x,j}^{n+1}={\cal I}_N(j,\Delta R)
+\Delta R\sum_mw_m{\cal I}_Q(j,s_m\Delta R).
\end{equation}
The first term carries old electrons along cooling characteristics, and the
second injects source electrons along the same radial step.

The uniform-grid semi-Lagrangian branch adds half of the source, traces cell faces
back through the log-energy cooling velocity, and then adds the remaining half:
\begin{equation}
N_j^{n+1}=\Pi_+\!\left[
\mathcal I_j(M_{1/2};x_{j-1/2}^{\rm dep},x_{j+1/2}^{\rm dep})
+\frac{\Delta R}{2}Q_{x,j}^{\rm S}\right].
\end{equation}
The three-level log-\(\gamma\) branch uses the same triangular sweep with
backward-Euler startup and then a BDF2 history state.  The WENO branch instead
reconstructs an explicit fifth-order WENO-Z face flux and advances the
conservative log-energy equation with the standard three-stage SSP Runge--Kutta
update.  These variants differ only in the radial discretization; their source,
cooling, and diagnostic outputs follow the shared contracts above.

\subsection{\texttt{fullhide\_1d}, DG, and thermal details}

\texttt{fullhide\_1d} resolves one shell with fixed radial substeps,
\begin{equation}
L_i=\max\!\left[N_{\rm fh,min},
\min\!\left(N_{\rm fh,max},
\left\lfloor\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{\Delta R_0}\right\rfloor\right)\right],
\qquad
\Delta R=\frac{R_i-R_{i-1}}{L_i}.
\end{equation}
In a uniform medium without thermal electrons, those substeps are accumulated
into one implicit solve by replacing the one-step source and face displacement
with shell-integrated quantities.  Nonuniform media and thermal electrons keep
explicit substeps, re-evaluating \(n(R)\), \(B'\), \(\gamma_m\), and
\(\gamma_{\max}\) at each substep endpoint.  Coupled electron--photon passes use
the same triangular primitive, with primary and secondary-pair sources included
in \(Q_x\).

The hybrid thermal branch changes only the injection source.  It joins a
Maxwell--Juttner part below \(\gamma_m\) to a cutoff power-law tail above
\(\gamma_m\), with the temperature \(\Theta\) fixed by the normalization root
\begin{equation}
F(\Theta)=\frac{\gamma_m}{\Theta}+\ln I_1(\Theta)-\ln C=0.
\end{equation}
The Bessel and incomplete-gamma functions used to evaluate \(I_1\), \(I_2\), and
the tail moment are special-function evaluators; they do not add another
transport equation.

The DG solver stores local Legendre representations on an active moving energy
mesh.  Source templates are normalized to the finite-cell source integral in the
same \(y\) measure, then projected into element moments.  The implicit element
block has the form
\begin{equation}
\sum_j A_{a,ij}N_{a,j}^{n+1}
=N_{a,i}^{n}+\Delta R\,Q_{a,i}
+\mathcal{F}_{a,i}^{\rm up},
\end{equation}
where \(A_{a,ij}\) is assembled from the Legendre--Gauss--Lobatto derivative
matrix and the local cooling velocity.  DG remapping preserves electron number
by mapping the same \(\gamma_e\) from the old coordinate to the new coordinate,
with the Jacobian \(dy_{\rm old}/dy_{\rm new}\).  Positivity limiting keeps the
element average fixed and modifies only high-order nodal content.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\chi\)}{chi}-resolved downstream state}

The two-dimensional branch distributes a shell spectrum into downstream cells
behind the forward shock.  Its active coordinate is \(q\), with shock front at
\(q=0\) and
\begin{equation}
q_\ast=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{\rm sh}}\right)^{r_{\rm sh}},\qquad
\Delta q=\frac{q_\ast}{N_\chi}.
\end{equation}
At the first radius, the shell spectrum is placed in the first downstream cell,
and \(\sum_aU_{ia}\Delta q\) recovers the shell spectrum.  The local downstream
radius and four-speed interpolate between the Blandford--McKee and planar
strong-shock limits; the resulting local state \((r_a,\Gamma_a,\beta_a)\) is
stored for cooling, emission, absorption, and projection.

When magnetic decay is active, the local magnetic fraction is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{B,a}=\epsilon_{B,\min}
+(\epsilon_B-\epsilon_{B,\min})
\left(1+\frac{t'_a}{t_B}\right)^{\alpha_t},
\end{equation}
otherwise \(\epsilon_{B,a}=\epsilon_B\).  The local field is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
e'_a&=r_{\rm sh}\Gamma_f(\Gamma_f-1)n(R)m_pc^2,\\
B'_a&=\left(8\pi\epsilon_{B,a}e'_a\right)^{1/2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the same \(B'_a\) used for synchrotron, SSA, cooling, and Bohm diffusion.

The photon-history branch carries seed photons from older downstream cells to
the current target cell only when light-travel time allows it:
\begin{equation}
x'_{m,a}\ge x'_{j,b},\qquad
c(t'_j-t'_m)\ge x'_{m,a}-x'_{j,b}.
\end{equation}
Accepted source spectra are Doppler mapped by the relative factor
\({\cal D}_{st}=\Gamma_{st}(1+\beta_{st})\), attenuated by SSA and local pair
opacity, and accumulated as the cooling/SSC target field.  The streamed
light-cone update applies the same mapping to carried history and newly emitted
previous-shell photons.

\subsection{Downstream transport in \texorpdfstring{\(q\)}{q} and energy}

The downstream \(q\)-transport evolves the cell average \(U_{ia}\) under
advection, Bohm diffusion, and first-cell injection:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial R}
+\frac{\partial(A_qU_i)}{\partial q}
=\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
\left({\cal K}_i\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial q}\right)
+Q_i^{(1)}(R)\delta_{a1}.
\end{equation}
The characteristic remap traces \(q\)-faces through the local affine speed
\(A_q(q)=\alpha_a+\zeta_aq\), while diffusion uses the metric converting physical
downstream distance to the \(q\) grid,
\begin{equation}
{\cal K}_{i,a+1/2}
=\frac{\kappa_{i,a+1/2}}{\beta_{\rm sh}c}J_{q,a+1/2}^{2}.
\end{equation}
The implicit diffusion or joint advection--diffusion step solves the resulting
tridiagonal system over downstream cells.

Each \(q\) column is then cooled on the natural-log electron grid.  The default
cooling speed is
\begin{equation}
\xi_{i+1/2,a}=D_{i+1/2,a}+\frac{1}{R},
\end{equation}
or \(D_{i+1/2,a}+A_{{\rm ad},a}\) in the PWN/CR downstream option.  The same
high-to-low sweep used in Equation~\eqref{eq:triangular-cooling-sweep} updates
each column.  Optional stochastic energy diffusion adds a conservative
second-difference solve in \(x=\ln\gamma_e\) with zero energy flux through the
grid boundaries.


\section{Photon, SSC, and SSA Kernels} \label{app:photon-kernels}

This appendix records the radiation-kernel contracts used by the particle and
observer sections.  The physical objects are the local synchrotron luminosity,
seed photon density, SSC luminosity, SSA heating coefficient, photon-survival
factors, and diagnostic polarization quantities.

\subsection{Radiation binding and analytic IC closure}

The radiation ABI layer only maps public names such as \texttt{annihilation},
\texttt{cal\_ebl}, \texttt{ssc\_spec}, and \texttt{ssc\_spec\_nonuniform} to
compiled kernels.  It does not create photon states beyond those defined in the
main text.

The Fan-style analytic Klein--Nishina closure estimates the synchrotron photon
energy fraction below the Thomson threshold.  For electron node \(i\), the
closure returns \(\eta_i^{\rm KN}\) and enters
\begin{equation}
Y_i^{\rm F}(1+Y_i^{\rm F})=
\eta_{\rm rad}\eta_i^{\rm KN}\frac{\epsilon_e}{\epsilon_B}.
\end{equation}
The implementation uses separate fast- and slow-cooling piecewise forms over
\((\gamma_m,\gamma_c,\gamma_{\max})\); the returned value is zero below the
lowest relevant break and unity above the cutoff.  This branch is an analytic IC
cooling closure, not a replacement for the tabulated photon field used by the
full IC/KN path.

\subsection{SSA heating and IC loss integrals}

The SSA heating coefficient evaluates the absorbed-photon energy integral over
the local photon field.  It clips the two boundary photon cells, uses cached
logarithmic Gauss weights for complete cells, and applies the same interpolation
primitive used elsewhere for \(n_\nu(\nu)\).  The local segment contribution has
the form
\begin{equation}
A_{\rm seg}^{\rm abs}
=c\sum_q w_q\,h\nu_q\,n_\nu(\nu_q)\sigma_{\rm SSA}(\nu_q),
\end{equation}
with the low- and high-frequency SSA cross-section branches fixed by the local
electron bin and magnetic field.  The helper evaluates \(A_i^{\rm abs}\); it does
not alter the stored photon field.

The grid-level IC cooling path integrates seed and scattered photons on the same
local frequency grid.  With \(\epsilon=h\nu/(m_ec^2)\), the branch uses
\begin{equation}
b_{ij}=4\gamma_i\epsilon_j,\qquad
q_{i\ell j}=\frac{\epsilon_\ell}{b_{ij}(\gamma_i-\epsilon_\ell)}
\end{equation}
and the Jones kernel \(G_{\rm J}(q,b)\) inside its kinematic domain.  The cooling
coefficient is accumulated as
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\rm IC}\simeq
\frac{3h\sigma_{\rm es}}{4m_e c\gamma_i^3}
\sum_j\frac{n_{\nu,j}}{\bar\nu_j}\Delta\nu_j
\sum_\ell \bar\nu_\ell G_{i\ell j}\Delta\nu_\ell .
\end{equation}
The coupled-budget IC path uses the same physical kernel with Simpson weights to
return the auxiliary coefficient \(A_i^{\rm IC,b}\) for the implicit
electron--photon solve.

\subsection{Synchrotron and interpolation helpers}

Fast synchrotron emissivity paths use an analytic approximation to the
single-particle kernel.  For \(\nu_c=C_\nu B'\gamma_e^2\) and \(x=\nu/\nu_c\),
\begin{equation}
\Phi(x)=
\begin{cases}
A_\Phi x^{1/3}, & x<x_{\rm L},\\
A_\Phi e^{-x}\left(x^{-2/3}+B_\Phi^2\right)^{-1/2},
& x_{\rm L}\le x\le x_{\rm H},\\
0, & x>x_{\rm H}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The constants are fixed coefficients of the approximation, not physical model
parameters.

The shared interpolation primitive evaluates a tabulated radiation field inside
one frequency cell.  Positive endpoints use a local power-law segment,
\begin{equation}
Y(\nu)=Y_0\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_0}\right)^{
\ln(Y_1/Y_0)/\ln(\nu_1/\nu_0)},
\end{equation}
whereas intervals touching a nonpositive endpoint use linear interpolation.  The
logarithmic Gauss helper integrates over a positive cell with
\begin{equation}
\nu_a=\exp(x_m+\Delta x\,\xi_a),\qquad
w_a=\Delta x\,\omega_a\,\nu_a,
\end{equation}
so \(d\nu=\nu\,dx\) is included in the quadrature weight.

\subsection{SSA frequency and polarization diagnostics}

The self-absorption-frequency solver finds \(\tau_\nu(\nu_a)=1\).  It expands a
logarithmic bracket around the named trial frequency, keeps the bracket inside
the declared frequency cap, and applies the log--log secant estimate
\begin{equation}
\ln\nu_\ast=
\ln\nu_l-\ln\tau_l
\frac{\ln\nu_h-\ln\nu_l}{\ln\tau_h-\ln\tau_l}.
\end{equation}
If a stored optical-depth grid already brackets unity, the same formula is
applied between adjacent grid frequencies.

The intrinsic polarization helper computes a local shell-frame diagnostic from
the transported electron spectrum.  With \(x=\nu/(C_\nu B'\gamma_e^2)\), it
integrates the standard synchrotron kernels \(F(x)\) and \(G(x)\) to obtain
\begin{equation}
I_\perp=\int N_eP_\perp\,d\gamma_e,\qquad
I_\parallel=\int N_eP_\parallel\,d\gamma_e,\qquad
\Pi_\nu^{\rm loc}=\frac{I_\perp-I_\parallel}{I_\perp+I_\parallel}.
\end{equation}
Analytic low- and high-\(x\) limits are used outside the direct quadrature
window.  The helper returns split local luminosities; it does not perform
observer-frame polarization projection.

\subsection{Thermal tables and adaptive radiation grid}

The thermal Bessel table supplies \(K_2(z)\) for Maxwell--Juttner normalizations,
using analytic small- and large-\(z\) limits and a tabulated middle interval.  It
is a special-function evaluator, not a separate thermal transport branch.

The adaptive radiation-grid helper selects electron points only for radiation
quadrature.  It works on positive electron bins, estimates local curvature in
\((x_i,y_i)=(\ln\gamma_i,\ln N_{e,i})\), and samples the cumulative weight
constructed from smoothed curvature plus a floor.  Endpoints and immediate
outside neighbors are retained when available.  The returned grid is denser near
cooling breaks, injection turnovers, and high-energy cutoffs, but it does not
modify \(N_e\).

\section{Hadronic and Pair Feedback} \label{app:hadronic-pair-feedback}\label{app:hadronic-figure}

The hadronic and pair-feedback appendix records the optional shell-averaged
high-energy state.  When this branch is active, the code reads the local magnetic
field, shell volume, target photon field, and nonthermal proton budget from the
one-dimensional radius sequence.  It then computes proton transport, photon
losses, neutrino and secondary-pair production, and photon survival factors
before observer projection.  This appendix documents that branch as a local
transport object; it does not claim that hadronic feedback controls every
afterglow light curve.

The hadronic branch has a hard dimensional boundary.  The formal forward-shock
feedback path is one-dimensional and shell averaged.  It is not a \(\chi\)-local
hadronic calculation, because the target photon field, hadron density, secondary

\(\gamma\gamma\) pair/synchrotron cascade.  IC-mediated electromagnetic cascades
are unsupported in the present state contract.

Its generator requires both an official AM3 table and a parent-version delta
table, and fails before producing an artifact when either input is absent.
Consequently, this manuscript makes no AM3-accuracy or end-to-end BH,
\(p\gamma\), or pair-cascade closure claim.

\section{Reverse-Shock Technical Details} \label{app:reverse-shock-technical}

This appendix holds the reverse-shock algebra that is too detailed for the main
method section.  It records the finite-strength MHD jump, magnetized upstream

shock diagnostics, and reacceleration remaps.  The main text keeps the ejecta

\subsection{Finite-Strength MHD Jump Cubic}

The finite-strength MHD jump gives the downstream shock-frame four-speed used by
the magnetized reverse shock.  The upstream state is described by the relative
Lorentz factor \(g=\gamma_{34}\) and the ejecta magnetization \(\sigma\).  The
local algebra uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
g&\equiv\gamma_{34},\\
\hat{\gamma}&=\frac{4}{3}+\frac{1}{3g},\\
h_1&=\hat{\gamma}-1,\qquad
h_2=\hat{\gamma}-2,\qquad
y&\equiv u_d^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(u_d\) is the downstream four-speed in the reverse-shock frame.  The
parameter \(\sigma_{\rm cut}\) is an algorithmic branch parameter; it is not a
physical magnetization boundary.

The low-\(\sigma\) branch returns the hydrodynamic-strength root of the same
shock-frame problem,
\begin{equation}
y=\frac{(g-1)h_1^2}{-\hat{\gamma}h_2(g-1)+2}.
\end{equation}
For \(\sigma>\sigma_{\rm cut}\), the MHD branch instead solves
\begin{equation}
a_3y^3+a_2y^2+a_1y+a_0=0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_3={}&-\hat{\gamma}h_2(g-1)+2,\\
a_2={}&-(g+1)\left[-h_2(\hat{\gamma}g^2+1)+\hat{\gamma}h_1g\right]\sigma\\
&-(g-1)\left[-\hat{\gamma}h_2(g^2-2)+2g+3\right],\\
a_1={}&(g+1)\left[\hat{\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{4}\right)
(g^2-1)+1\right]\sigma^2\\
&+(g^2-1)\left[2g+h_2(\hat{\gamma}g-1)\right]\sigma
+(g+1)(g-1)^2h_1^2,\\
a_0={}&-\frac{1}{4}(g-1)(g+1)^2h_2^2\sigma^2 .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These coefficients encode the finite upstream magnetic pressure and enthalpy in
the jump condition.

The cubic is solved by reducing it to a depressed form.  Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
b_c&=\frac{a_2}{a_3},\qquad
c_c=\frac{a_1}{a_3},\qquad
d_c=\frac{a_0}{a_3},\\
p_c&=c_c-\frac{b_c^2}{3},\\
q_c&=\frac{2b_c^3}{27}-\frac{b_cc_c}{3}+d_c,\\
a_c&=\left(-\frac{p_c}{3}\right)^{1/2},\\
\psi_c&=\frac{1}{3}\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{3q_c}{2p_ca_c}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The physical root used by the shell dynamics is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
y&=2a_c\cos\left(\psi_c-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)-\frac{b_c}{3},\\
u_d&=y^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The upstream shock-frame four-speed is then
\begin{equation}
u_u=\left[(1+u_d^2)(g^2-1)\right]^{1/2}+g u_d .
\end{equation}
Thus the jump returns \(u_d\), the compression ratio \(r_\sigma=u_u/u_d\), and
the magnetized downstream heat used by the primary reverse-shock state.

\subsection{Forward-Dynamics Runtime Details}

The forward-dynamics runtime record is an ABI-ordered container.  The public
model first builds one boundary vector, and the compiled dynamics kernel unpacks
only the slots it needs for the forward shock:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf b_{\rm dyn}\mapsto
\begin{gathered}
\left(
\Gamma_0,\epsilon_e,\epsilon_B,p,z,n_{\rm ISM},A_\ast,E_{\rm iso},
\log_{10}t_{{\rm on},\rm end},f_e
\right),\\
\left(
t_1,t_2,L_0,q,R_{\rm tr},f_{\rm jump},w_{\rm jump},R_0
\right).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Here \(t_1,t_2,L_0\), and \(q\) describe the optional luminosity source, while
\(R_{\rm tr}\), \(f_{\rm jump}\), \(w_{\rm jump}\), and \(R_0\) describe the
analytic density jump and inner wind cap.  The container itself is not a new
physical model; it only fixes how public inputs are passed to the compiled
kernel.  The kernel then starts from
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_0=(\Gamma_0-\Delta\Gamma_{\rm init},
Y_{2,{\rm init}},U_{\rm init},R_{\rm init}),
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta\Gamma_{\rm init}\), \(Y_{2,{\rm init}}\), \(U_{\rm init}\), and
\(R_{\rm init}\) are fixed integration-start parameters.  These values set the
first numerical state before the radius-ordered shock history is written.

The forward-dynamics output grid is built in the source-frame on-axis time
coordinate.  The first positive grid time is
\begin{equation}
t_{{\rm on},\min}=\min(t_{\rm floor},\eta_{\rm dec}t_{\rm dec}) ,
\end{equation}
where \(t_{\rm floor}\) and \(\eta_{\rm dec}\) are fixed grid parameters.  The
grid and time-ordered update are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_i&=\log_{10}t_{{\rm on},\min}
+\frac{\log_{10}t_{{\rm on},\rm end}
-\log_{10}t_{{\rm on},\min}}{N_R-1}(i-1),\\
t_{{\rm on},i}&=10^{\ell_i},\\
\Delta t_i&=t_{{\rm on},i}-t_{{\rm on},i-1},\qquad t_{{\rm on},0}=0,\\
\mathbf{y}_i&=\mathbf{y}_{i-1}
+\int_{t_{{\rm on},i-1}}^{t_{{\rm on},i}}
F(t_{\rm on},\mathbf{y})\,dt_{\rm on}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(F\) is the selected forward-dynamics right-hand side from
Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}.  After each accepted step, the stored state is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_i=(Y_{\Gamma,i},Y_{2,i},Y_{U,i},Y_{R,i}).
\end{equation}
The public output arrays then record \((1+z)t_{{\rm on},i}\), \(Y_{\Gamma,i}\),
\(Y_{R,i}\), and the swept mass.  Branches that evolve a swept number column
convert it to mass before writing the public output.

The RK substep variable controls the positive-time integration interval.  It
obeys
\begin{equation}
0\le s\le \ln(t_{{\rm on},i}/t_{{\rm on},i-1}),
\qquad
|\Delta s|\le\Delta s_{\max},
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta s_{\max}\) is a fixed integration parameter.  The refinement
compares a trial state with the previous accepted trial and accepts only states
inside the dynamical domain \(\Gamma>1\) with positive evolved components.
